\section{Scope}

This document defines a complete map from an electrolyte formulation to one
bulk ionic conductivity:
\[
r =
\left(
T,\,
\{\widehat{\phi}_s\}_{s\in\mathcal S},\,
\{m_\ell\}_{\ell\in\mathcal L},\,
\{p_a\}_{a\in\mathcal A}
\right),
\]
where solvent loadings are volume ratios, salt loadings are molarities, and
additive loadings are weight percent. The output is one scalar bulk ionic
conductivity:
\[
\sigma(r,T) \quad [\Sm],
\qquad
\sigma_{\mScm}(r,T)=10\,\sigma(r,T).
\]

The calculation has four layers:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{formulation}
\mapsto
\text{physical mixture and species library}\\
\mapsto
\text{projected generator primitives}
\mapsto
\text{projected diffusivity density}\\
\mapsto
\text{conductivity}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The main computational contract is:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{formulation in v/v, M, wt\%}
\mapsto (r,\mathcal L)\\
\mapsto (U_x,D_x,P_x,\mathcal A,\Psi)\\
\mapsto (c,K,Q,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h)
\mapsto \sigma_{\rm mS/cm}
\end{gathered}
}
\]
Every conductivity effect must enter through one or more projected primitives:
\[
c_i,\quad K_{ij},\quad Q_{ij},\quad d_{ij},\quad M_{ij},\quad
D_i^{\rm self},\quad A,\quad h,\quad C^Q,\quad C^\psi,\quad D_{\rm proj}.
\]
The scalar conductivity is read from those primitives. A terminal fitted
multiplier is outside this model.

\section{Constants, Data, and Configuration}

\subsection{Constants}

The conductivity prefactor uses the Faraday constant \(F\), the molar gas
constant \(R\), and the temperature \(T\):
\[
\sigma=\frac{F^2}{3RT}\operatorname{tr}(D_{\rm proj}).
\]
The particle-scale mobility relation uses Boltzmann's constant:
\[
D=k_BT\,R_{\rm fric}^{\#}.
\]
Avogadro's number \(N_A\) converts molecular counts to molar amounts. The
elementary charge \(e\) and vacuum permittivity \(\epsilon_0\) enter Coulomb,
Born, and atmosphere terms. All charge-polarization vectors are Cartesian, so
the trace readout averages over three spatial axes.

\section{Notation and Numerical Operations}

\subsection{Vectors, matrices, tensors}

Vectors are column vectors. For \(u,v\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
u^\T v=\sum_i u_i v_i.
\]
For a square matrix \(B\),
\[
\tr(B)=\sum_iB_{ii}.
\]
For a symmetric matrix,
\[
B\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y^\T By\ge0\ \text{for all }y.
\]
For \(T_i\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}\),
\[
\tr(T_i)=T_{i,xx}+T_{i,yy}+T_{i,zz}.
\]

\subsection{Pseudoinverse and nullspace checks}

For a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix \(B\), write
\[
B=V\Lambda V^\T,
\qquad
\Lambda=\diag(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m),
\qquad
\lambda_r\ge0.
\]
Choose
\[
\tau_\#=10^{-12}\max_r\lambda_r.
\]
Define
\[
\lambda_r^\#=
\begin{cases}
1/\lambda_r, & \lambda_r>\tau_\#,\\
0, & \lambda_r\le\tau_\#.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
B^\#=V\diag(\lambda_1^\#,\ldots,\lambda_m^\#)V^\T.
\]
For every current-coupling vector \(y\) entering a memory correction, require
\[
\|(I-BB^\#)y\|\le\tau_{\rm null}\|y\|,
\qquad
\tau_{\rm null}=10^{-8}.
\]
The finite-state generator uses a constrained Poisson solve rather than direct
formation of \((-Q)^\#\).

\subsection{Species property library}

Every species appearing in the formulation must carry the physical descriptors
needed by the equations below. Missing required descriptors make the
conductivity map undefined.

For every liquid component used in mixture averages, the model requires:
\[
\rho_i\,[\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}],
\qquad
\eta_i\,[\cp],
\qquad
\epsilon_i,
\qquad
DN_i.
\]
For every ionic transport constituent, the model requires:
\[
\Lambda^0_i,\quad
r^-_i\,[\Ang],\quad
r^+_i\,[\Ang],\quad
B_i,\quad
E^{\rm pair}_i\,[\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}],\quad
\delta^\epsilon_i\,[\mathrm{M^{-1}}],
\]
where \(\Lambda_i^0\) is the limiting molar conductivity, \(r_i^-\) and
\(r_i^+\) are anion and cation radii, \(B_i\) is the Jones-Dole coefficient,
\(E_i^{\rm pair}\) is the association free-energy parameter, and
\(\delta_i^\epsilon\) is the dielectric decrement. Each ionic constituent also
requires \(\rho_i\) and molecular weight \(M_i\,[\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}]\).

The lithium center requires a solvated hydrodynamic radius and a
Stokes-Einstein exponent:
\[
\bar r_{\rm Li}^{\rm solv}\,[\Ang],
\qquad
\alpha_{\rm Li}.
\]

\subsection{Configuration parameters}

The model uses the parameter blocks
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_{\rm cond}&=\text{projected conductivity primitive parameters},\\
\Theta_{\rm pair}&=\text{ion-pairing parameters},\\
\Theta_{\eta}&=\text{viscosity parameters},\\
\Theta_{\rm SE}&=\text{Stokes-Einstein parameters},\\
\Theta_{\rm solv}&=\text{solvation parameters}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The conductivity block contains
\[
\lambda_{\rm assoc},\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\rm pol},
\lambda_{\rm loc},p_I,p_V,p_A,p_S,p_Q,p_D,
\lambda_{\rm ep},\lambda_{\rm rel},\lambda_{\rm cross},
\lambda_{\rm cloud},\lambda_{\rm act},
\lambda_{\rm crowd},\lambda_{\rm orient}.
\]
The pairing block contains \(\epsilon_{\rm ref}\). The viscosity block contains
\(B_\eta,B_\eta^{\rm lin},D_\eta\). The Stokes-Einstein block contains
\(c_{\rm SE},\eta_{\rm ref},\alpha_{\rm anion}\). The solvation block contains
the shell thickness \(\Delta r_{\rm shell}\).

\section{Full Physical Generator Construction}
\label{sec:fullgenerator}

\subsection{Recipe non-identifiability}

A formulation tuple gives names and loadings. Conductivity requires a physical
library that determines
\[
U_x(q),\qquad D_x(q),\qquad P_x(q),\qquad \mathcal A,\qquad \Psi.
\]
Here \(U_x\) is free energy, \(D_x\) is the coordinate mobility tensor, \(P_x\)
is unwrapped charge polarization, \(\mathcal A=\{A_i\}\) is the basin partition,
and \(\Psi=(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_m)\) is the memory-coordinate basis.

Recipe loadings alone do not determine conductivity. If two admissible physical
libraries share the same species names and concentrations but have
\[
D_x^{(2)}(q)=\alpha D_x^{(1)}(q),
\qquad
\alpha\ne1,
\]
then overdamped capacity fluxes scale as
\[
K_{ij}^{(2)}
=
\alpha K_{ij}^{(1)}
\]
because
\[
K_{ij}
=
\int
\nabla q_{ij}^{\T}D_x\nabla q_{ij}\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\]
and the self-current tensors scale as
\[
D_{i,2}^{\rm self}=\alpha D_{i,1}^{\rm self}.
\]
The scalar conductivity therefore changes unless every memory correction
compensates exactly. The complete model input is the formulation plus the
physical descriptors that build the generator primitives.

\subsection{Species library and topology}

Let \(\mathcal S_{\rm mol}\) be the set of molecular, ionic, solvent, salt, and
additive species. The physical library is
\[
\mathcal L=
\left(
\{\mathcal T_s\}_{s\in\mathcal S_{\rm mol}},
\{\mathcal Q_s\}_{s\in\mathcal S_{\rm mol}},
\{\Theta_{st}\}_{s,t},
\mathcal B,
\mathcal M,
\mathcal R,
\Psi
\right).
\]
\(\mathcal T_s\) is topology, \(\mathcal Q_s\) is the charge-site model,
\(\Theta_{st}\) are pair and many-body interaction parameters, \(\mathcal B\)
defines basins, \(\mathcal M\) defines mobility and friction, and
\(\mathcal R\) defines transition surfaces and reaction coordinates.

For species \(s\), the site set is
\[
\mathcal I_s=\{1,\ldots,n_s\}.
\]
Each site \(u\in\mathcal I_s\) has
\[
m_{su}>0,\qquad
a_{su}>0,\qquad
\sigma_{su}>0,\qquad
\epsilon_{su}\ge0,\qquad
z_{su}\in\mathbb R,\qquad
\alpha_{su}\ge0.
\]
These are mass, hydrodynamic radius, short-range size, short-range energy,
formal or partial charge number, and polarizability. The topology also contains
bond, angle, and torsion sets:
\[
\mathcal E_s^{\rm bond}\subset\mathcal I_s^2,\qquad
\mathcal E_s^{\rm angle}\subset\mathcal I_s^3,\qquad
\mathcal E_s^{\rm torsion}\subset\mathcal I_s^4.
\]

\subsection{Molecule counts and volume filling}

For a cell of volume \(V_{\rm cell}\), a conserved component concentration
\(C_a\,[\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}]\) gives
\[
N_a=\operatorname{round}(C_aN_AV_{\rm cell}).
\]
For a liquid species \(s\) with volume fraction \(\phi_s\), molar mass \(M_s\),
and density \(\rho_s\),
\[
n_s^{\rm pure}=\frac{\rho_s}{M_s}N_A,
\qquad
N_s=\operatorname{round}(\phi_sn_s^{\rm pure}V_{\rm cell}).
\]
When finite salt and additive volumes are included, solve
\[
V_{\rm cell}
=
\sum_s\frac{N_sM_s}{\rho_sN_A}
+
\sum_a\bar v_aN_a
\]
by fixed-point iteration until
\[
\frac{|V_{\rm cell}^{(k+1)}-V_{\rm cell}^{(k)}|}
{V_{\rm cell}^{(k)}}<\tau_V.
\]

\subsection{Configuration coordinate}

The microscopic coordinate is
\[
q\in\Omega_x\subseteq\mathbb R^d.
\]
For an atomistic construction,
\[
q=(r_1,\ldots,r_N),
\qquad
r_\alpha\in\mathbb R^3.
\]
For a reduced analytical construction, \(q\) can contain distances, pair-state
coordinates, Li-solvent coordination numbers, ligand-shell occupancies, anion
orientations, cluster sizes, local density, and ion-atmosphere coordinates.
Every coordinate must have a unit, a physical meaning, a gradient, a mobility
entry, and a role in the energy, polarization, basin assignment, or memory
coordinate.

\subsection{Charge polarization}

The formal-charge polarization is
\[
P_x(q)
=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal Q}
z_\alpha r_\alpha^{\rm unwrap}(q)
\in\mathbb R^3.
\]
The gradient is
\[
G_P(q)=\nabla_qP_x(q)\in\mathbb R^{3\times d},
\qquad
(G_P)_{a\ell}=\frac{\partial P_{x,a}}{\partial q_\ell}.
\]
Formal charge units give the conductivity prefactor \(F^2/(RT)\). Physical
charge units would use \(1/(k_BT V)\).

\subsection{Free energy}
\label{sec:forcefield}

The molar free energy is
\[
U_x(q)=
U_{\rm bonded}
+
U_{\rm LJ}
+
U_{\rm Coul}
+
U_{\rm pol}^{\rm eff}
+
U_{\rm solv}
+
U_{\rm coord}
+
U_{\rm pack}
+
U_{\rm atm}
+
U_{\rm ext},
\]
with bonded terms
\[
U_{\rm bond}
=
\sum_{(u,v)}\frac12k_{uv}^{\rm b}(\ell_{uv}-\ell_{uv}^0)^2,
\]
\[
U_{\rm angle}
=
\sum_{(u,v,w)}\frac12k_{uvw}^{\theta}(\theta_{uvw}-\theta_{uvw}^0)^2,
\]
\[
U_{\rm torsion}
=
\sum_{(u,v,w,z)}\sum_n
V_{uv wz,n}\,[1+\cos(n\varphi_{uv wz}-\gamma_{uv wz,n})].
\]
The short-range term is
\[
U_{\rm LJ}
=
\sum_{\alpha<\beta}
N_A\,4\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
\left[
\left(\frac{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{r_{\alpha\beta}}\right)^{12}
-
\left(\frac{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{r_{\alpha\beta}}\right)^6
\right]
f_{\rm cut}(r_{\alpha\beta}),
\]
where the mixing rules are
\[
\sigma_{\alpha\beta}
=
\frac{\sigma_\alpha+\sigma_\beta}{2},
\qquad
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
=
\sqrt{\epsilon_\alpha\epsilon_\beta},
\]
and the smooth cutoff is
\[
f_{\rm cut}(r)
=
\begin{cases}
1, & r\le r_{\rm on},\\
1-10s^3+15s^4-6s^5, & r_{\rm on}<r<r_{\rm off},\\
0, & r\ge r_{\rm off},
\end{cases}
\qquad
s=\frac{r-r_{\rm on}}{r_{\rm off}-r_{\rm on}}.
\]
The separation \(r_{\alpha\beta}\) uses the minimum-image convention under
periodic boundaries.

For formal site charge \(z_\alpha\), the SI charge is
\[
q_\alpha^{\rm SI}=e z_\alpha.
\]
For periodic boundaries, electrostatics are evaluated by the Ewald split
\[
U_{\rm Coul}
=
\frac{N_A}{4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_\infty}
\left[
U_{\rm real}
+
U_{\rm reciprocal}
+
U_{\rm self}
+
U_{\rm surface}
\right],
\]
with
\[
U_{\rm real}
=
\frac12
\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}
q_\alpha^{\rm SI}q_\beta^{\rm SI}
\frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\alpha_E r_{\alpha\beta})}{r_{\alpha\beta}},
\]
\[
U_{\rm reciprocal}
=
\frac{2\pi}{V_{\rm cell}}
\sum_{k\ne0}
\frac{\exp[-\|k\|^2/(4\alpha_E^2)]}{\|k\|^2}
\left|
\sum_\alpha q_\alpha^{\rm SI}
\exp(\mathrm i\, k\cdot r_\alpha)
\right|^2,
\]
\[
U_{\rm self}
=
-\frac{\alpha_E}{\sqrt{\pi}}
\sum_\alpha (q_\alpha^{\rm SI})^2.
\]
For conducting external boundary conditions,
\[
U_{\rm surface}=0.
\]
For vacuum external boundary conditions,
\[
U_{\rm surface}
=
\frac{2\pi}{3V_{\rm cell}}
\left|
\sum_\alpha q_\alpha^{\rm SI}r_\alpha
\right|^2.
\]

For induced dipoles, the polarization energy is
\[
U_{\rm pol}(q,\{\mu_\alpha\})
=
N_A
\left[
\sum_\alpha
\frac12
\mu_\alpha^\T\alpha_\alpha^{-1}\mu_\alpha
-
\sum_\alpha \mu_\alpha^\T E_\alpha(q)
+
\frac12
\sum_{\alpha\ne\beta}
\mu_\alpha^\T T_{\alpha\beta}\mu_\beta
\right].
\]
The induced dipoles are eliminated by energy minimization:
\[
\{\mu_\alpha^\star(q)\}
=
\argmin_{\{\mu_\alpha\}}
U_{\rm pol}(q,\{\mu_\alpha\}),
\qquad
U_{\rm pol}^{\rm eff}(q)
=
U_{\rm pol}(q,\{\mu_\alpha^\star(q)\}).
\]
If polarizability is not part of the physical species description, then
\[
U_{\rm pol}^{\rm eff}(q)=0.
\]

The compact pairwise Coulomb expression
\[
U_{\rm Coul}^{\rm pair}
=
\frac{F^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
\sum_{\alpha<\beta}
\frac{z_\alpha z_\beta}{\epsilon_{\rm loc}(q)r_{\alpha\beta}},
\]
is only the nonperiodic limiting form after the boundary and screening
convention has been chosen.
Solvation and coordination terms use descriptors such as donor number,
acceptor number, denticity, ligand asymmetry, Born radius, and coordination
site count:
\[
U_{\rm coord}
=
\sum_{\rm Li}\sum_{\ell}
g_\ell(n_{\rm Li,\ell},r_{\rm Li,\ell},\Omega_{\rm Li,\ell}).
\]
Packing and free-volume terms penalize dense states:
\[
U_{\rm pack}=RT\,\Phi_{\rm pack}(\eta_{\rm hs},v_{\rm free},n_{\rm cluster}).
\]
The total equilibrium density is
\[
\rho_x(\dd q)
=
Z_x^{-1}\exp[-U_x(q)/(RT)]\,\dd q.
\]

\subsection{Mobility tensor}
\label{sec:mobility}

The coordinate mobility tensor satisfies
\[
D_x(q)=D_x(q)^\T,\qquad D_x(q)\succeq0.
\]
When built from resistance,
\[
D_x(q)=k_BT\,R_x(q)^\#,
\]
with
\[
R_x=
R_{\rm hydro}
+
R_{\rm crowd}
+
R_{\rm charge\ cloud}
+
R_{\rm atmosphere}
+
R_{\rm cage}
+
R_{\rm constraint}.
\]
Equivalently, an explicit atomistic resistance split is
\[
R_x(q)
=
R_{\rm Stokes}(q)
+
R_{\rm hydro}(q)
+
R_{\rm constraint}(q)
+
R_{\rm atmosphere}(q)
+
R_{\rm cage}(q)
+
R_{\rm microvisc}(q).
\]
Local viscosity can depend on composition and coordinate:
\[
\eta(q)
=
\eta_{\rm bulk}(x)
f_{\rm salt}(I(q))
f_{\rm additive}(q)
f_{\rm free}( \varphi(q)).
\]
A concrete salt, additive, and free-volume form is
\[
f_{\rm salt}(I)
=
1+A_\eta\sqrt I+B_\eta I,
\]
\[
f_{\rm additive}(q)
=
1+\sum_L a_L n_L(q),
\]
\[
f_{\rm free}(\varphi)
=
\left(1-\frac{\varphi}{\varphi_{\max}}\right)^{-\gamma}.
\]
A site Stokes resistance is
\[
R_{\alpha\alpha}^{\rm Stokes}(q)
=
6\pi\eta(q)a_\alpha I_3.
\]
Hydrodynamic cross mobility may use a positive semidefinite Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa
block:
\[
H_{\alpha\beta}(q)
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{6\pi\eta(q)a_\alpha}I_3,
& \alpha=\beta,\\[7pt]
\dfrac{1}{8\pi\eta(q)r_{\alpha\beta}}
\left[
I_3+\hat r_{\alpha\beta}\hat r_{\alpha\beta}^{\T}
+
\dfrac{2(a_\alpha^2+a_\beta^2)}{3r_{\alpha\beta}^2}
\left(
I_3-3\hat r_{\alpha\beta}\hat r_{\alpha\beta}^{\T}
\right)
\right],
& \alpha\ne\beta,\ r_{\alpha\beta}>a_\alpha+a_\beta.
\end{cases}
\]
The hydrodynamic contribution is
\[
D_{\rm hydro}(q)=k_BT\,H(q).
\]
Constraint resistance penalizes bonded, caged, or aggregated motions, and
atmosphere resistance penalizes slow charge-cloud relaxation. Each resistance
contribution must be symmetric positive semidefinite before forming
\(D_x(q)=k_BT\,R_x(q)^\#\).

\subsection{Reversible generator}
\label{sec:generator}

The thermal factor is
\[
\beta_{\rm mol}=\frac{1}{RT}.
\]
For overdamped Smoluchowski dynamics,
\[
L_x f(q)
=
\frac{1}{\rho_x(q)}
\nabla_q\cdot
\left(\rho_x(q)D_x(q)\nabla_q f(q)\right).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
L_x f
=
D_x:\nabla_q\nabla_q f
+
\left[
\nabla_q\cdot D_x
-
\beta_{\rm mol}D_x\nabla_qU_x
\right]\cdot\nabla_qf.
\]
This generator is reversible with respect to \(\rho_x\).

\subsection{Reduced generator projection}

When the calculation uses reduced coordinates, define a smooth map
\[
\xi=\Xi(q).
\]
The reduced free energy is the potential of mean force
\[
U_{\rm red}(\xi)
=
-RT
\log
\int_{\Omega_x}
\delta(\Xi(q)-\xi)
\exp[-U_x(q)/(RT)]\,\dd q.
\]
The reduced mobility is the conditional projected mobility
\[
D_{\rm red}(\xi)
=
\mathbb E_{\rho_x}
\left[
\nabla_q\Xi(q)D_x(q)\nabla_q\Xi(q)^\T
\mid
\Xi(q)=\xi
\right].
\]
The reduced polarization is
\[
P_{\rm red}(\xi)
=
\mathbb E_{\rho_x}
\left[
P_x(q)
\mid
\Xi(q)=\xi
\right].
\]
The reduced basins are
\[
A_i^{\rm red}
=
\{\xi:\xi\in B_i\},
\qquad
B_i=\Xi(A_i).
\]
After this projection, the same Smoluchowski form is used with
\[
U_x,D_x,P_x,A_i
\]
replaced by
\[
U_{\rm red},D_{\rm red},P_{\rm red},A_i^{\rm red}.
\]

\subsection{Projection basins}
\label{sec:basins}

Choose disjoint basins
\[
\mathcal A=\{A_1,\ldots,A_N\},
\qquad
A_i\subset\Omega_x,
\qquad
A_i\cap A_j=\varnothing.
\]
Each coordinate belongs to one basin. Smooth switching functions can define pair
basins, ligand-conditioned basins, anion-orientation basins, aggregate basins,
bridge/network basins, and local free-volume basins. A basin has a
stoichiometry vector
\[
\nu_i=(\nu_{i,a})_{a\in\mathcal C},
\]
where \(\nu_{i,a}\) counts conserved component \(a\) in motif \(i\).

\subsection{Quadrature and mass balance}
\label{sec:massbalance}

For each basin \(A_i\), choose quadrature points and weights
\[
\{(q_{i\ell},w_{i\ell})\}_{\ell=1}^{n_i}.
\]
The restricted grand-canonical weight at chemical potentials
\(\lambda=(\lambda_a)_{a\in\mathcal C}\) is
\[
Z_i(\lambda)
=
\sum_\ell
w_{i\ell}
\exp[-\beta_{\rm mol}U_x(q_{i\ell})
+\lambda\cdot\nu_i].
\]
The basin concentration is
\[
c_i(\lambda)=\frac{Z_i(\lambda)}{V_{\rm ref}}.
\]
Mass balance solves
\[
r_a(\lambda)
=
\sum_i\nu_{i,a}c_i(\lambda)-C_a
=0
\qquad
\forall a\in\mathcal C.
\]
The Jacobian is
\[
\frac{\partial r_a}{\partial\lambda_b}
=
\sum_i\nu_{i,a}\nu_{i,b}c_i(\lambda).
\]
Damped Newton updates
\[
\lambda^{(k+1)}=\lambda^{(k)}-\alpha_kJ(\lambda^{(k)})^\#r(\lambda^{(k)})
\]
with line-search damping until the residual and step norms meet tolerance.

\subsection{Committors and capacity flux}
\label{sec:fluxes}

For a transition \(i\to j\), the committor \(q_{ij}\) solves
\[
L_x q_{ij}=0
\quad\text{on }\Omega_x\setminus(A_i\cup A_j),
\]
\[
q_{ij}=0\quad\text{on }A_i,
\qquad
q_{ij}=1\quad\text{on }A_j.
\]
The symmetric capacity flux is
\[
K_{ij}
=
\int_{\Omega_x}
\nabla q_{ij}^{\T}D_x\nabla q_{ij}\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\qquad
K_{ij}=K_{ji}\ge0.
\]
The finite reversible generator is
\[
Q_{ij}=\frac{K_{ij}}{c_i}\quad(i\ne j),
\qquad
Q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}Q_{ij}.
\]
It satisfies detailed balance:
\[
c_iQ_{ij}=c_jQ_{ji}.
\]

\subsection{Transition-path displacement moments}
\label{sec:tpmoments}

For a reactive transition path from \(i\) to \(j\), let
\[
\Delta P_{ij}=P_x(q_{\rm exit})-P_x(q_{\rm entry})
\]
be the unwrapped charge-polarization displacement. The first and second
moments are
\[
d_{ij}=\mathbb E[\Delta P_{ij}],
\qquad
M_{ij}=\mathbb E[\Delta P_{ij}\Delta P_{ij}^{\T}].
\]
They satisfy
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij}.
\]
Moment estimators must reject path samples with unphysical displacement scale
\(\|\Delta P_{ij}\|>L_{\max}\).

\subsection{Self-current and multi-center covariance}
\label{sec:selfcurrent}

Within basin \(A_i\), define an unbounded transport projector \(\Pi_i(q)\) that
maps coordinate displacement to unwrapped charge-polarization displacement. The
self-current tensor is
\[
D_i^{\rm self}
=
\frac{1}{c_i}
\int_{A_i}
\Pi_i(q)D_x(q)\Pi_i(q)^\T
\rho_x(\dd q).
\]
For a multi-center charged motif with centers \(a,b\), charges \(z_a,z_b\), and
center diffusivity covariance \(D_{ab}\),
\[
D_i^{\rm self}
=
\sum_{a,b\in i}z_az_bD_{ab}.
\]
This expression handles neutral pairs and multi-charge motifs correctly because
neutral center-of-mass motion contributes according to the full charge covariance,
not according to net charge squared alone.

\section{Concrete Projection and Memory Construction}
\label{sec:concreteprojection}

\subsection{Switching functions}

Use smooth switching functions so basin assignments can be tested against
continuous descriptor changes. For a distance \(r\), threshold \(r_0\), and
width \(\Delta r>0\),
\[
s_-(r;r_0,\Delta r)=
\frac{1}{1+\exp[(r-r_0)/\Delta r]},
\qquad
s_+(r;r_0,\Delta r)=1-s_-(r;r_0,\Delta r).
\]
For a coordination shell around a lithium center,
\[
n_{\rm Li,\ell}(q)
=
\sum_{u\in\ell}s_-(r_{{\rm Li},u};r_{\rm shell},\Delta r_{\rm shell}).
\]
For a local density or packing coordinate,
\[
\rho_{\rm loc}(q)
=
\sum_{\alpha}
s_-(\|r_\alpha-r_{\rm Li}\|;r_{\rm loc},\Delta r_{\rm loc}).
\]

\subsection{Pair, ligand, cluster, and orientation basins}

For an anion \(A\), let \(r_{\rm LiA}\) be the Li-anion separation. Define pair
membership weights for free, solvent-separated, contact, and aggregate-like
states:
\[
w_{\rm free}=s_+(r_{\rm LiA};r_{\rm SSIP},\Delta r),
\]
\[
w_{\rm SSIP}=s_-(r_{\rm LiA};r_{\rm SSIP},\Delta r)
s_+(r_{\rm LiA};r_{\rm CIP},\Delta r),
\]
\[
w_{\rm CIP}=s_-(r_{\rm LiA};r_{\rm CIP},\Delta r)
s_+(n_{\rm cluster};n_{\rm agg},\Delta n),
\]
\[
w_{\rm AGG}=s_-(n_{\rm cluster};n_{\rm agg},\Delta n).
\]
Ligand-conditioned states use binned ligand counts
\[
b_\ell(q)=\operatorname{bin}(n_{\rm Li,\ell}(q)),
\]
for solvent and additive ligand families. Anion orientation uses an orientation
coordinate
\[
\omega_A(q)=\hat u_A(q)\cdot\hat r_{\rm LiA}(q),
\]
with bins for end-on, side-on, and bridging geometries. The final basin index is
the joint index
\[
i=
\mathcal I\!\left(
\text{pair class},
\{b_\ell\}_{\ell},
\text{orientation bin},
\text{cluster bin},
\text{free-volume bin}
\right).
\]
Empty or physically inconsistent joint states are removed before the generator
is assembled.

\subsection{Memory coordinates}

The memory basis \(\Psi=(\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_m)\) contains coordinates whose
relaxation carries current memory not represented by Markov jumps alone.

Charge-density and atmosphere modes are
\[
\psi_{k,c}(q)=
\sum_\alpha z_\alpha\cos(k\cdot r_\alpha),
\qquad
\psi_{k,s}(q)=
\sum_\alpha z_\alpha\sin(k\cdot r_\alpha),
\]
for the lowest reciprocal vectors \(k\). A cage coordinate for ion \(\alpha\) is
\[
\psi_{\rm cage,\alpha}
=
(r_\alpha-r_{\rm cage,\alpha})\cdot\hat e,
\]
where \(r_{\rm cage,\alpha}\) is the local neighbor-center coordinate and
\(\hat e\) is a Cartesian direction or local principal direction. Partner
residence is
\[
\psi_{{\rm partner},\alpha A}
=
s_-(r_{\alpha A};r_{\rm pair},\Delta r).
\]
Ligand-shell residence is
\[
\psi_{{\rm ligand},\alpha\ell}
=
\sum_{u\in\ell}s_-(r_{\alpha u};r_{\rm shell},\Delta r_{\rm shell}).
\]
Anion-orientation memory is
\[
\psi_{\rm orient,A}
=
\omega_A-\langle\omega_A\rangle_{A_i}.
\]
Local free-volume memory is
\[
\psi_{\rm fv}
=
v_{\rm free}(q)-\langle v_{\rm free}\rangle_{A_i}.
\]

\subsection{State-indicator orthogonalization}

Memory coordinates must not duplicate state indicators. For each raw memory
coordinate \(\psi_\alpha\), define its state mean
\[
\bar\psi_{\alpha i}
=
\frac{1}{c_i}
\int_{A_i}\psi_\alpha(q)\,\rho_x(\dd q).
\]
The orthogonalized coordinate is
\[
\psi_\alpha^\perp(q)
=
\psi_\alpha(q)
-
\sum_i\mathbf 1_{A_i}(q)\bar\psi_{\alpha i}.
\]
The memory basis used in the Mori correction is \(\Psi^\perp\), not the raw
coordinate set.

\subsection{Weak-form committor solve}

For each edge \(i\leftrightarrow j\), seek \(q_{ij}\) in the admissible
Sobolev space with boundary values \(q_{ij}=0\) on \(A_i\) and \(q_{ij}=1\) on
\(A_j\). For every test function \(v\) vanishing on those boundaries,
\[
\int_{\Omega_x\setminus(A_i\cup A_j)}
\nabla v^\T D_x\nabla q_{ij}\,\rho_x(\dd q)
=0.
\]
With finite basis functions \(\{\varphi_a\}\), the linear system is
\[
\sum_b
\left[
\int\nabla\varphi_a^\T D_x\nabla\varphi_b\,\rho_x(\dd q)
\right]\theta_b
=
-
\int\nabla\varphi_a^\T D_x\nabla q_{\rm bc}\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\]
where \(q_{\rm bc}\) carries the boundary values. The capacity flux follows from
the converged weak solution.

\subsection{Deterministic transition-moment equations}

The unconditioned drift of the reversible diffusion is
\[
b(q)=\nabla_q\cdot D_x(q)-\beta_{\rm mol}D_x(q)\nabla_qU_x(q).
\]
Let \(h(q)=q_{ij}(q)\). The generator conditioned to reach \(A_j\) before
\(A_i\) is
\[
L_{ij}^h f
=
L_x f+2D_x\nabla_q\log h\cdot\nabla_q f.
\]
For Cartesian component \(a\), solve the endpoint mean boundary-value problem
\[
L_{ij}^h u_a=0
\quad\text{in }\Omega_{ij},
\qquad
u_a=P_{x,a}
\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
For Cartesian pair \(a,b\), solve the endpoint second-moment problem
\[
L_{ij}^h w_{ab}=0
\quad\text{in }\Omega_{ij},
\qquad
w_{ab}=P_{x,a}P_{x,b}
\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
The reactive exit density from \(\partial A_i\) is
\[
\eta_i(q)
=
\frac{\rho_x(q)n_i(q)^\T D_x(q)\nabla_q h(q)}{K_{ij}}.
\]
The first transition moment is
\[
d_{ij,a}
=
\int_{\partial A_i}
\eta_i(q)[u_a(q)-P_{x,a}(q)]\,\dd S(q).
\]
The second transition moment is
\[
M_{ij,ab}
=
\int_{\partial A_i}
\eta_i(q)
\left[
w_{ab}-P_{x,a}u_b-P_{x,b}u_a+P_{x,a}P_{x,b}
\right](q)\,\dd S(q).
\]
Validation requires
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij},
\qquad
\|\Delta P\|\le L_{\max}.
\]

\subsection{Density quadrature weights}

After chemical potentials are solved, the normalized density weight at basin
point \(q_{i\ell}\) is
\[
W_{i\ell}
=
\frac{
w_{i\ell}
\exp[-\beta_{\rm mol}U_x(q_{i\ell})+\lambda^\star\cdot\nu_i]
}{
\sum_{j,k}
w_{jk}
\exp[-\beta_{\rm mol}U_x(q_{jk})+\lambda^\star\cdot\nu_j]
}.
\]
All quadrature estimates of self-current, memory matrices, and basin moments
use these density weights. A calculation that computes populations with one
weighting rule and transport moments with another fails the model contract.

\Delta P_{ij}.
\]
The transition families include free-SSIP, SSIP-CIP, aggregate formation and
breakup, Li-ligand binding, Li-ligand-anion formation, additive-separated SSIP,
partner switching, identity diffusion, structural hopping, bridge/network
formation and breakup, cage capture and release, and atmosphere capture and
release. Pure motif exchange uses
\[
d_{ij}=0,\qquad M_{ij}=0.
\]
A structural displacement uses
\[
\Delta P=P(q_{\rm end})-P(q_{\rm start}),
\qquad
d_{ij}=\mathbb E[\Delta P],
\qquad
M_{ij}=\mathbb E[\Delta P\Delta P^\T].
\]

The committor calculation requires
\[
\Omega_{ij},\quad \partial A_i,\quad \partial A_j,\quad
\mathcal T_{ij}^{(r)},\quad \{\varphi_m\},\quad
{\rm quadrature\ points\ and\ weights},\quad
\theta_{\rm refine},\quad \tau_K,\quad c_{\min},\quad K_{\min}.
\]
The transition-path calculation requires
\[
p_{ij}^{\rm exit},\quad q_{\min},\quad \Delta t_{\max},\quad
\gamma_t,\quad \gamma_b,\quad L_{\max},\quad
B_{\rm block},\quad \tau_d,\quad \tau_M,\quad N_{\max}.
\]
The committor numerical record must also prescribe the linear solver, the
preconditioner, and the quadrature rule used in every stiffness entry:
\[
S_{mn}^{(r)}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D\nabla\varphi_n\,\rho(\dd q),
\qquad
S^{(r)}u^{(r)}=f^{(r)}.
\]
The capacity estimate is
\[
K_{ij}^{(r)}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)\T}D\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)}\,\rho(\dd q),
\]
and refinement stops only when
\[
\frac{|K_{ij}^{(r+1)}-K_{ij}^{(r)}|}
{\max(K_{ij}^{(r)},K_{\min})}
<\tau_K.
\]
The transition-moment record must prescribe the boundary quadrature, conditioned
moment basis, conditioned linear solver, endpoint interpolation rule, and
residual tolerance. The conditioned generator is
\[
L_{ij}^h f
=
L f+2D(q)\nabla\log q_{ij}(q)\cdot\nabla f,
\]
\[
\nabla\log q_{ij}(q)
=
\frac{\nabla q_{ij}(q)}{\max(q_{ij}(q),q_{\min})}.
\]
For Cartesian component \(a\), solve
\[
L_{ij}^h u_a=0,
\qquad
u_a=P_a\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
For Cartesian pair \(a,b\), solve
\[
L_{ij}^h w_{ab}=0,
\qquad
w_{ab}=P_aP_b\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
Surface quadrature on \(\partial A_i\) gives
\[
d_{ij,a}=
\sum_\ell w_\ell^\Sigma\eta_i(q_\ell^\Sigma)
[u_a(q_\ell^\Sigma)-P_a(q_\ell^\Sigma)],
\]
\[
M_{ij,ab}=
\sum_\ell w_\ell^\Sigma\eta_i(q_\ell^\Sigma)
\left[
w_{ab}-P_au_b-P_bu_a+P_aP_b
\right]_{q=q_\ell^\Sigma}.
\]
Doob-conditioned sample paths are an alternate estimator for the same local
boundary-value equations.

For every state \(\alpha\), prescribe charged centers
\[
\mathcal C_\alpha=\{p\},
\qquad
z_{\alpha p},\qquad
\rho_{\alpha p},\qquad
R_\alpha^{\rm Stokes},R_\alpha^{\rm hydro},R_\alpha^{\rm shape},
R_\alpha^{\rm free\ volume},R_\alpha^{\rm charge\ cloud},
R_\alpha^{\rm atmosphere},R_\alpha^{\rm constraint},R_\alpha^{\rm cage}.
\]
Then
\[
R_\alpha
=
R_\alpha^{\rm Stokes}
+R_\alpha^{\rm hydro}
+R_\alpha^{\rm shape}
+R_\alpha^{\rm free\ volume}
+R_\alpha^{\rm charge\ cloud}
+R_\alpha^{\rm atmosphere}
+R_\alpha^{\rm constraint}
+R_\alpha^{\rm cage},
\qquad
D_\alpha=k_BT R_\alpha^\#.
\]
If \(Z_\alpha\) maps center velocities to charge displacement, the state-local
charge covariance is
\[
D_\alpha^Q=Z_\alpha D_\alpha Z_\alpha^\T.
\]
For a Li-anion state,
\[
D_Q(\alpha)=D_{\rm Li|\alpha}+D_{A|\alpha}-2D_{\rm Li,A|\alpha}.
\]
Ligand-conditioned anticorrelation \(D_{\rm Li,A|\alpha}<0\) is therefore an
allowed conductivity-increasing covariance channel.

Memory coordinates must be supplied as numerical functions:
\[
\psi_{\rm atm},\quad
\psi_{\rm charge\ density},\quad
\psi_{\rm cage},\quad
\psi_{\rm partner},\quad
\psi_{\rm ligand},\quad
\psi_{\rm orient},\quad
\psi_{\rm fv/stress}.
\]
Examples are
\[
\psi_{k,c}(q)=\sum_pz_p\cos(k\cdot r_p),
\qquad
\psi_{k,s}(q)=\sum_pz_p\sin(k\cdot r_p),
\]
\[
\psi_{\rm ligand}=n_{\rm Li,L}-\bar n_{\rm Li,L},
\qquad
\psi_{\rm orient}=u_A\cdot\hat r_{\rm LiA},
\qquad
\psi_{\rm fv}=\varphi-\bar\varphi.
\]
State orthogonalization is
\[
\psi_\alpha^\perp(q)
=
\psi_\alpha(q)-\mathbb E[\psi_\alpha\mid A_{s(q)}].
\]

Every basin, transition surface, and transition path must carry quadrature:
\[
\{q_{i\ell},w_{i\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n_i},
\qquad
\{q_{ij,\ell}^{\Sigma},w_{ij,\ell}^{\Sigma}\},
\qquad
\{\Delta P_{ij,\ell},w_{ij,\ell}^{\rm path}\}.
\]
The numerical tolerance set is part of \(\mathcal L\):
\[
\tau_{\rm mass},\quad \tau_K,\quad \tau_d,\quad \tau_M,\quad
\tau_{\rm PSD},\quad \tau_\#,\quad \tau_{\rm null},\quad
L_{\max},\quad Z_{\min},\quad c_{\min},\quad K_{\min}.
\]
The required numerical parameter groups are

\subsection{Physical-library population and builder requirements}

Recipe names and concentrations do not determine conductivity until the recipe
is expanded into a populated physical library. For a formulation, define
\[
x=(r,\mathcal L),
\qquad
r=(T,\{C_a\},\{\phi_s\}),
\]
where \(r\) is the recipe and \(\mathcal L\) is the numerical physical library.
The builder is the deterministic map
\[
(r,\mathcal L)
\rightarrow
\left[
U_x(q),D_x(q),P_x(q),A_i,\psi_\alpha(q),
\Sigma_{ij},\Delta P_{ij}
\right]
\rightarrow
(c,K,Q,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h)
\rightarrow
\sigma.
\]
Here \(\Sigma_{ij}\) is the transition surface or transition domain between
basins \(A_i\) and \(A_j\), and \(\Delta P_{ij}\) is the charge-polarization
displacement along local transition paths. Every conductivity effect enters
through
\[
c_i,\qquad K_{ij}/Q_{ij},\qquad d_{ij}/M_{ij},\qquad D_i/R_i,\qquad A,h.
\]
There is no additional terminal scalar correction.

The physical library is decomposed as
\[
\mathcal L=
\left(
\mathcal L_{\rm species},
\mathcal L_{\rm interactions},
\mathcal L_{\rm mixture},
\mathcal L_{\rm mobility},
\mathcal L_{\rm basis},
\mathcal L_{\rm transition/memory}
\right).
\]
Each sublibrary is populated from direct molecular data and force-field data,
quantum calculations, trajectory-derived primitive measurements, or
primitive-level calibration against measured primitive observables. Scalar
conductivity labels are outer validation targets for the completed primitive
map. They are not a substitute for hidden primitives such as occupancies,
reversible rates, residence times, displacement moments, state-local
covariances, and memory couplings.

\subsubsection{Determining \(U_x(q)\)}

The free-energy model is
\[
U_x(q)=
U_{\rm bonded}
+U_{\rm LJ}
+U_{\rm Coul}
+U_{\rm pol}
+U_{\rm Born}
+U_{\rm coord}
+U_{\rm solv}
+U_{\rm pack}
+U_{\rm activity}
+U_{\rm cluster}.
\]
For each species \(s\), the species record contains sites
\[
\mathcal I_s=\{1,\ldots,n_s\}.
\]
For each site \(u\), it contains
\[
m_{su},\ r_{su}^{\rm atom},\ a_{su}^{\rm hyd},\ v_{su},\
\sigma_{su},\ \epsilon_{su},\ z_{su},\
a_{su}^{\rm cloud},\ a_{su}^{\rm Born},\ \alpha_{su}.
\]
Masses come from atomic composition. Bonds, angles, torsions, and constraints
come from the molecular graph and selected force-field topology. Formal charge
comes from ion identity and oxidation state. Partial charge comes from an
electrostatic-potential fit, charge equilibration, population analysis, or a
selected force field. Charge-cloud radius comes from electron-density extent,
fitted charge smearing, or solvation/atmosphere response. Born radius comes
from cavity construction or solvation free-energy fit. Polarizability comes
from quantum polarizability or a library value. LJ parameters come from
force-field atom types or fits to liquid structure, density, solvation, PMF,
and diffusion.
The topology is site-level. Molecule-level scalar records do not determine
\(P_x(q)\), charge-cloud resistance, state geometry, or multi-center mobility.

For site pairs \(a,b\), prescribe or compute
\[
\sigma_{ab},\qquad \epsilon_{ab}.
\]
The default mixing rule is
\[
\sigma_{ab}=\frac{\sigma_a+\sigma_b}{2},
\qquad
\epsilon_{ab}=\sqrt{\epsilon_a\epsilon_b}.
\]
When this rule fails to reproduce ion-pair PMFs or solvation structure, fit
specific cross corrections
\[
\Delta\sigma_{ab},\qquad \Delta\epsilon_{ab}
\]
against Li-anion PMFs, Li-solvent coordination numbers, anion-solvent radial
distribution functions, density, solvation free energies, diffusion
coefficients, and pair association constants. Then
\[
U_{\rm LJ}
=
N_A\sum_{a<b}
4\epsilon_{ab}
\left[
\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r_{ab}}\right)^{12}
-
\left(\frac{\sigma_{ab}}{r_{ab}}\right)^6
\right]f_{\rm cut}(r_{ab}).
\]

The Coulomb policy specifies
\[
\epsilon_\infty,\qquad
\alpha_{\rm Ewald},\qquad
r_{\rm real},\qquad
k_{\max},\qquad
{\rm boundary\ convention},
\]
and fixes
\[
U_{\rm Coul}
=
N_A\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0\epsilon_\infty}
\left[
U_{\rm real}
+U_{\rm reciprocal}
+U_{\rm self}
+U_{\rm surface}
\right].
\]
For a nonpolarizable model, the electronic screening convention must be
specified: fixed partial charges either already include electronic screening or
are scaled consistently with \(\epsilon_\infty\). Mixed conventions are invalid.

For every charge site \(u\), Born charging is
\[
\Delta G_{su}^{\rm Born}(e)
=
-\frac{N_Ae^2z_{su}^2}
{8\pi\epsilon_0a_{su}^{\rm Born}}
\left(1-\frac{1}{\epsilon_e}\right).
\]
The Born radius comes from cavity construction or solvation free-energy fitting;
\(\epsilon_e\) is the local dielectric for the relevant basin; and contact
occlusion weights measure the fraction of solvation surface lost on pair or
cluster formation. For pair \(s,t\),
\[
\Delta G_{st}^{\rm desolv}
=
\sum_{u\in s}\sum_{v\in t}
\omega_{uv}^{\rm contact}
\left[
\Delta G_{su}^{\rm Born}(e_{\rm paired})
+\Delta G_{tv}^{\rm Born}(e_{\rm paired})
-\Delta G_{su}^{\rm Born}(e_{\rm bulk})
-\Delta G_{tv}^{\rm Born}(e_{\rm bulk})
\right].
\]
The occlusion weights are computed from solvent-accessible surface loss or
from contact geometry in the pair or cluster ensemble.

For each center \(a\) and ligand class \(L\),
\[
s_{aL}(r)=\frac{1}{1+(r/r_{aL}^0)^{p_{aL}}},
\qquad
n_{aL}(q)=\sum_{b\in L}s_{aL}(r_{ab}).
\]
The parameters \(r_{aL}^0\), \(p_{aL}\), and \(\Delta g_{aL}^{\rm coord}\)
come from radial-distribution minima, coordination-number plateaus,
umbrella-sampled PMFs, ab initio cluster binding free energies,
trajectory-measured shell occupancy, or experimental coordination data. Then
\[
U_{\rm coord}
=
N_A\sum_{a,L}\Delta g_{aL}^{\rm coord}n_{aL}(q).
\]

For a solvated center, set
\[
n_a=(n_{aL_1},\ldots,n_{aL_m}).
\]
The target occupancy \(\bar n_a\) and competition matrix \(K^a\) are determined
from coordination covariance, cluster free-energy scans, donor/acceptor
competition, and solvation-shell composition data:
\[
U_{\rm solv}
=
N_A\sum_a\frac12(n_a-\bar n_a)^\T K^a(n_a-\bar n_a).
\]
Solvent and additive competition, including EC, DMC, EMC, FEC, and VC
coordination effects, enters \(U_x\) through this term and the coordination
PMFs, not through a final conductivity multiplier.

Enumerate cluster stoichiometries
\[
{\rm LiA},\quad {\rm Li_2A^+},\quad {\rm LiA_2^-},\quad
{\rm Li_2A_2},\quad {\rm higher\ clusters}.
\]
For cluster \(C\),
\[
G_C=
G_C^0
+G_C^{\rm Coul}
+G_C^{\rm desolv}
+G_C^{\rm coord}
+G_C^{\rm steric}
+G_C^{\rm entropy}
+G_C^{\rm activity}.
\]
The components are determined from representative site-charge cluster
geometries, desolvation occlusion, Li-solvent/Li-ligand/Li-anion coordination,
molecular volumes and available free volume, association degeneracy,
orientation count, conformational loss, ionic-strength activity, and
hard-sphere activity. Cluster populations then follow from mass action or
restricted partition integrals rather than direct scalar-conductivity fitting.

For packing, store molecular and site volumes \(V_s,v_{su}\), compute
\[
\varphi(q)=\sum_av_aW_a(q),
\]
and prescribe \(\varphi_{\max}\) and \(\gamma_{\rm fv}\). The packing term is
\[
U_{\rm pack}
=
RT\int
\frac{\varphi(r)(4-3\varphi(r))}
{(1-\varphi(r))^2}\,\dd r.
\]
If any quadrature point satisfies
\[
\varphi(r)\ge\varphi_{\max},
\]
then that configuration is physically invalid and \(U_{\rm pack}=+\infty\).

\subsubsection{Determining \(D_x(q)\)}

Mobility is obtained from resistance:
\[
D_x(q)=k_BT R_x(q)^\#,
\]
\[
R_x=
R_{\rm Stokes}
+R_{\rm hydro}
+R_{\rm shape}
+R_{\rm free\ volume}
+R_{\rm charge\ cloud}
+R_{\rm atmosphere}
+R_{\rm cage}
+R_{\rm constraint}.
\]
Each site or transport center has a hydrodynamic radius \(a_p^{\rm hyd}\)
determined from pure-component diffusivity, dilute Stokes-Einstein inversion,
bead models from molecular volume or solvent-accessible area, or isolated
species self-diffusion. For a state \(\alpha\), build a state geometry
\[
R_\alpha^{\rm geom}
=
\{r_{\alpha p},a_{\alpha p}^{\rm hyd}\}_{p\in\alpha}
\]
and construct resistance from all centers. A single molecule scalar does not
define the state mobility.

If Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa hydrodynamics is used,
\[
H_{pq}
=
\frac{1}{8\pi\eta r_{pq}}
\left[
I+\hat r\hat r^\T
+
\frac{2(a_p^2+a_q^2)}{3r_{pq}^2}
(I-3\hat r\hat r^\T)
\right],
\qquad p\ne q,
\]
\[
H_{pp}=\frac{1}{6\pi\eta a_p}I,
\qquad
D_{\rm hydro}=k_BTH.
\]
The viscosity \(\eta\) comes from the mixture viscosity closure.

For an anion or cluster, compute a shape tensor \(S_p\) from the inertia tensor,
site-position covariance, convex hull, or solvent-accessible surface tensor.
With normalized tensor
\[
S_p=\frac{I_p}{\operatorname{Tr}(I_p)},
\]
the scalar asymmetry is
\[
\ell_p^{\rm asym}
=
\frac{\lambda_{\max}(S_p)}{\lambda_{\min}(S_p)}-1.
\]
The shape resistance is
\[
R_p^{\rm shape}
=
\zeta_p(1+\ell_p^{\rm asym})^{\gamma_{\rm shape}}I.
\]
The exponent \(\gamma_{\rm shape}\) is determined from trajectory diffusion
anisotropy, ion mobility data, or primitive-level calibration against
\(D_i^{\rm self}\).

Charge-cloud density and resistance are
\[
\rho_p^{\rm charge}=\frac{|z_p|}{(a_p^{\rm cloud})^3},
\]
\[
R_p^{\rm cloud}
=
\lambda_{\rm cloud}
(\rho_p^{\rm charge})^{\gamma_{\rm cloud}}
F_{\rm screen}(\kappa a_p^{\rm cloud})I.
\]
The charge-cloud radius comes from charge distribution extent, charge smearing,
or solvation response. The coefficients \(\lambda_{\rm cloud}\) and
\(\gamma_{\rm cloud}\) are determined from ion self-diffusion, single-ion
mobility, or trajectory state-local self-current.

For every bound or caged state, define constraint modes
\[
s_m(q),\qquad \ell_m,\qquad \tau_m.
\]
Here \(s_m\) is the normalized gradient of the constrained coordinate,
\(\ell_m\) is the basin width or transition length, and \(\tau_m\) is the
residence lifetime from a PMF/capacity calculation or trajectory survival
function:
\[
S_m(t)=\Pr({\rm state\ persists\ beyond}\ t),
\qquad
\tau_m=\int_0^\infty S_m(t)\,\dd t.
\]
The resistance contribution is
\[
R_m=k_BT\frac{\tau_m}{\ell_m^2}s_ms_m^\T.
\]

The ion-atmosphere operator is a finite-size matrix response. With
\[
\kappa^2=
\frac{F^2}{\epsilon_0\epsilon_rRT}
\sum_i z_i^2c_i,
\]
\[
\rho_z(k;q)=\sum_pz_pe^{\mathrm i k\cdot r_p},
\]
and
\[
F_\alpha(k)
=
\sum_{p\in\alpha}
z_{\alpha p}
e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho_{\alpha p}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac12k^2(a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud})^2\right],
\]
use
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)=
\mathcal R_{\rm ep}(k)+\mathcal R_{\rm rel}(k),
\]
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm ep}(k)=
\Gamma_{\rm ep}\frac{k^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}\eta,
\]
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm rel}(k)=
\Gamma_{\rm rel}
\frac{\kappa^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}
\frac{1}{D_{\rm amb}k^2+\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}}.
\]
The coefficients \(\Gamma_{\rm ep}\) and \(\Gamma_{\rm rel}\) come from
limiting-law atmosphere theory, trajectory cross-current decomposition, or
primitive calibration of \(A,h\) and state resistance. They are not final
conductivity scale factors. Then
\[
R_\alpha^{\rm atm}
=
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
w_k|F_\alpha(k)|^2
\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)
G_{\alpha k}G_{\alpha k}^\T.
\]
Co-located neutral translation cancels because
\[
(+1)e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho}-1e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho}=0.
\]

\subsubsection{Determining \(P_x(q)\)}

Polarization is determined by charge sites and unwrapped coordinates:
\[
P_x(q)=
\sum_m\sum_u z_{m,u}R_{m,u}^{\rm unwrap}(q).
\]
The unwrapping convention is:
\[
R_{m,u}^{\rm unwrap}
=
r_{m,u}^{\rm wrapped}+L\,n_{m,u},
\qquad
n_{m,u}\in\mathbb Z^3.
\]
Each molecule is unwrapped so bonded atoms remain contiguous, molecular or site
image crossings are tracked between frames, and the integer image vector is
stored for every charged site. For analytical coordinates
\[
\xi=\Xi(q),
\qquad
J_\Xi=\frac{\partial\Xi}{\partial q},
\]
the reduced gradient is
\[
\nabla_\xi P=\nabla_qP\,J_\Xi^\#.
\]
The exact Cartesian derivative is
\[
\frac{\partial P_a}{\partial r_{m,u,b}}
=
z_{m,u}\delta_{ab}.
\]
If charge sites and unwrapping are absent, \(P_x\) is not determined.

\subsubsection{Determining basins, features, and stoichiometry}

Pair basins are determined from the Li-anion PMF
\[
F_{\rm LiA}(r)=-RT\log p(r).
\]
Use RDF minima or PMF saddle points to define \(r_{\rm CIP}\), \(r_{\rm SSIP}\),
and \(r_{\rm free}\):
\[
A_{\rm CIP}=\{r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm CIP}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm SSIP}=\{r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm free}=\{r_{\rm LiA}\ge r_{\rm free}\}.
\]

For additive-separated SSIP, define a Li-anion corridor and set
\[
\chi_{L{\rm sep}}(q)=1
\]
when ligand sites lie between Li and anion within the prescribed corridor
radius. The radius is determined from Li-ligand-anion trajectory geometry or
ligand steric radius. Then
\[
A_{\rm addSSIP}=A_{\rm SSIP}\cap\{\chi_{L{\rm sep}}=1\}.
\]

For Li-ligand basins,
\[
n_{\rm LiL}(q)=\sum_{b\in L}s_{\rm LiL}(r_{\rm Li,b}),
\]
and the cutoff \(n_L^{\rm cut}\) comes from minima in the coordination-number
distribution. Then
\[
A_{\rm LiL}=\{n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm LiL\cdots A}
=
\{n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut},\
r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP}\}.
\]
This ternary state is required so additives can form Li-ligand-anion states
instead of acting only through viscosity.

Aggregate and bridge basins come from the graph
\[
(a,b)\in E(q)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_{ab}<r_{\rm pair}^{\rm cut},
\]
where \(r_{\rm pair}^{\rm cut}\) is determined from PMF or RDF minima. Connected
components define
\[
N_{\rm Li}(C),\qquad N_A(C),\qquad z_C,
\]
and instantiate LiA, Li\(_2\)A\(^+\), LiA\(_2^-\), Li\(_2\)A\(_2\), higher
clusters, and bridge/network states.

Anion feature bins use
\[
a_A^{\rm hyd},\qquad
a_A^{\rm cloud},\qquad
\ell_A^{\rm asym},\qquad
m_A^{\rm dent},\qquad
\omega_A.
\]
Bin thresholds come from physical cutoffs or prescribed library quantiles. For
example,
\[
b_{\rm hyd}(A)=
\begin{cases}
{\rm compact}, & a_A<a_1,\\
{\rm medium}, & a_1\le a_A<a_2,\\
{\rm bulky}, & a_A\ge a_2.
\end{cases}
\]
Orientation is
\[
\omega_A=u_A\cdot\hat r_{\rm LiA},
\]
with radial, tangential, and bridging bins from prescribed thresholds.

Local environment bins use
\[
\varphi(q),\qquad I(q),\qquad \epsilon_{\rm loc}(q),\qquad \eta_{\rm loc}(q).
\]
The binning policy for packing, dielectric, viscosity, and ionic strength is
fixed before evaluation. It may use physical thresholds or adaptive quantiles,
but it must be part of \(\mathcal L\).

The joint state label is
\[
\alpha=
(f_{\rm pair},f_{\rm LiShell},f_{\rm ligand},f_{\rm anion},
f_{\rm orient},f_{\rm cluster},f_{\rm env},f_{\rm atm}).
\]
Instantiate \(A_\alpha\) only if
\[
Z_\alpha>Z_{\min}
\]
and the stoichiometry is feasible. Assign \(\nu_{\alpha,a}\) for free Li, free
anion, SSIP LiA, CIP LiA, Li-ligand, Li-ligand-anion, Li\(_2\)A\(^+\),
LiA\(_2^-\), Li\(_2\)A\(_2\), higher aggregates, and bridge/network states.
The mass balance uses a shared Li pool and per-anion free concentrations.

\subsubsection{Determining transitions and moments}

Each transition family prescribes
\[
\Omega_{ij},\qquad \partial A_i,\qquad \partial A_j,\qquad \xi_{ij}(q).
\]
The required families are free-SSIP, SSIP-CIP, free/SSIP/CIP-aggregate,
Li-Li-ligand, LiA-Li-ligand-anion, SSIP-additive-separated SSIP, partner
switching, identity diffusion, structural hopping, bridge/network formation and
breakup, cage capture/release, and atmosphere capture/release.

The committor solves
\[
Lq_{ij}=0
\]
on
\[
\Omega_{ij}=\Omega\setminus(A_i\cup A_j),
\]
with
\[
q_{ij}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial A_i,
\qquad
q_{ij}=1\ {\rm on}\ \partial A_j.
\]
The weak form is
\[
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla v^\T D\nabla q_{ij}\,\rho(\dd q)=0.
\]
The capacity flux is
\[
K_{ij}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla q_{ij}^\T D\nabla q_{ij}\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
In one-dimensional PMF reductions, the PDE is replaced by the Smoluchowski
resistance integral. In trajectory or Markov-state reductions, \(K_{ij}\) is
estimated by reversible transition counts and symmetrization.

For transition moments, solve the deterministic Doob-conditioned endpoint
moment hierarchy. The conditioned generator is
\[
L^h f
=
Lf+2D(q)\nabla\log q_{ij}(q)\cdot\nabla f,
\]
where \(h=q_{ij}\). Endpoint mean and second-moment functions satisfy
\[
L^hu_a=0,
\qquad
u_a=P_a\quad\text{on }\partial A_j,
\]
\[
L^hw_{ab}=0,
\qquad
w_{ab}=P_aP_b\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
The normalized reactive exit density is
\[
\eta_i(q)=
\frac{\rho(q)n_i(q)^\T D(q)\nabla h(q)}{K_{ij}}
\quad\text{on }\partial A_i.
\]
Then
\[
d_{ij,a}=
\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(q)[u_a(q)-P_a(q)]\,\dd S(q),
\]
\[
M_{ij,ab}=
\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(q)
[w_{ab}-P_au_b-P_bu_a+P_aP_b](q)\,\dd S(q).
\]
Pure motif exchange has
\[
d_{ij}=0,\qquad M_{ij}=0.
\]
Structural hops, identity diffusion, and partner switches have nonzero
\(d_{ij}\) or \(M_{ij}\) only when \(\Delta P\) is computed from unwrapped
charge polarization. Reversal requires
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij}.
\]

\subsubsection{Determining memory matrices}

Choose slow current-coupled memory functions
\[
\psi_\alpha(q)
\]
for atmosphere polarization, charge-density relaxation, cage/backjump,
partner residence, ligand-shell residence, anion orientation/denticity, local
free-volume/stress, and bounded internal polarization. For state \(i\),
\[
\bar\psi_{\alpha,i}=\mathbb E[\psi_\alpha\mid A_i],
\]
and the orthogonalized coordinate is
\[
\psi_\alpha^\perp(q)
=
\psi_\alpha(q)-\bar\psi_{\alpha,s(q)}.
\]
The Mori matrices are
\[
A_{\alpha\gamma}
=
\int
\nabla\psi_\alpha^\perp{}^\T
D
\nabla\psi_\gamma^\perp
\,\rho(\dd q),
\]
\[
h_{\alpha a}
=
\int
\nabla\psi_\alpha^\perp{}^\T
D
\nabla P_a
\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
With trajectory data, estimate \(A\) and \(h\) from the finite projected process
or from short-time Dirichlet forms of memory-coordinate increments. The
trajectory primitive output must include occupancies, reversible transition
rates, residence times, displacement moments, and Markov-additive readouts.

\subsubsection{Practical determination hierarchy}

Use direct physical specification for molecular graphs, topology, known force
fields, PBC/Ewald conventions, pure-liquid density, pure viscosity, pure
dielectric constant, and known coordination chemistry. Use quantum chemistry
for partial charges, polarizability, Born radii, Li-ligand binding energies,
Li-anion contact energies, desolvation energies, coordination energies, and
cluster conformer energetics. Use classical or machine-learned molecular
dynamics for RDF minima, PMFs, coordination distributions, SSIP/CIP
populations, residence times, diffusion tensors, Li-anion cross covariance,
anion orientation lifetimes, cage/backjump memory, partner switching, and
trajectory-derived \(K,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h\). Use experiments for density,
viscosity, dielectric constant, self diffusion, transference and Haven ratios,
association constants, spectroscopic coordination populations, and final
conductivity sanity checks.

Primitive-level calibration may fit only parameters that feed
\[
U,\quad D,\quad A_i,\quad K,\quad d,M,\quad D^{\rm self},\quad A,h.
\]
It must not fit a final conductivity scale, species bonus, species damping
term, salt-family correction, or row-bucket correction.

\subsubsection{Concrete builder workflow}

For a recipe such as EC:DMC 3:7, LiPF\(_6\) 1.0 M, FEC 5 wt\%, and
\(T=298.15\,{\rm K}\), the builder performs the following operations:
\begin{enumerate}[label={B\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
\item Convert salts into conserved ions, for example
\[
{\rm LiPF_6}\rightarrow{\rm Li^+}+{\rm PF_6^-},
\]
and set
\[
\mathcal C=\{{\rm Li},{\rm PF6},{\rm EC},{\rm DMC},{\rm FEC}\}.
\]
Convert all loadings to \(C_a\,[{\rm mol\,m^{-3}}]\) and \(\phi_s\).
\item Load species records: site topology, charge sites, LJ parameters, Born
radius, charge-cloud radius, polarizability, hydrodynamic parameters,
coordination sites, donor/acceptor data, molecular volume, pure viscosity,
pure dielectric constant, and density.
\item Build \(U(q)\) from Coulomb/Ewald, LJ, Born/desolvation, Li-solvent
coordination, Li-FEC coordination, Li-PF6 coordination, solvation competition,
packing/free-volume, cluster free energy, and activity/nonideality.
\item Build \(D(q)\) from local-viscosity Stokes resistance, hydrodynamic
coupling, PF6 shape friction, charge-cloud resistance, free-volume
obstruction, cage resistance, constraint resistance, and ion-atmosphere
resistance. Then set \(D(q)=k_BT R(q)^\#\).
\item Build
\[
P(q)=\sum_{\rm charge\ sites}z_aR_a^{\rm unwrap}(q).
\]
\item Generate sparse joint states: free Li, free PF6, LiPF6 SSIP, LiPF6 CIP,
Li-FEC, Li-FEC-PF6, additive-separated SSIP, Li\(_2\)PF6\(^+\),
Li(PF6)\(_2^-\), neutral Li\(_2\)(PF6)\(_2\), bridge/network states, and the
same states conditioned on PF6 orientation, local viscosity, and packing bins.
State labels use features rather than species-name branches.
\item Solve chemical potentials \(\lambda\) so that
\[
\sum_\alpha\nu_{\alpha,a}c_\alpha=C_a
\]
for every conserved component.
\item Build edges for free-SSIP, SSIP-CIP, SSIP-additive-separated SSIP,
free Li-Li-FEC, LiPF6-Li-FEC-PF6, pair-aggregate, partner switch, cage
capture/release, and orientation transition. Compute \(K_{ij}\) from
committor/capacity or a validated reversible kinetic approximation.
\item For each edge, set \(d=M=0\) for pure motif exchange. For structural
hops, identity diffusion, and partner switches, sample local conditioned paths
and compute
\[
\Delta P=P_{\rm end}-P_{\rm start},
\qquad
d=\mathbb E[\Delta P],
\qquad
M=\mathbb E[\Delta P\Delta P^\T].
\]
\item For every state,
\[
D_i^{\rm self}
=
c_i^{-1}
\int_{A_i}
G_P\Pi_iD\Pi_i^\T G_P^\T\rho(\dd q).
\]
For Li-anion states,
\[
D_Q=D_{\rm Li}+D_A-2D_{\rm Li,A}.
\]
Ligand-conditioned or bulky-anion anticorrelation appears here when present.
\item Compute memory matrices from atmosphere modes, charge-density modes,
cage/backjump, partner residence, ligand-shell residence, PF6 orientation, and
free-volume/stress:
\[
A_{\alpha\gamma}
=
\int\nabla\psi_\alpha^\T D\nabla\psi_\gamma\,\rho(\dd q),
\qquad
h_{\alpha a}
=
\int\nabla\psi_\alpha^\T D\nabla P_a\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
\item Read out
\[
B=
\sum_i c_iD_i^{\rm self}
+\frac12\sum_{ij}K_{ij}M_{ij},
\]
compute \(C^Q\) from the finite-state Poisson solve, compute
\[
C^\psi=h^\T A^\#h,
\]
and return
\[
\sigma_{\rm mS/cm}
=
10\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\operatorname{Tr}(B-C^Q-C^\psi).
\]
\end{enumerate}

{\rm Li\text{-}solvent\ coordination\ PMF},\quad

{\rm cluster\ free\ energies},\quad

\subsection{Deterministic basis generation}

Define the physical feature map
\[
\mathcal F(q)=
\left(
f_{\rm pair},
f_{\rm LiShell},
f_{\rm ligand},
f_{\rm anion},
f_{\rm orient},
f_{\rm cluster},
f_{\rm env},
f_{\rm atm}
\right).
\]
For Li center \(\ell\) and anion center \(a\),
\[
r_{\ell a}(q)=|r_\ell-r_a|,
\qquad
r_{\rm CIP}<r_{\rm SSIP}<r_{\rm free}.
\]
The pair feature is
\[
f_{\rm pair}=
\begin{cases}
{\rm CIP}, & r_{\ell a}<r_{\rm CIP},\\
{\rm SSIP}, & r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\ell a}<r_{\rm SSIP},\\
{\rm free}, & r_{\ell a}\ge r_{\rm free},\\
{\rm additive\ separated\ SSIP}, &
r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\ell a}<r_{\rm SSIP},\

\end{cases}

For ligand class \(L\),
\[
n_{\ell L}(q)=\sum_{b\in L}s_{\ell L}(r_{\ell b}),
\]
\[
n_{\ell{\rm solv}}=\sum_{L\in{\rm solvent}}n_{\ell L},
\quad
n_{\ell{\rm add}}=\sum_{L\in{\rm neutral\ additive}}n_{\ell L},
\quad
n_{\ell{\rm anion}}=\sum_{a\in{\rm anion}}s_{\ell a}(r_{\ell a}).
\]
The Li-shell feature is

f_{\rm LiShell}=
\begin{cases}

n_{\ell{\rm add}}<n_{\rm add}^{\rm cut},\

{\rm neutral\ ligand\ bound}, &
n_{\ell{\rm add}}\ge n_{\rm add}^{\rm cut},\\
{\rm anion\ coordinated}, &
n_{\ell{\rm anion}}\ge n_{\rm anion}^{\rm cut},\\
{\rm overcrowded}, &
n_{\ell{\rm solv}}+n_{\ell{\rm add}}+n_{\ell{\rm anion}}
>n_{\rm shell}^{\max}.
\end{cases}
\]
Neutral ligand state is
\[
f_{\rm ligand}=
\begin{cases}
{\rm none}, & n_{\ell L}<n_L^{\rm cut},\\
{\rm monodentate\ bound}, &
n_{\ell L}\ge n_L^{\rm cut},\ m_L^{\rm dent}=1,\\
{\rm multidentate\ bound}, &
n_{\ell L}\ge n_L^{\rm cut},\ m_L^{\rm dent}>1,\\
{\rm ligand\ bridge}, &
n_{\ell_1L}\ge n_L^{\rm cut},\
n_{\ell_2L}\ge n_L^{\rm cut}.
\end{cases}
\]
For anion \(a\),
\[
f_{\rm anion}(a)=
\left(
b_{\rm hyd}(a_a^{\rm hyd}),
b_{\rm cloud}(a_a^{\rm cloud}),
b_{\rm asym}(\ell_a^{\rm asym}),
b_{\rm dent}(m_a^{\rm dent})
\right),
\]
with representative bins
\[
b_{\rm hyd}(a)=
\begin{cases}
{\rm compact}, & a<a_{\rm hyd}^{(1)},\\
{\rm medium}, & a_{\rm hyd}^{(1)}\le a<a_{\rm hyd}^{(2)},\\
{\rm bulky}, & a\ge a_{\rm hyd}^{(2)},
\end{cases}
\]
\[
b_{\rm cloud}(a)=
\begin{cases}
{\rm localized}, & a<a_{\rm cloud}^{(1)},\\
{\rm delocalized}, & a\ge a_{\rm cloud}^{(1)},
\end{cases}
\qquad
b_{\rm dent}(m)=
\begin{cases}
{\rm monodentate}, & m=1,\\
{\rm multidentate}, & m>1.
\end{cases}
\]
Let \(u_a(q)\) be the anion principal axis and
\[
\hat r_{\ell a}=\frac{r_a-r_\ell}{|r_a-r_\ell|},
\qquad
\omega_a(q)=u_a(q)\cdot\hat r_{\ell a}.
\]
Then

f_{\rm orient}=
\begin{cases}
{\rm radial}, & \omega_a>\omega_+,\\
{\rm tangential}, & |\omega_a|\le\omega_0,\\
{\rm bridging}, & \omega_a<-\omega_-,\\
{\rm free\ rotating}, & \tau_{\rm orient}<\tau_{\rm orient}^{\rm cut}.
\end{cases}
\]
The cluster graph \(G(q)=(V,E(q))\) has charged-center vertices and
\[
(a,b)\in E(q)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r_{ab}<r_{\rm pair}^{\rm cut}.
\]
For cluster \(C\),
\[
N_{\rm Li}(C)=\sum_{v\in C}\mathbf 1_{\{v={\rm Li}\}},
\quad
N_A(C)=\sum_{v\in C}\mathbf 1_{\{v={\rm anion}\}},
\quad
z_C=\sum_{v\in C}z_v.
\]
The cluster feature is
\[
f_{\rm cluster}(C)=
\left(N_{\rm Li}(C),N_A(C),z_C,\tau_C,\operatorname{topology}(C)\right),
\]
with labels including
\[
{\rm Li^+},\ {\rm A^-},\ {\rm LiA},\ {\rm Li_2A^+},\
{\rm LiA_2^-},\ {\rm Li_2A_2},\
{\rm higher\ cluster},\ {\rm bridge\ network}.
\]
Local environment features are built from
\[
\varphi(q)=\sum_b v_bW(|r_b-r_0|),
\quad
I(q)=\frac12\sum_i z_i^2c_i^{\rm loc}(q),
\]
\[
\epsilon_{\rm loc}(q)=\sum_s\phi_s^{\rm loc}(q)\epsilon_s,
\quad
\eta_{\rm loc}(q)=
\eta_{\rm bulk}
f_{\rm salt}(I(q))
f_{\rm free\ volume}(\varphi(q))
f_{\rm additive}(q).
\]
Thus
\[
f_{\rm env}=
\left(
b_\varphi(\varphi),b_I(I),b_\epsilon(\epsilon_{\rm loc}),

\right).
\]
The joint state label is

\alpha(q)=

f_{\rm anion},
f_{\rm orient},
f_{\rm cluster},
f_{\rm env},

Z_\alpha=

and

\qquad\forall a.

\subsection{Physical state classification and DC projector}

For each basin \(A_\alpha\), define charged centers
\[
\mathcal C_\alpha=\{p=1,\ldots,m_\alpha\},
\quad
z_{\alpha p}\in\R,\quad r_{\alpha p}\in\R^3,\quad
a_{\alpha p}^{\rm hyd},\quad a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud},\quad
S_{\alpha p}^{\rm shape}.
\]
Let
\[
z_\alpha=(z_{\alpha1},\ldots,z_{\alpha m_\alpha})^\T.
\]
The projector \(\Pi_\alpha(q)\) selects unbounded DC charge-transport
directions. For a free ion,
\[
\Pi_\alpha=\Pi_{\rm trans}(p),
\]
where \(\Pi_{\rm trans}(p)\) selects the three unwrapped translational
coordinates of center \(p\). For a charged cluster,
\[
z_{\rm net,\alpha}=\sum_pz_{\alpha p}\ne0,
\qquad
R_{\rm COM,\alpha}
=
\frac{\sum_pm_{\alpha p}r_{\alpha p}}{\sum_pm_{\alpha p}},
\]
and
\[
\Pi_\alpha=\Pi_{\rm COM,\alpha}
\]
only when cluster translation is unbounded. If
\[
z_{\rm net,\alpha}=0
\]
and all charged centers co-move rigidly, then
\[
G_P\Pi_{\rm COM,\alpha}=0,
\]
so neutral COM motion gives no DC self-current. For bounded internal
polarization coordinates,
\[
\Pi_\alpha\xi_{\rm int}=0,
\]
and their contribution enters \(A,h\), not \(D_\alpha^{\rm self}\).

For charge-identity transfer in an associated state, define coordinate \(y_a\)
and projector \(\Pi_{\rm id,a}\). The identity diffusivity is
\[
D_{{\rm id},a}
=
\frac16
k_{{\rm switch},a}
p_{{\rm switch},a}
\mathbb E[\ell_{{\rm switch},a}^{2}].
\]
If any of the switching rate, switch probability, or unwrapped displacement is
unavailable, set \(D_{{\rm id},a}=0\).

The state self-current is
\[
D_\alpha^{\rm self}
=
\frac{1}{c_\alpha}
\int_{A_\alpha}
G_P(q)\Pi_\alpha(q)D_x(q)\Pi_\alpha(q)^\T
G_P(q)^\T\rho(\dd q).
\]
For center covariance
\[
D_\alpha^{\rm centers}\in\R^{m_\alpha\times m_\alpha},
\]
the charge mobility is
\[
D_Q(\alpha)=z_\alpha^\T D_\alpha^{\rm centers}z_\alpha.
\]
For Li-anion motion,
\[
D_Q=D_{\rm Li}+D_{\rm A}-2D_{\rm Li,A}.
\]
If \(D_{\rm Li,A}<0\), anti-correlated motion increases \(D_Q\) and therefore
can increase conductivity.

\subsection{Transition-family enumeration}

Every transition is an ordered edge
\[
e=(i,j,\kappa)
\]
from \(A_i\) to \(A_j\) with family \(\kappa\). Each family satisfies
\[
K_{ij}^{(\kappa)}=K_{ji}^{(\kappa)}\ge0,
\quad
Q_{ij}^{(\kappa)}=\frac{K_{ij}^{(\kappa)}}{c_i},
\quad
d_{ji}^{(\kappa)}=-d_{ij}^{(\kappa)},
\quad
M_{ji}^{(\kappa)}=M_{ij}^{(\kappa)}.
\]
The total capacity and generator entries are
\[
K_{ij}=\sum_\kappa K_{ij}^{(\kappa)},
\qquad
Q_{ij}=K_{ij}/c_i.
\]
The required transition families are:
\[
{\rm free}\leftrightarrow{\rm SSIP},\quad
{\rm SSIP}\leftrightarrow{\rm CIP},\quad
{\rm free/SSIP/CIP}\leftrightarrow{\rm aggregate},
\]
\[
{\rm Li}\leftrightarrow{\rm LiL},\quad
{\rm LiA}\leftrightarrow{\rm LiL\cdots A},\quad
{\rm SSIP}\leftrightarrow{\rm additive\ separated\ SSIP},
\]
\[
{\rm partner\ switch},\quad
{\rm identity\ diffusion},\quad
{\rm structural\ hop},\quad
{\rm cage\ capture/release},\quad
{\rm atmosphere\ capture/release}.
\]
The free-SSIP and SSIP-CIP coordinates are
\[
\xi_{\rm free,SSIP}=r_{\rm LiA},
\qquad
\xi_{\rm SSIP,CIP}=r_{\rm LiA}.
\]
The basins are
\[
A_{\rm free}=\{r_{\rm LiA}\ge r_{\rm SSIP}\},
\quad
A_{\rm SSIP}=\{r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP}\},
\quad
A_{\rm CIP}=\{r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm CIP}\}.
\]
If conversion only changes association label,
\[
d=0,\qquad M=0.
\]
If it includes physical charge displacement, then
\[
d=\mathbb E[\Delta P],
\qquad
M=\mathbb E[\Delta P\Delta P^\T].
\]
Aggregate coordinates are
\[
\xi_{\rm agg}=
\left(
r_{\rm LiA_1},\ldots,r_{\rm LiA_k},
N_{\rm cluster},z_{\rm cluster}
\right),
\]
with edges such as
\[
{\rm free}\leftrightarrow{\rm Li_2A^+},
\quad
{\rm free}\leftrightarrow{\rm LiA_2^-},
\quad
{\rm SSIP}\leftrightarrow{\rm Li_2A_2},
\quad
{\rm CIP}\leftrightarrow{\rm bridge}.
\]
Li-ligand binding uses
\[
\xi_{\rm Li,L}=n_{\rm LiL},
\quad
A_{\rm Li}=\{n_{\rm LiL}<n_L^{\rm cut}\},
\quad
A_{\rm LiL}=\{n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut}\}.
\]
The ternary Li-ligand-anion coordinate is
\[
\xi_{\rm ternary}=(r_{\rm LiA},n_{\rm LiL}),
\]
with
\[
A_{\rm LiA}
=
\{r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP},\,n_{\rm LiL}<n_L^{\rm cut}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm LiL\cdots A}
=
\{r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP},\,
n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut}\}.
\]
The additive-separated coordinate is
\[
\xi_{\rm addSSIP}=(r_{\rm LiA},n_{\rm LiL},\chi_L),
\]
where \(\chi_L\) indicates ligand insertion between Li and anion. Partner
switching from old partner \(a\) to new partner \(b\) uses
\[
\xi_{\rm switch}=r_{\rm Li,a}-r_{\rm Li,b},
\qquad
\Delta P=P(q_{\rm after})-P(q_{\rm before}).
\]
This transition has nonzero \(d\) or \(M\) only if unwrapped charge identity
changes. Identity diffusion uses
\[
y\to y+\ell,
\qquad
d=\mathbb E[\ell],
\qquad
M=\mathbb E[\ell\ell^\T].
\]
Cage capture and atmosphere relaxation can have \(d=M=0\); their transport
impact then enters through finite-state memory or \(A,h\).

\subsection{Committor numerical policy}

For edge \(i\leftrightarrow j\),
\[
\Omega_{ij}=\Omega\setminus(A_i\cup A_j),
\]
and the committor solves
\[
Lq_{ij}=0\quad{\rm in}\ \Omega_{ij},
\qquad
q_{ij}=0\ {\rm on}\ \partial A_i,
\qquad
q_{ij}=1\ {\rm on}\ \partial A_j.
\]
On refinement level \(r\), mesh \(\Omega_{ij}\) by \(\mathcal T_{ij}^{(r)}\)
with continuous piecewise-linear basis \(\{\varphi_m\}_{m=1}^{M_r}\):
\[
q_{ij}^{(r)}(q)=q_D(q)+\sum_{m=1}^{M_r}u_m\varphi_m(q).
\]
The stiffness matrix and right-hand side are
\[
S_{mn}^{(r)}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D(q)\nabla\varphi_n\,\rho(\dd q),
\]
\[
f_m^{(r)}
=
-
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D(q)\nabla q_D\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
Solve
\[
S^{(r)}u^{(r)}=f^{(r)}.
\]
Use a symmetric positive solver when \(S^{(r)}\) is positive definite and a
symmetric indefinite solver when constraints create semidefinite blocks.
Capacity is
\[
K_{ij}^{(r)}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)\T}D(q)\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)}\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
Element residuals are
\[
\eta_E=
\int_E\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)\T}D\nabla q_{ij}^{(r)}\,\rho(\dd q).
\]
Refine the smallest element set \(\mathcal R\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{E\in\mathcal R}\eta_E
\ge
\theta_{\rm refine}\sum_E\eta_E,
\qquad
0.3\le\theta_{\rm refine}\le0.7.
\]
Stop refinement when
\[
\frac{|K_{ij}^{(r+1)}-K_{ij}^{(r)}|}
{\max(K_{ij}^{(r)},K_{\min})}
<\tau_K,
\]
with
\[
\tau_K=10^{-3},
\qquad
K_{\min}=10^{-30}\ {\rm mol\,m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}.
\]
If \(\Omega_{ij}\) has disconnected components, solve each component; include
only components touching both boundaries:
\[
K_{ij}=\sum_mK_{ij}^{(m)}.
\]
If
\[
c_i<c_{\min}
\quad\text{or}\quad
c_j<c_{\min},
\qquad
c_{\min}=10^{-18}\ {\rm mol\,m^{-3}},
\]
remove the edge from the active generator or merge the basin into an active
neighbor.

\subsection{Doob-conditioned moment boundary-value solver}

Use the converged committor \(q_{ij}\). The overdamped drift is
\[
b(q)=\nabla\cdot D(q)-\frac{1}{RT}D(q)\nabla U(q).
\]
The conditioned generator is
\[
L^h f=Lf+2D(q)\nabla\log q_{ij}(q)\cdot\nabla f,
\]
with regularization
\[
\nabla\log q_{ij}
=
\frac{\nabla q_{ij}}{\max(q_{ij},q_{\min})},
\qquad
q_{\min}=10^{-8}.
\]
The exit density is
\[
p_{ij}^{\rm exit}(q)
=
\frac{\rho(q)\,n_{ij}(q)^\T D(q)\nabla q_{ij}(q)}
{K_{ij}}.
\]
Surface quadrature samples \(q_\ell^\Sigma\) with probability
\[
\Pr(q=q_\ell^\Sigma)
=
\frac{
w_\ell^\Sigma\rho(q_\ell^\Sigma)
n^\T D(q_\ell^\Sigma)\nabla q_{ij}(q_\ell^\Sigma)
}{
\sum_m w_m^\Sigma\rho(q_m^\Sigma)
n^\T D(q_m^\Sigma)\nabla q_{ij}(q_m^\Sigma)
}.
\]
Use the same finite-element basis as the committor on
\(\Omega_{ij}\). For each Cartesian component, solve
\[
S^h u_a=f_a^h,
\qquad
u_a=P_a\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
For each Cartesian pair, solve
\[
S^h w_{ab}=g_{ab}^h,
\qquad
w_{ab}=P_aP_b\quad\text{on }\partial A_j.
\]
The conditioned stiffness matrix is
\[
S^h_{mn}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D\nabla\varphi_n\,h^2\rho\,\dd q.
\]
The right-hand sides contain the lifted boundary values in the same way as the
committor Dirichlet solve. Stop when
\[
\frac{\|S^h u_a-f_a^h\|_2}{s(f_a^h)}\le\tau_{\rm moment},
\qquad
\frac{\|S^h w_{ab}-g_{ab}^h\|_2}{s(g_{ab}^h)}\le\tau_{\rm moment}.
\]
Surface quadrature gives
\[
d_{ij,a}=
\sum_\ell w_\ell^\Sigma\eta_i(q_\ell^\Sigma)
[u_a(q_\ell^\Sigma)-P_a(q_\ell^\Sigma)],
\]
\[
M_{ij,ab}=
\sum_\ell w_\ell^\Sigma\eta_i(q_\ell^\Sigma)
[w_{ab}-P_au_b-P_bu_a+P_aP_b]_{q=q_\ell^\Sigma}.
\]
Check
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij},
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(M_{ij})\ge-\tau_{\rm psd}.
\]

\subsection{State-local resistance and charged-center covariance}

The state resistance is

R_\alpha=

+R_\alpha^{\rm hydro}
+R_\alpha^{\rm shape}
+R_\alpha^{\rm free\ volume}
+R_\alpha^{\rm charge\ cloud}
+R_\alpha^{\rm atmosphere}
+R_\alpha^{\rm constraint}
+R_\alpha^{\rm cage}.

For center \(p\),
\[
\zeta_{\alpha p}=6\pi\eta_\alpha a_{\alpha p}^{\rm hyd},
\qquad
R_{\alpha,pp}^{\rm Stokes}=\zeta_{\alpha p}I_3.
\]
If \(S_{\alpha p}\) is a shape tensor,
\[
R_{\alpha p}^{\rm shape}
=
\zeta_{\alpha p}\left[I_3+\lambda_{\rm shape}(S_{\alpha p}-I_3)\right].
\]
For scalar asymmetry,
\[
R_{\alpha p}^{\rm shape}
=
\zeta_{\alpha p}\chi_{\alpha p}^{\rm shape}I_3,
\qquad
\chi_{\alpha p}^{\rm shape}
=
(1+\ell_{\alpha p}^{\rm asym})^{\gamma_{\rm shape}}.
\]
The free-volume factor is
\[
\varphi_\alpha=\mathbb E[\varphi(q)\mid A_\alpha],
\qquad
\chi_\alpha^{\rm fv}
=
\left(1-\frac{\varphi_\alpha}{\varphi_{\max}}\right)^{-\gamma_{\rm fv}},
\]
\[
R_\alpha^{\rm free\ volume}
=
(\chi_\alpha^{\rm fv}-1)R_\alpha^{\rm Stokes}.
\]
Charge-density resistance uses
\[
\rho_{\alpha p}^{\rm charge}
=
\frac{|z_{\alpha p}|}{(a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud})^3},
\]
\[
R_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud}
=
\lambda_{\rm cloud}
(\rho_{\alpha p}^{\rm charge})^{\gamma_{\rm cloud}}
F_{\rm screen}(\kappa a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud})I_3,
\qquad
F_{\rm screen}(x)=\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}.
\]
Constraint mode \(m\) contributes

R_{\alpha}^{\rm constraint,m}
=
k_BT
\frac{\tau_{\alpha m}}{\ell_{\alpha m}^2}
s_{\alpha m}s_{\alpha m}^\T,
\qquad
R_\alpha^{\rm constraint}=\sum_mR_\alpha^{\rm constraint,m}.
\]
Cage resistance is

=

\frac{\tau_{{\rm cage},\alpha}}{\ell_{{\rm cage},\alpha}^2}
s_{\rm cage}s_{\rm cage}^\T.
\]
The mobility is

\]
If \(Z_\alpha\) maps center velocities to charge displacement,

\qquad

The projected state-local self-current is

\subsection{Ion-atmosphere operator}

The ionic strength and inverse Debye length are
\[
I=\frac12\sum_s z_s^2c_s,
\qquad
\kappa^2=
\frac{F^2}{\epsilon_0\epsilon_rRT}\sum_s z_s^2c_s,
\qquad
\lambda_D=\kappa^{-1}.
\]
For wavevector \(k\),
\[
\rho_z(k;q)=\sum_p z_pe^{\mathrm i k\cdot r_p},
\qquad
g_k(q)=\nabla_q\rho_z(k;q).
\]
The bulk response is
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)
=
\mathcal R_{\rm ep}(k)+\mathcal R_{\rm rel}(k),
\]
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm ep}(k)
=
\Gamma_{\rm ep}
\frac{k^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}\eta,
\]
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm rel}(k)
=
\Gamma_{\rm rel}
\frac{\kappa^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}
\frac{1}{D_{\rm amb}k^2+\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}},
\]
\[
D_{\rm amb}
=
\frac{\sum_s z_s^2c_sD_s}{\sum_s z_s^2c_s}.
\]
For state \(\alpha\),
\[
F_\alpha(k)
=
\sum_{p\in\alpha}
z_{\alpha p}
e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho_{\alpha p}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac12k^2(a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud})^2\right].
\]
The state atmosphere resistance is
\[
R_\alpha^{\rm atmosphere}
=
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
|F_\alpha(k)|^2
\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)
G_{\alpha k}G_{\alpha k}^\T.
\]
For a co-located neutral pair,
\[
z_1=+1,\quad z_2=-1,\quad \rho_1=\rho_2,
\]
\[
F_\alpha(k)=e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho_1}-e^{\mathrm i k\cdot\rho_1}=0,
\qquad
R_\alpha^{\rm atmosphere}=0
\]
for neutral translation. For separated Li-anion pair distance \(r\),
\[
F_\alpha(k)=e^{\mathrm i k\cdot r_{\rm Li}}
-e^{\mathrm i k\cdot r_{\rm A}}.
\]
At small \(kr\),
\[
|F_\alpha(k)|^2\approx(k\cdot r)^2,
\]
and at large \(kr\),
\[
\langle|F_\alpha(k)|^2\rangle\to2.
\]
Thus SSIP/CIP atmosphere resistance interpolates between neutral cancellation
and independent-ion resistance.

The atmosphere relaxation time is
\[
\tau_{\rm atm}=\frac{1}{D_{\rm amb}\kappa^2}.
\]

\subsubsection{Trajectory identification of atmosphere response primitives}

The atmosphere coefficients are primitive response properties. They are not
universal constants and they are not fitted to conductivity. A periodic molecular
dynamics trajectory identifies them from species-resolved diffusion, charge-density
relaxation, ionic current, and neutral-solvent momentum.

For every charged species \(s\), the long-time molecular-center covariance gives

\[
D_s
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}
\frac{1}{6t}
\left\langle
\left|R_s(t)-R_s(0)\right|^2
\right\rangle,
\]

and the ambipolar diffusivity is

\[
D_{\rm amb}
=
\frac{\sum_s z_s^2c_sD_s}{\sum_s z_s^2c_s}.
\]

For each reciprocal-cell wavevector \(k\in\mathcal K\), define

\[
\rho_z(k,t)=\sum_p z_p\exp\!\left[\mathrm i k\cdot r_p(t)\right],
\]

\[
C_{zz}(k,t)
=
\frac{
\left\langle\rho_z(k,t)\rho_z(k,0)^*\right\rangle
}{
\left\langle|\rho_z(k,0)|^2\right\rangle
}.
\]

Only a time interval with stable block estimates and resolved exponential decay is
admissible. On that interval,

\[
C_{zz}(k,t)\simeq\exp[-\lambda(k)t],
\qquad
\lambda(k)=D_{\rm amb}k^2+\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}.
\]

The slope of the multi-\(k\) regression must agree with the independently estimated
\(D_{\rm amb}\). The intercept determines \(\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}\). A single
wavevector magnitude cannot identify both quantities and is diagnostic only.

Let \(J_{z,L}(k,t)\) and \(J_{z,T}(k,t)\) be the longitudinal and transverse
species-resolved ionic charge currents, and let \(P_{{\rm solv},T}(k,t)\) be the
transverse neutral-solvent momentum. The relaxation response is identified from the
charge-density/current correlation

\[
C_{\rho J}(k,t)
=
\left\langle
\rho_z(k,0)^*J_{z,L}(k,t)
\right\rangle,
\]

and the electrophoretic response is identified from

\[
C_{JP}(k,t)
=
\left\langle
J_{z,T}(k,t)P_{{\rm solv},T}(k,0)^*
\right\rangle.
\]

Projection of the ionic current onto these resolved modes followed by the converged
zero-frequency memory integral gives the trajectory primitives

\[
R_{\rm rel}^{\rm MD}(k),
\qquad
R_{\rm ep}^{\rm MD}(k).
\]

The preferred production representation stores these wavevector-resolved kernels
directly. If a scalar kernel parameterization is retained, its coefficients are
identified by zero-intercept weighted regression,

\[
\Gamma_{\rm ep}
=
\underset{\Gamma}{\arg\min}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}w_k
\left[
R_{\rm ep}^{\rm MD}(k)
-\Gamma
\frac{\eta k^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}
\right]^2,
\]

\[
\Gamma_{\rm rel}
=
\underset{\Gamma}{\arg\min}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}w_k
\left[
R_{\rm rel}^{\rm MD}(k)
-\Gamma
\frac{\kappa^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}
\frac{1}{D_{\rm amb}k^2+\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}}
\right]^2.
\]

The weights \(w_k\) are inverse block-bootstrap variances of the trajectory response
estimates. The scalar representation is rejected when the inferred coefficient is not
statistically constant over the converged wavevector interval. Every accepted response
record stores its trajectory identifier, composition, temperature, box, wavevectors,
fit interval, block uncertainty, and the provenance label
\texttt{trajectory\_derived\_primitive}. Conductivity values and conductivity
validation ranges are forbidden inputs to this identification.

For state lifetime \(\tau_\alpha\), define the diagnostic gate
\[
g_\alpha^{\rm diag}
=
\frac{\tau_\alpha}{\tau_\alpha+\tau_{\rm atm}}.
\]
If this gate is included in relaxation resistance,
\[
R_\alpha^{{\rm rel},\rm eff}=g_\alpha^{\rm diag}R_\alpha^{\rm rel}.
\]
It is never applied as a terminal multiplier on conductivity.

\subsection{High-concentration closure}

Finite occupied volume is

Packing free energy is

\frac{\varphi(r)(4-3\varphi(r))}

If \(\varphi(r)\ge\varphi_{\max}\) at any quadrature point, the state has

{1+B_\gamma a_i\sqrt I}.

Hard-sphere activity is

=

\left[
\frac{\varphi(4-3\varphi)}{(1-\varphi)^2}
\right].
\]
The activity free energy is

=

\left(

\right).

This enters

\left[

\right].

\eta_{\rm loc}(q)

Cluster mass action is

where \(\lambda^\star\) solves shared component mass balance.

\subsubsection{Mobility and polarization closures}

The mobility closure \(\mathcal D\) constructs resistance first:
\[
R=
R_{\rm Stokes}+R_{\rm hydro}+R_{\rm shape}+R_{\rm free\ volume}
+R_{\rm charge\ cloud}+R_{\rm atmosphere}+R_{\rm cage}
+R_{\rm constraint},
\qquad
D=k_BT\,R^\# .
\]
It supplies local viscosity, Jones--Dole salt correction, additive
microviscosity, free-volume viscosity, hydrodynamic radii per site or transport
center, hydrodynamic coupling policy, anion and cluster shape tensors,
denticity/orientation factors, \(\varphi_{\max}\), \(\gamma_{\rm fv}\),
charge-cloud radii and screening form factors, cage lengths and lifetimes,
pair/ligand/aggregate residence lifetimes, and the ion-atmosphere matrix
response. Atmosphere resistance is projected through state charge geometry:
free ions receive monopolar response, while neutral and solvent-separated
pairs receive geometry-dependent cancellation.

The polarization closure \(\mathcal P\) supplies charge centers, charge numbers,
state geometry, unwrapped displacement convention, and the reduced-coordinate
map to charge-center displacement:
\[
P(y)=\sum_p z_pR_p^{\rm unwrap}(y),
\qquad
G_P(y)=\nabla_yP(y).
\]
For a multi-center state,
\[
D_Q(\alpha)=z_\alpha^\T D_\alpha^{\rm centers}z_\alpha.
\]
For a Li--anion state,
\[
D_Q=D_{\rm Li}+D_A-2D_{\rm Li,A}.
\]
A negative conditional covariance \(D_{\rm Li,A|\alpha}\) increases the charge
mobility primitive for that state.

\subsubsection{Basis and speciation closures}

The basis closure \(\mathcal B\) prescribes thresholds and bins for pair
basins, Li-shell states, ligand states, anion size/cloud/asymmetry/denticity,
anion orientation, cluster charge and size, bridge/network states, and local
packing, dielectric, viscosity, and ionic-strength environments. The sparse
joint state is
\[
A_\alpha=
A_{\rm pair}\cap A_{\rm shell}\cap A_{\rm ligand}\cap A_{\rm anion}
\cap A_{\rm orient}\cap A_{\rm cluster}\cap A_{\rm env}.
\]
Only states satisfying \(Z_\alpha>Z_{\min}\) and feasible stoichiometry are
retained.

The speciation closure \(\mathcal S\) assigns stoichiometry to free Li, free
anion, SSIP and CIP LiA, Li--ligand, Li--ligand--anion, additive-separated
SSIP, \(\mathrm{Li_2A^+}\), \(\mathrm{LiA_2^-}\),
\(\mathrm{Li_2A_2}\), higher aggregates, and bridge/network states. It solves
one shared component balance:
\[
\sum_\alpha\nu_{\alpha,a}c_\alpha=C_a,
\]
with Newton residual and Jacobian
\[
r_a(\lambda)=\sum_\alpha\nu_{\alpha,a}c_\alpha(\lambda)-C_a,
\qquad
J_{ab}=\sum_\alpha\nu_{\alpha,a}\nu_{\alpha,b}c_\alpha .
\]
State concentrations are molar concentrations. Stationary probabilities are
derived from those concentrations.

\subsubsection{Transition, rate, and moment closures}

The transition closure \(\mathcal R\) supplies edge list, transition family,
reaction coordinate \(\xi_{ij}(y)\), transition domain \(\Omega_{ij}\),
boundary surfaces \(\partial A_i,\partial A_j\), barrier or free-energy
formula, committor/capacity solver data, and displacement policy. Required edge
families are free \(\leftrightarrow\) SSIP, SSIP \(\leftrightarrow\) CIP,
free/SSIP/CIP \(\leftrightarrow\) aggregate, Li \(\leftrightarrow\) Li--ligand,
LiA \(\leftrightarrow\) Li--ligand--anion, SSIP \(\leftrightarrow\)
additive-separated SSIP, partner switch, identity diffusion, structural hop,
bridge/network formation and breakup, cage capture and release, and discrete
atmosphere capture and release when represented.

The committor or rate closure \(\mathcal K\) uses the strongest available
closed form. For a one-dimensional coordinate,
\[
k_{i\to j}^{-1}
=
\int_{\xi_i}^{\xi_j}
\frac{e^{F(\xi)/(RT)}}{D_\xi(\xi)}
\left[
\int_{\xi_L}^{\xi}e^{-F(\eta)/(RT)}\dd\eta
\right]\dd\xi,
\qquad
K_{ij}=c_i k_{i\to j}=c_j k_{j\to i}.
\]
For a reduced PDE, it solves \(Lq_{ij}=0\) with \(q_{ij}=0\) on \(A_i\) and
\(q_{ij}=1\) on \(A_j\), then computes
\[
K_{ij}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}\nabla q_{ij}^\T D\nabla q_{ij}\rho\,\dd y.
\]
For Kramers/capacity asymptotics,
\[
k_{i\to j}
=
\frac{\omega_i\omega^\ddagger}{2\pi\zeta^\ddagger}
\exp[-(F^\ddagger-F_i)/(RT)],
\]
and detailed balance is enforced by constructing \(K_{ij}\), not by fitting
independent forward and reverse rates.

The moment closure \(\mathcal M\) computes \(d_{ij}\) and \(M_{ij}\) from the
zero-displacement rule, endpoint geometry, identity diffusion statistics, or
the deterministic moment BVP:
\[
L^h u_a=0,\quad u_a|_{A_j}=P_a,
\qquad
L^h w_{ab}=0,\quad w_{ab}|_{A_j}=P_aP_b,
\]
\[
L^hf=Lf+2D\nabla\log h\cdot\nabla f,\qquad h=q_{ij}.
\]
Surface moments are
\[
d_{ij,a}=\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(y)[u_a(y)-P_a(y)]\dd S,
\]
\[
M_{ij,ab}
=
\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(y)
[w_{ab}-P_au_b-P_bu_a+P_aP_b]\dd S.
\]
Pure motif exchange has \(d_{ij}=0\) and \(M_{ij}=0\). Nonzero displacement
requires physical unwrapped charge displacement
\(\Delta P=P(y_{\rm end})-P(y_{\rm start})\), with
\(d_{ji}=-d_{ij}\) and \(M_{ji}=M_{ij}\).

\subsubsection{Self-current, memory, and numerical closures}

The self-current closure \(\mathcal I\) defines \(\Pi_i(y)\): free ions use an
unbounded translation projector, charged clusters use the charged COM or
internal unbounded coordinate projector, neutral co-moving clusters satisfy
\(G_P\Pi_i=0\), bounded internal polarization uses \(\Pi_i=0\), and identity
diffusion uses a projector only when identity coordinate and switching
statistics are defined. It then computes
\[
D_i^{\rm self}
=
\frac1{c_i}
\int_{A_i}G_P\Pi_iD\Pi_i^\T G_P^\T\rho\,\dd y.
\]

The memory closure \(\Psi\) defines charge-density/ion-atmosphere modes,
solvent-cage/backjump coordinate, partner residence, ligand-shell residence,
anion orientation/denticity, aggregate residence, local free-volume/stress, and
bounded internal polarization coordinates. It state-orthogonalizes
\[
\psi_\alpha^\perp
=
\psi_\alpha-\mathbb E[\psi_\alpha\mid A_{s(y)}],
\]
and computes \(A_{\alpha\gamma}\), \(h_{\alpha a}\), and
\(C^\psi=h^\T A^\#h\).

The numerical closure \(\mathcal N\) fixes basin quadrature, transition-surface
quadrature, memory quadrature, Newton tolerance, committor/PDE or 1D quadrature
tolerance, \(K_{ij}\) convergence tolerance, moment-BVP tolerance, PSD
tolerance, pseudoinverse tolerance, nullspace tolerance, and finite-basis
refinement. Candidate memory or coordinate \(g\) is scored by
\[
\tilde h_g=h_g-A_{g\Phi}A_{\Phi\Phi}^\#h_\Phi,
\qquad
\tilde A_{gg}=A_{gg}-A_{g\Phi}A_{\Phi\Phi}^\#A_{\Phi g},
\]
\[
S(g)=\frac{|\tilde h_g|^2}{\tilde A_{gg}}.
\]
Add the candidate with largest \(S(g)\), then stop when the maximum score and
\(|\sigma_{n+1}-\sigma_n|\) are below tolerance.

\subsubsection{Deterministic function body}

The deterministic body is:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
1.&\text{Parse recipe into }C_a\text{ and }\phi_s.\\
2.&\text{Build species and interaction feature records.}\\
3.&\text{Build }y\text{ and coordinate domains.}\\
4.&\text{Build }U(y).\\
5.&\text{Build }R(y)\text{ and }D(y)=k_BT\,R(y)^\#.\\
6.&\text{Build }P(y)\text{ and }G_P(y).\\
7.&\text{Build sparse }A_i\text{ and }\nu_{i,a}.\\
8.&\text{Solve mass balance for }c_i.\\
9.&\text{Build transition edges.}\\
10.&\text{Compute }K_{ij}\text{ by 1D integral, PDE, or capacity formula.}\\
11.&\text{Compute }Q_{ij}=K_{ij}/c_i.\\
12.&\text{Compute }d_{ij},M_{ij}.\\
13.&\text{Compute }D_i^{\rm self}.\\
14.&\text{Compute memory }A,h.\\
15.&\text{Solve finite-state Poisson correction }C^Q.\\
16.&\text{Compute }\sigma\text{ from the fixed readout.}
\end{array}
\]
An input that does not feed
\[
c_i,\quad K_{ij}/Q_{ij},\quad d_{ij}/M_{ij},\quad
D_i^{\rm self},\quad A,h
\]
is outside the conductivity function.

\subsection{Reduced free energy}

The scalar free energy used by every integral is
\[
U=
U_{\rm pair}+U_{\rm shell}+U_{\rm ligand}+U_{\rm cluster}
+U_{\rm Born}+U_{\rm Coul}+U_{\rm pack}+U_{\rm activity}
+U_{\rm atmosphere}.
\]
For \(r=r_{\rm LiA}\),
\[
U_{\rm pair}(r)
=
-RT\log\!\left[
e^{-F_{\rm free}/RT}
+e^{-F_{\rm SSIP}/RT}
+e^{-F_{\rm CIP}/RT}
\right],
\]
\[
F_{\rm free}=0,
\quad
F_{\rm SSIP}=\Delta G_{\rm SSIP}
+\frac12k_{\rm SSIP}(r-r_{\rm SSIP}^0)^2,
\]
\[
F_{\rm CIP}=\Delta G_{\rm CIP}
+\frac12k_{\rm CIP}(r-r_{\rm CIP}^0)^2.
\]
For \(n=(n_S,n_L,n_A)^\T\),
\[
U_{\rm shell}=\frac12(n-\bar n)^\T K_{\rm shell}(n-\bar n).
\]
Ligand insertion is
\[
U_{\rm ligand}
=
\Delta G_{\rm LiL}n_L+\Delta G_{\rm LiLA}\chi_L
+\frac12k_{\rm comp}(n_L+n_A-n_{\rm shell}^\star)^2.
\]
Cluster states use
\[
G_C=
G_C^{\rm Coul}+G_C^{\rm desolv}+G_C^{\rm coord}
+G_C^{\rm steric}+G_C^{\rm entropy}+G_C^{\rm activity},
\qquad
U_{\rm cluster}=\sum_CG_C\one_C(y).
\]
Packing and activity are
\[
U_{\rm pack}(\varphi)
=
RT\frac{\varphi(4-3\varphi)}{(1-\varphi)^2},
\]
\[
U_{\rm activity}
=
RT\sum_i n_i(\ln\gamma_i^{\rm DH}+\ln\gamma_i^{\rm HS}),
\qquad
\ln\gamma_i^{\rm DH}
=
-\frac{A_\gamma z_i^2\sqrt I}{1+B_\gamma a_i\sqrt I}.
\]

\subsection{Reduced mobility}

Build
\[
R=
R_{\rm Stokes}+R_{\rm shape}+R_{\rm free\ volume}
+R_{\rm charge\ cloud}+R_{\rm atmosphere}
+R_{\rm cage}+R_{\rm constraint},
\qquad
D=k_BT\,R^\#.
\]
For center \(p\),
\[
\zeta_p=6\pi\eta_{\rm loc}a_p^{\rm hyd},
\qquad
R_{{\rm Stokes},pp}=\zeta_p I_3.
\]
Shape, free-volume, and charge-cloud resistance are
\[
R_p^{\rm shape}
=
\zeta_p(1+\ell_p^{\rm asym})^{\gamma_{\rm shape}}I_3,
\]
\[
R^{\rm fv}
=
\left[
\left(1-\frac{\varphi}{\varphi_{\max}}\right)^{-\gamma_{\rm fv}}
-1
\right]R^{\rm Stokes},
\]
\[
R_p^{\rm cloud}
=
\lambda_{\rm cloud}
\left(\frac{|z_p|}{(a_p^{\rm cloud})^3}\right)^{\gamma_{\rm cloud}}
\frac{(\kappa a_p^{\rm cloud})^2}
{1+(\kappa a_p^{\rm cloud})^2}I_3.
\]
Cage or binding resistance is
\[
R_m^{\rm cage}
=
k_BT\frac{\tau_m}{\ell_m^2}s_ms_m^\T.
\]
For atmosphere response,
\[
F_\alpha(k)
=
\sum_{p\in\alpha}
z_{\alpha p}e^{ik\cdot\rho_{\alpha p}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac12k^2(a_{\alpha p}^{\rm cloud})^2\right],
\]
\[
\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)
=
\Gamma_{\rm ep}\frac{k^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}\eta
+
\Gamma_{\rm rel}\frac{\kappa^2}{(k^2+\kappa^2)^2}
\frac{1}{D_{\rm amb}k^2+\tau_{\rm chem}^{-1}},
\]
\[
R_\alpha^{\rm atm}
=
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
w_k|F_\alpha(k)|^2\mathcal R_{\rm bulk}(k)
G_{\alpha k}G_{\alpha k}^\T.
\]
A co-located neutral pair has \(F_\alpha(k)=0\) for its neutral translation
mode.

\subsection{Polarization, basins, and populations}

For centers \(R_p(y)\in\R^3\),
\[
P(y)=\sum_p z_pR_p^{\rm unwrap}(y),
\qquad
G_P=\nabla_yP.
\]
The one-axis charge mobility in state \(\alpha\) is
\[
D_Q(\alpha)=z_\alpha^\T D_\alpha^{\rm centers}z_\alpha.
\]
For a Li--anion state,
\[
D_Q=D_{\rm Li}+D_A-2D_{\rm Li,A}.
\]
Negative conditional covariance \(D_{\rm Li,A}<0\) increases the charge
displacement term.

Basins are subsets of reduced coordinate space:
\[
A_{\rm free}=\{y:r_{\rm LiA}\ge r_{\rm free}\},
\quad
A_{\rm SSIP}=\{y:r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm CIP}=\{y:r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm CIP}\},
\quad
A_{\rm LiL}=\{y:n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut}\},
\]
\[
A_{\rm LiL\cdots A}
=
\{y:n_{\rm LiL}\ge n_L^{\rm cut},\
r_{\rm CIP}\le r_{\rm LiA}<r_{\rm SSIP}\},
\qquad
A_{\rm addSSIP}=A_{\rm SSIP}\cap\{y:\chi_L=1\}.
\]
The sparse joint state is
\[
A_\alpha=
A_{\rm pair}\cap A_{\rm ligand}\cap A_{\rm cluster}
\cap A_{\rm orientation}\cap A_{\rm local\ env}\cap A_{\rm atmosphere}.
\]
Retain \(A_\alpha\) when its partition weight exceeds \(Z_{\min}\).

For stoichiometry \(\nu_{i,a}\),
\[
Z_i(\lambda)
=
\int_{A_i}
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{U(y)}{RT}+\sum_a\nu_{i,a}\lambda_a
\right]\dd y,
\qquad
c_i=C^\circ Z_i,\quad C^\circ=1000\ \molm.
\]
Mass residuals and Jacobian are
\[
r_a=\sum_i\nu_{i,a}c_i-C_a,
\qquad
J_{ab}=\sum_i\nu_{i,a}\nu_{i,b}c_i.
\]
Newton steps solve \(J\Delta\lambda=-r\). Backtracking accepts a step that
reduces
\[
\max_a\frac{|r_a(\lambda+\alpha\Delta\lambda)|}
{\max(C_a,C_{\min})}.
\]

\subsection{Capacity fluxes and transition moments}

For edge \(i\leftrightarrow j\),
\[
\Omega_{ij}=\Omega\setminus(A_i\cup A_j).
\]
Solve
\[
Lq_{ij}=0,\qquad
q_{ij}=0\text{ on }\partial A_i,\qquad
q_{ij}=1\text{ on }\partial A_j.
\]
With \(q_{ij}=q_D+\sum_mu_m\varphi_m\),
\[
S_{mn}=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D\nabla\varphi_n\rho\,\dd y,
\qquad
f_m=
-\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D\nabla q_D\rho\,\dd y.
\]
Solve \(Su=f\), then compute
\[
K_{ij}=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla q_{ij}^\T D\nabla q_{ij}\rho\,\dd y,
\qquad
Q_{ij}=K_{ij}/c_i,
\qquad
Q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}Q_{ij}.
\]

Let \(h=q_{ij}\). The conditioned generator is
\[
L^h f=Lf+2D\nabla\log h\cdot\nabla f.
\]
The canonical production calculation for \(d_{ij}\) and \(M_{ij}\) is the
boundary-value hierarchy below. Doob-conditioned sample paths estimate the same
boundary-value objects and are used only as a numerical estimator when the
deterministic solve is replaced by stochastic quadrature.
Endpoint functions solve
\[
L^h u_a=0,\quad u_a=P_a\text{ on }\partial A_j,
\qquad
L^h w_{ab}=0,\quad w_{ab}=P_aP_b\text{ on }\partial A_j.
\]
The exit density on \(\partial A_i\) is
\[
\eta_i(y)=
\frac{\rho(y)n_i(y)^\T D(y)\nabla h(y)}{K_{ij}}.
\]
Then
\[
d_{ij,a}
=
\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(y)[u_a(y)-P_a(y)]\,\dd S(y),
\]
\[
M_{ij,ab}
=
\int_{\partial A_i}\eta_i(y)
[w_{ab}-P_au_b-P_bu_a+P_aP_b](y)\,\dd S(y).
\]
Pure motif exchange has \(d_{ij}=0\) and \(M_{ij}=0\). Reverse edges satisfy
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij}.
\]

\subsection{Self-current, memory, and readout}

For transport projector \(\Pi_i(y)\),
\[
D_i^{\rm self}
=
\frac1{c_i}
\int_{A_i}
G_P\Pi_iD\Pi_i^\T G_P^\T\rho\,\dd y.
\]
Use \(\Pi_i=I\) for free unbounded charge translation and \(\Pi_i=0\) for
bounded internal polarization.

Memory coordinates are orthogonalized by state:
\[
\psi_\alpha^\perp(y)
=
\psi_\alpha(y)-\mathbb E[\psi_\alpha\mid A_{s(y)}].
\]
Then
\[
A_{\alpha\gamma}
=
\int
\nabla\psi_\alpha^\perp{}^\T
D\nabla\psi_\gamma^\perp\rho\,\dd y,
\qquad
h_{\alpha a}
=
\int
\nabla\psi_\alpha^\perp{}^\T
D\nabla P_a\,\rho\,\dd y.
\]
The memory correction is \(C^\psi=h^\T A^\#h\). The direct term and
finite-state drift are
\[
B=\sum_i c_iD_i^{\rm self}
+\frac12\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}M_{ij},
\qquad
b_i=\sum_jQ_{ij}d_{ij}.
\]
Solve
\[
-Q\chi_a=b_a,
\qquad
\sum_ic_i\chi_{i,a}=0,
\]
using
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
-Q & \one\\
c^\T & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\chi_a\\ \lambda_a
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
b_a\\0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
C^Q_{ab}=\sum_i c_i b_{i,a}\chi_{i,b},
\qquad
D_{\rm proj}=B-C^Q-C^\psi,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mScm}
=
10\frac{F^2}{3RT}\tr(D_{\rm proj}).
\]

\subsection{Coherence checks and GK limit}

Mass balance and generator checks are
\[
\left|\sum_i\nu_{i,a}c_i-C_a\right|
<
\tau_{\rm mass}\max(C_a,C_{\min}),
\]
\[
K_{ij}=K_{ji},
\quad
\sum_jQ_{ij}=0,
\quad
c_iQ_{ij}=c_jQ_{ji}.
\]
Solver residuals require
\[
\|Su-f\|_2<\tau_{\rm PDE},
\qquad
\|S^hu_a-f_a^h\|_2<\tau_{\rm moment},
\qquad
\|S^hw_{ab}-g_{ab}^h\|_2<\tau_{\rm moment}.
\]
Positivity and range checks are
\[
\|\Delta P\|\le L_{\max},
\quad
D_i^{\rm self}=D_i^{{\rm self},\T}\succeq0,
\quad
A=A^\T\succeq0,
\quad
\|(I-AA^\#)h_a\|\le\tau_{\rm null}\|h_a\|.
\]
The exact Green--Kubo form is
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\sum_{a=1}^3
\langle J_a,(-L)^\#J_a\rangle_\mu.
\]
For \(V_n=\operatorname{span}\{\one_{A_i},\psi_\alpha\}\), the projected
corrector solves
\[
\langle v,(-L)u_{n,a}\rangle_\mu
=
\langle v,J_a\rangle_\mu
\quad
\forall v\in V_n.
\]
With \(u_a^\star=(-L)^\#J_a\),
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}-\sigma_n
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\sum_{a=1}^3
\|u_a^\star-u_{n,a}\|_E^2.
\]
Refining basins, memory coordinates, quadrature, committor spaces, and moment
BVP spaces drives the projected conductivity to the Green--Kubo value for the
same generator.

\section{External Input and Canonical Recipe}

\subsection{External units}

The external formulation units are:
\[
\widehat{\phi}_s \ge 0
\quad\text{solvent volume ratio for solvent }s,
\]
\[
m_\ell>0
\quad [\molL]
\quad\text{salt molarity for salt }\ell,
\]
\[
p_a\ge0
\quad [\mathrm{wt\%}]
\quad\text{additive loading for additive }a.
\]
The model converts additive loading to a weight fraction
\[
w_a = \frac{p_a}{100}.
\]
Temperature is supplied in kelvin. If the external value is Celsius, use
\[
T=T_{{}^\circ{\rm C}}+273.15.
\]

\subsection{Solvent normalization}

The model requires a positive total solvent loading:
\[
\widehat{\Phi}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}\widehat{\phi}_s > 0.
\]
Each solvent entry retained in the recipe must be positive. The normalized
solvent fraction is
\[
\phi_s=\frac{\widehat{\phi}_s}{\widehat{\Phi}},
\qquad
\sum_s \phi_s=1.
\]
When additives are represented by explicit volume displacement, the solvent
fractions are first normalized to \(\phi_s^{\rm raw}\) and then scaled by the
nonadditive liquid volume:
\[
\phi_s
=
\phi_s^{\rm raw}(1-V_{\rm add}),
\qquad
\phi_s^{\rm raw}
=
\frac{\widehat{\phi}_s}{\widehat{\Phi}}.
\]
The additive volume \(V_{\rm add}\) is obtained from the mass-density conversion
below. Starting with \(V_{\rm add}=0\), one to three fixed-point updates are
enough for ordinary dilute additive loadings; the stopping rule is
\[
\left|
V_{\rm add}^{(k+1)}-V_{\rm add}^{(k)}
\right|
\le
\tau_{\rm add}.
\]

\subsection{Salt and additive normalization}

Salt entries with \(m_\ell>0\) are retained as ionic constituents. Additive
entries with \(w_a=0\) are skipped. Additives with \(w_a>0\) are split into:
\[
\mathcal A_{\rm ion}
=
\{a:\,a\text{ has all ionic transport descriptors}\},
\]
\[
\mathcal A_{\rm liq}
=
\mathcal A\setminus\mathcal A_{\rm ion}.
\]
Ionic additives enter the ionic constituent list after weight-fraction to
molarity conversion. Nonionic additives enter liquid density, liquid viscosity,
liquid dielectric, donor number, and additive-solvation support.

Salt molarity is converted to SI concentration by
\[
C_\ell^{\rm SI}=1000\,m_\ell
\qquad[\molm].
\]
For a salt with stoichiometric coefficients \(\nu_{+,\ell}\) and
\(\nu_{-,\ell}\),
\[
C_{\rm Li}^{\rm SI}
=
\sum_\ell \nu_{+,\ell}C_\ell^{\rm SI},
\qquad
C_{A_\ell}^{\rm SI}
=
\nu_{-,\ell}C_\ell^{\rm SI}.
\]
Charge neutrality is required:
\[
\sum_b z_b^{\rm net}C_b^{\rm SI}=0.
\]

\subsection{Mass-density conversion for weight-percent additives}

Additive wt\% is defined relative to the total electrolyte mass:
\[
w_a
=
\frac{\text{mass of additive }a}{\text{total solution mass}}.
\]
Let \(V_0=1\,{\rm m^3}\) be the nonadditive reference volume after solvent
normalization. Using SI densities for this conversion, the nonadditive mass is
\[
M_0
=
\sum_s \rho_s\phi_s V_0
+
\sum_\ell C_\ell^{\rm SI}M_\ell^{\rm kg}V_0,
\]
and the nonadditive occupied volume is
\[
V_{\rm occ,0}
=
\sum_s\phi_sV_0
+
\sum_\ell C_\ell^{\rm SI}\bar v_\ell V_0,
\qquad
\bar v_\ell=\frac{M_\ell^{\rm kg}}{\rho_\ell}.
\]
For total additive mass fraction
\[
w_\Sigma=\sum_a w_a<1,
\]
the total solution mass and additive masses are
\[
M_{\rm soln}
=
\frac{M_0}{1-w_\Sigma},
\qquad
M_a^{\rm add}=w_aM_{\rm soln}.
\]
The additive volume and solution density are
\[
V_a^{\rm add}
=
\frac{M_a^{\rm add}}{\rho_a},
\qquad
\rho_{\rm soln}
=
\frac{M_{\rm soln}}
{V_{\rm occ,0}+\sum_aV_a^{\rm add}}.
\]
The g mL\(^{-1}\) value used with molar masses in g mol\(^{-1}\) is
\[
\rho_{\rm soln}^{\rm g\,mL^{-1}}
=
10^{-3}\rho_{\rm soln}.
\]
The equivalent density iteration is
\[
\rho_a^{\rm mass}
=
w_a\rho_{\rm soln},
\qquad
C_a
=
\frac{\rho_a^{\rm mass}}{M_a^{\rm kg}}
\quad[\molm],
\]
\[
\phi_a
=
C_a\bar v_a,
\qquad
\bar v_a=\frac{M_a^{\rm kg}}{\rho_a}.
\]
A neutral additive may enter as a dilute liquid component or as a conserved
component for motif formation. It is counted once. Ionic additives enter the
ionic constituent list through \(C_a\).

\section{Liquid-Phase Mixture Properties}

Let the liquid component set be
\[
\mathcal U = \mathcal S \cup \mathcal A_{\rm liq}.
\]
For liquid-property averages, use the volume contributions
\[
v_i=\phi_i,
\qquad
V_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}v_i,
\qquad
\bar v_i=\frac{v_i}{V_{\rm liq}}.
\]
Then
\[
\epsilon_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_i\,\epsilon_i,
\qquad
DN_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_i\,DN_i.
\]

The bulk liquid density is
\[
\rho_{\rm liq}
=
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal U} v_i\rho_i}{V_{\rm liq}}
\quad [\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}].
\]
The neutral-liquid viscosity uses log-volume mixing:
\[
\log \eta_{\rm liq}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_i\log \eta_i,
\qquad
\eta_{\rm liq}=\exp(\log\eta_{\rm liq})
\quad [\cp].
\]

\section{Ionic Constituents}

\subsection{Explicit salts}

Each explicit salt \(\ell\in\mathcal L\) produces one ionic constituent
\[
i=\ell,
\qquad
m_i=m_\ell
\quad [\molL].
\]

\subsection{Ionic additives}

Each ionic additive \(a\in\mathcal A_{\rm ion}\) produces one ionic constituent
with molarity
\[
m_a
=
\frac{w_a\,\rho_{\rm soln}^{\rm g\,mL^{-1}}\,1000}{M_a}
\quad [\molL],
\]
where \(w_a\) is g additive per g electrolyte,
\(\rho_{\rm soln}^{\rm g\,mL^{-1}}\) is the solution density converted from
\(\rho_{\rm soln}\), and \(M_a\) is g mol\(^{-1}\).

\subsection{Constituent record}

For each ionic constituent \(i\), define:
\[
m_i,\quad
\Lambda^0_i,\quad
r^-_i,\quad
r^+_i,\quad
M_i,\quad
\rho_i,\quad
B_i,\quad
E^{\rm pair}_i,\quad
\delta^\epsilon_i.
\]
The model requires at least one ionic constituent:
\[
N_I = |\mathcal I| \ge 1.
\]

\section{Salt-Corrected Viscosity and Dielectric Constant}

The total ionic molarity is
\[
m_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_I}m_i.
\]

\subsection{Jones-Dole viscosity correction}

Let
\[
\beta_\eta =
\frac{
\Theta_\eta[B_\eta]
}{
\Theta_\eta[B_\eta^{\rm lin}]
},
\qquad
D_\eta=\Theta_\eta[D_\eta].
\]
The salt-corrected viscosity is
\[
\eta_{\rm cP}
=
\eta_{\rm liq}
\exp\!\left(
\beta_\eta\sum_i m_i B_i + D_\eta m_{\rm tot}^2
\right)
\quad [\cp],
\]
\[
\eta_{\rm Pa\,s}=10^{-3}\eta_{\rm cP}.
\]

\subsection{Dielectric decrement}

The molarity-weighted dielectric-decrement coefficient is
\[
\bar\delta^\epsilon
=
\frac{\sum_i m_i\delta^\epsilon_i}{m_{\rm tot}}.
\]
The electrolyte dielectric constant is
\[
\epsilon
=
\epsilon_{\rm liq}\exp(-\bar\delta^\epsilon m_{\rm tot}).
\]

\section{Reduced State Space}

The state count is
\[
n = 1+2N_I.
\]
State \(0\) is free lithium:
\[
s_0=\mathrm{Li^+}.
\]
For each ionic constituent \(i=1,\ldots,N_I\), define:
\[
s_{2i-1}=\mathrm{free\ anion\ }i,
\qquad
s_{2i}=\mathrm{associated\ pair\ }(\mathrm{Li},i).
\]
\subsection{Conserved components}

The conserved component vector has length \(N_I+1\):
\[
a=0\quad\text{for total lithium},
\qquad
a=i\quad\text{for anion constituent }i.
\]
The total component concentrations are
\[
C_0=1000\sum_i m_i,
\qquad
C_i=1000\,m_i
\quad [\molm].
\]

\subsection{Basin stoichiometry}

The stoichiometry matrix \(\nu\in\R^{n\times(N_I+1)}\) is
\[
\nu_{0,0}=1,
\]
\[
\nu_{2i-1,i}=1,
\]
\[
\nu_{2i,0}=1,
\qquad
\nu_{2i,i}=1.
\]
All omitted entries are zero.

\section{Ion-Pair Mass Action}

\subsection{Association constants}

For each ionic constituent \(i\), define the dielectric-screened binding energy
\[
\Delta G_i
=
\frac{1000\,E^{\rm pair}_i}{\epsilon}
\quad [\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}].
\]
The association driver is
\[
g_i = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta G_i}{RT}\right).
\]
The preliminary associated fraction is
\[
\alpha_i
=
\frac{g_i m_i}{1+g_i m_i}.
\]
The mass-action constant used by the reduced population solve is
\[
K_i
=
\frac{\alpha_i}{(1-\alpha_i)^2m_i}
\quad [\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}].
\]

\subsection{Shared-lithium solve}

Let
\[
L_{\rm tot}=\sum_i m_i.
\]
The free lithium molarity \(l\) is the unique root on \([0,L_{\rm tot}]\) of
\[
F_{\rm Li}(l)
=
l+\sum_i m_i\frac{K_i l}{1+K_i l}-L_{\rm tot}
=0.
\]
The numerical solve uses bisection with tolerance
\[
\frac{|F_{\rm Li}(l)|}{\max(L_{\rm tot},10^{-30})}
<10^{-10}
\]
and at most \(100\) iterations.

Then
\[
a_i=\frac{m_i}{1+K_i l},
\qquad
p_i=m_i-a_i,
\]
where \(a_i\) is free anion molarity and \(p_i\) is associated-pair molarity.

\subsection{State concentrations}

The state concentration vector \(c\in\R^n\) in mol m\(^{-3}\) is
\[
c_0 = 1000\,l,
\qquad
c_{2i-1}=1000\,a_i,
\qquad
c_{2i}=1000\,p_i.
\]
The mass balances enforced by construction are
\[
c_0+\sum_i c_{2i} = 1000\sum_i m_i,
\]
\[
c_{2i-1}+c_{2i}=1000\,m_i
\qquad\forall i.
\]

\section{Local Obstruction and Diffusivities}

\subsection{Ionic occupied volume}

The ionic occupied volume fraction is
\[
\varphi_{\rm ion}
=
\sum_i \frac{m_i M_i}{1000\,\rho_i}.
\]
The model requires
\[
0\le\varphi_{\rm ion}<1.
\]
The free-volume fraction is
\[
\varphi_{\rm free}=1-\varphi_{\rm ion}.
\]

\subsection{Size, charge-density, and additive-support factors}

Let \(r_{\rm Li}\) be the lithium solvated radius. Define molarity-weighted
ratios
\[
R_{\rm size}
=
\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}
\sum_i m_i\frac{r_{\rm Li}+r^-_i}{r_{\rm Li}},
\]
\[
R_{\rm charge}
=
\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}
\sum_i m_i\frac{r^+_i}{r^-_i}.
\]
The solvation-resistance driver is
\[
S_{\rm solv}=1+\frac{m_{\rm tot}}{DN_{\rm liq}}.
\]
The additive-solvation support factor is
\[
S_{\rm add}
=
1+
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{\rm liq}}
w_a\,\frac{DN_a}{DN_{\rm liq}}.
\]

\subsection{Diffusion attenuation}

Introduce the following positive configuration symbols from \(\Theta_{\rm cond}\):
\[
\begin{gathered}
\lambda_{\rm loc}, p_I,p_V,p_A,p_S,p_Q,p_D,
\lambda_{\rm ep},\lambda_{\rm rel},\lambda_{\rm cross},
\lambda_{\rm cloud},\lambda_{\rm act}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here \(\lambda_{\rm loc}\) is the obstruction strength, \(p_I,p_V,p_A,p_S,p_Q,p_D\)
are the ionic-strength, free-volume, additive-solvation, size,
charge-density, and solvation exponents, and
\(\lambda_{\rm ep},\lambda_{\rm rel},\lambda_{\rm cross},\lambda_{\rm cloud},
\lambda_{\rm act}\) set electrophoretic, relaxation, cross-relaxation,
charge-cloud, and activity drag.
Let
\[
\epsilon_{\rm ref}=\Theta_{\rm pair}[\epsilon_{\rm ref}].
\]
The local resistance factor is
\[
R_{\rm loc}
=
\lambda_{\rm loc}
m_{\rm tot}^{p_I}
R_{\rm size}^{p_S}
R_{\rm charge}^{p_Q}
\left(\frac{1}{\varphi_{\rm free}}\right)^{p_V}
S_{\rm solv}^{p_D}
S_{\rm add}^{-p_A}.
\]
The atmosphere, charge-cloud, and activity factors are
\[
R_{\rm atm}
=
(\lambda_{\rm ep}+\lambda_{\rm rel}+\lambda_{\rm cross})
m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon},
\]
\[
R_{\rm cloud}
=
\lambda_{\rm cloud}
R_{\rm charge}
m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon},
\]
\[
R_{\rm act}
=
\lambda_{\rm act}
m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon}.
\]
The diffusion attenuation factor is
\[
S_D
=
\frac{1}{1+R_{\rm loc}+R_{\rm atm}+R_{\rm cloud}+R_{\rm act}}.
\]

\subsection{Stokes-Einstein diffusivity}

For a radius \(r\,[\Ang]\) and Stokes-Einstein exponent \(\alpha\), the model
computes
\[
D_{\rm SE}(r,\eta,T,\alpha)
=
\frac{k_B T}
{c_{\rm SE}\pi\eta(r\,10^{-10})},
\]
where
\[
c_{\rm SE}=\Theta_{\rm SE}[c_{\rm SE}].
\]
If fractional Stokes-Einstein scaling is active, the value is multiplied by
\[
\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{\rm ref}}\right)^{1-1/\alpha},
\qquad
\eta_{\rm ref}=\Theta_{\rm SE}[\eta_{\rm ref}].
\]

The lithium diffusivity is
\[
D_{\rm Li}=D_{\rm SE}(r_{\rm Li},\eta_{\rm Pa\,s},T,\alpha_{\rm Li})\,S_D.
\]
For an anion \(i\), the solvated anion radius is
\[
\tilde r^-_i =
\begin{cases}
\bar r_i^{-,{\rm solv}}, & \text{if a solvated radius is specified},\\
r^-_i+\Theta_{\rm solv}[\Delta r_{\rm shell}], & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The anion Stokes-Einstein exponent is read from the ionic record if present
and otherwise from
\(\Theta_{\rm SE}[\alpha_{\rm anion}]\). The anion diffusivity is
\[
D^-_i=D_{\rm SE}(\tilde r^-_i,\eta_{\rm Pa\,s},T,\alpha^-_i)\,S_D.
\]

\section{Reduced Generator Primitives}

\subsection{Symmetric capacity fluxes}

Let
\[
\lambda_{\rm assoc}
=
\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_{\rm assoc}].
\]
For each ionic constituent \(i\), define a capture radius
\[
R^{\rm cap}_i=(r_{\rm Li}+\tilde r^-_i)\,10^{-10}\quad [\mathrm m].
\]
The association rate coefficient is
\[
k^{\rm assoc}_i
=
\lambda_{\rm assoc}
4\pi N_A
(D_{\rm Li}+D^-_i)
R^{\rm cap}_i.
\]
Using state concentrations \(c\) in mol m\(^{-3}\), the association flux is
\[
J_i
=
k^{\rm assoc}_i c_0 c_{2i-1}
\quad [\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}\,s^{-1}}].
\]
The symmetric capacity-flux matrix \(K\in\R^{n\times n}\) has nonzero entries
\[
K_{0,2i}=K_{2i,0}=\frac{1}{2}J_i,
\qquad
K_{2i-1,2i}=K_{2i,2i-1}=\frac{1}{2}J_i.
\]
The diagonal of \(K\) is zero.

\subsection{Reversible generator}

The finite-state reversible generator \(Q\) is
\[
Q_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
K_{ij}/c_i, & i\ne j,\\
-\sum_jK_{ij}/c_i, & i=j.
\end{cases}
\]
The generator identities are:
\[
\sum_j Q_{ij}=0,
\qquad
c_iQ_{ij}=c_jQ_{ji},
\qquad
c^\T Q=\zero^\T.
\]

\subsection{Transition pairs and path moments}

The transition-edge list contains two edges per ionic constituent:
\[
(0,2i),
\qquad
(2i-1,2i).
\]
Let
\[
\lambda_{\rm jump}
=\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_\ell],
\qquad
\lambda_P
=\exp\left(\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_{\rm pol}]\right).
\]
The transition length for constituent \(i\) is
\[
\ell_i
=
\lambda_{\rm jump}\lambda_P(r_{\rm Li}+\tilde r^-_i)10^{-10}.
\]
For each edge, the path sample set is
\[
\{\pm\ell_i e_x,\ \pm\ell_i e_y,\ \pm\ell_i e_z\},
\]
with equal weights. Therefore
\[
d_{ij}
=
\frac{1}{6}\sum_{q=1}^6\Delta P_{ijq}
=\zero,
\qquad
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\]
\[
M_{ij}
=
\frac{1}{6}\sum_{q=1}^6\Delta P_{ijq}\Delta P_{ijq}^{\T}
=
\frac{\ell_i^2}{3}I_3,
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij}.
\]
Every sampled displacement must satisfy
\[
\|\Delta P_{ijq}\|\le10^{-8}\ \mathrm m.
\]

\subsection{Self-current tensors}

The charge-polarization gradient is \(+I_3\) for free lithium, \(-I_3\) for a
free anion, and zero for an associated pair. The state-local self-current
tensors are:
\[
D^{\rm self}_0=D_{\rm Li}I_3,
\qquad
D^{\rm self}_{2i-1}=D^-_iI_3,
\qquad
D^{\rm self}_{2i}=\zero_{3\times3}.
\]

\section{Continuous Mori Memory}

\subsection{Memory-gradient scales}

Define the dielectric driver
\[
\Gamma_\epsilon
=
m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon}.
\]
Define the mixed ion-pair fraction used for memory scaling:
\[
\overline{E}^{\rm pair}
=
\frac{\sum_i m_i E^{\rm pair}_i}{m_{\rm tot}},
\qquad
\Delta\overline G
=
\frac{1000\,\overline{E}^{\rm pair}}{\epsilon},
\]
\[
\overline g=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\Delta\overline G}{RT}\right),
\qquad
\overline\alpha
=
\frac{\overline g\,m_{\rm tot}}{1+\overline g\,m_{\rm tot}}.
\]
The four memory coordinates are atmosphere, cage, partner, and orientation.
Their gradient scales are:
\[
g_{\rm atm}
=
\left[
(\lambda_{\rm ep}+\lambda_{\rm rel}+\lambda_{\rm cross})
\Gamma_\epsilon
\right]^{1/2},
\]
\[
g_{\rm cage}
=
\left[
\lambda_{\rm loc}
m_{\rm tot}^{p_I}
\left(\frac{1}{\varphi_{\rm free}}\right)^{p_V}
S_{\rm add}^{-p_A}
\right]^{1/2},
\]
\[
g_{\rm partner}
=
\left[
\lambda_{\rm cloud}R_{\rm charge}\Gamma_\epsilon
+\lambda_{\rm crowd}\overline\alpha
\right]^{1/2},
\]
\[
g_{\rm orient}
=
\left[
\lambda_{\rm orient}R_{\rm size}\Gamma_\epsilon
\right]^{1/2}.
\]
Here
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\rm crowd}
&=
\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_{\rm crowd}],\\
\lambda_{\rm orient}
&=
\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_{\rm orient}].
\end{aligned}
\]
Collect the scales into
\[
\begin{aligned}
g
&=
(g_{\rm atm},g_{\rm cage},g_{\rm partner},g_{\rm orient})^\T,\\
g&\in\R^4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The reduced-coordinate memory-gradient matrix is
\[
\nabla\psi =
\begin{bmatrix}
g_{\rm atm} & 0 & 0 & 0\\
g_{\rm cage} & 0 & 0 & 0\\
g_{\rm partner} & 0 & 0 & 0\\
g_{\rm orient} & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\subsection{Memory-current mobility coupling}

The memory-current coupling fraction is
\[
f_{\rm mem}
=
\lambda_{\rm cross}\frac{\Gamma_\epsilon}{1+\Gamma_\epsilon}.
\]
The range condition requires
\[
f_{\rm mem}<1.
\]
For a state with transport diffusivity \(D_s\), the reduced mobility matrix
has diagonal entries \(1,D_s,D_s,D_s\) and cross entries
\[
\mathcal D_{0a}=\mathcal D_{a0}
=
f_{\rm mem}\sqrt{\frac{D_s}{3}},
\qquad
a=1,2,3.
\]
Associated-pair states use \(D_s=0\).

\subsection{Mori matrices}

The continuous memory Dirichlet matrix \(A\in\R^{4\times4}\) and
current-coupling matrix \(h\in\R^{4\times3}\) are
\[
A
=
\sum_s c_s
\nabla\psi_s\,\mathcal D_s\,\nabla\psi_s^\T,
\]
\[
h
=
\sum_s c_s
\nabla\psi_s\,\mathcal D_s\,\nabla P_s^\T.
\]
In the reduced composition path, \(A\) is positive semidefinite by construction,
and \(h\) is nonzero when free charged states couple to memory coordinate \(0\).

For any symmetric positive semidefinite matrix \(A=V\Lambda V^\T\), define
\[
A^\#=V\diag(\lambda_1^\#,\ldots,\lambda_m^\#)V^\T,
\]
\[
\lambda_k^\#=
\begin{cases}
1/\lambda_k, & \lambda_k>10^{-12}\max_j\lambda_j,\\
0, & \lambda_k\le10^{-12}\max_j\lambda_j.
\end{cases}
\]
The model checks, for every Cartesian current-coupling vector \(h_a\), that
\[
\|(I-AA^\#)h_a\|
\le
10^{-8}\|h_a\|.
\]
If this fails, the current couples to a null memory mode and the calculation
raises an exception.

The continuous Mori correction is
\[
C_M=h^\T A^\# h.
\]

\section{Projected Diffusivity Density}

\subsection{Direct term}

The direct projected diffusivity-density tensor is
\[
B
=
\sum_i c_iD^{\rm self}_i
+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}M_{ij}.
\]
The first term is state-local self current. The second term is the direct
state-changing jump contribution.

\subsection{Finite-state memory correction}

Define the finite-state current drift
\[
b_i=\sum_j Q_{ij}d_{ij}\in\R^3.
\]
For each Cartesian axis \(a\), solve the constrained weighted Poisson system
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
-Q & \one\\
c^\T & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\chi^{(a)}\\
\lambda^{(a)}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
b^{(a)}\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The finite-state memory correction is
\[
(C_Q)_{ab}
=
\sum_i c_i\,b_{ia}\,\chi_i^{(b)}.
\]
For the exact current reduced composition path above, \(d_{ij}=\zero\), so
\[
C_Q=\zero.
\]
\(C_Q\) remains part of the primitive set because nonzero displacement first
moments are valid projected-generator inputs.

\subsection{Final diffusivity tensor}

The projected diffusivity-density tensor is
\[
D_{\rm proj}
=
\operatorname{PSD}\!\left(\frac{B-C_Q-C_M+(B-C_Q-C_M)^\T}{2}\right).
\]
The \(\operatorname{PSD}\) operation only removes roundoff-scale negative
eigenvalues. If the most negative eigenvalue is below
\[
-10^{-10}\max_k|\lambda_k|,
\]
the model raises an exception.

\section{Scalar Conductivity}

The scalar conductivity is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\Sm}
=
\frac{F^2}{RT}\frac{\tr(D_{\rm proj})}{3}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\mScm}=10\,\sigma_{\Sm}.
}
\]

The numerical output should include the scalar conductivity and the primitive
ledger
\[
\sigma_{\Sm},\quad
\sigma_{\mScm},\quad
D_{\rm proj},\quad
B,\quad
C_Q,\quad
C_M,\quad
c,\quad
K,\quad
Q,\quad
d,\quad
M,\quad
D^{\rm self},\quad
A,\quad
h.
\]
The trace ledger must separate state self current, direct transition terms,
finite-state memory correction, continuous Mori correction, and final projected
diffusivity.

\section{Complete Algorithm}

This section gives the exact numerical procedure for the reduced composition
calculation. The same primitive definitions also accept quadrature-derived
generator primitives from the full generator construction.

\subsection{Dimensions and numerical tolerances}

Let
\[
N_S=|\mathcal S|,
\qquad
N_L=|\mathcal L|,
\qquad
N_{\rm add}=|\mathcal A|,
\qquad
d=3.
\]
After ionic additives are converted, let \(N_I\) be the number of ionic
constituents. The finite state count is
\[
n=1+2N_I.
\]
The reduced memory basis has
\[
m_\psi=4
\]
coordinates in the composition-level path. Therefore
\[
c\in\R^n,
\quad
K,Q\in\R^{n\times n},
\quad
d\in\R^{n\times n\times 3},
\quad
M\in\R^{n\times n\times 3\times 3},
\]
\[
D^{\rm self}_i\in\R^{3\times3},
\quad
A\in\R^{m_\psi\times m_\psi},
\quad
h\in\R^{m_\psi\times3},
\quad
B,C_Q,C_M,D_{\rm proj}\in\R^{3\times3}.
\]

The default numerical tolerances are
\[
\tau_{\rm add}=10^{-12},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm Li}=10^{-10},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm mass}=10^{-10},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm row}=10^{-12},
\]
\[
\tau_{\rm bal}=10^{-10},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm sym}=10^{-12},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm psd}=10^{-10},
\qquad
\tau_{\rm null}=10^{-8}.
\]
The maximum iteration counts are
\[
N_{\rm add}^{\max}=50,
\qquad
N_{\rm bis}^{\max}=100.
\]
For relative residuals, use the scale
\[
s(x)=\max(\|x\|_\infty,10^{-30})
\]
for vector \(x\), and \(s(x)=\max(|x|,10^{-30})\) for scalar \(x\).
Every failed tolerance check terminates the calculation.

\subsection{Indexed numerical procedure}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.}]
\item Convert the temperature:
\[
T=
\begin{cases}
T_{{}^\circ{\rm C}}+273.15, & \text{if Celsius is supplied},\\
T_{\rm K}, & \text{if kelvin is supplied}.
\end{cases}
\]
Require
\[
T>0.
\]

\item Validate external loadings:
\[
\widehat\phi_s\ge0\quad(s=1,\ldots,N_S),
\qquad
m_\ell\ge0\quad(\ell=1,\ldots,N_L),
\qquad
p_a\ge0\quad(a=1,\ldots,N_{\rm add}),
\]
\[
\sum_{s=1}^{N_S}\widehat\phi_s>0,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_L}m_\ell>0.
\]
Convert additive wt\% to mass fraction:
\[
w_a=p_a/100,
\qquad
w_\Sigma=\sum_{a=1}^{N_{\rm add}}w_a.
\]
Require
\[
0\le w_\Sigma<1.
\]

\item Normalize solvent ratios:
\[
\widehat\Phi=\sum_{s=1}^{N_S}\widehat\phi_s,
\qquad
\phi_s^{\rm raw}=\widehat\phi_s/\widehat\Phi.
\]
Check
\[
\left|\sum_s\phi_s^{\rm raw}-1\right|\le\tau_{\rm mass}.
\]

\item Convert salt molarities to SI concentrations:
\[
C_\ell^{\rm SI}=1000\,m_\ell
\qquad(\ell=1,\ldots,N_L).
\]
For each salt, read stoichiometric coefficients \(\nu_{+,\ell}\) and
\(\nu_{-,\ell}\). The conserved lithium and anion concentrations are
\[
C_{\rm Li}^{\rm salt}
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_L}\nu_{+,\ell}C_\ell^{\rm SI},
\qquad
C_{A_\ell}^{\rm salt}
=
\nu_{-,\ell}C_\ell^{\rm SI}.
\]
The salt charge residual is
\[
r_z^{\rm salt}
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_L}
\left(
\nu_{+,\ell}z_{\rm Li}
+
\nu_{-,\ell}z_{A_\ell}
\right)C_\ell^{\rm SI}.
\]
Require
\[
|r_z^{\rm salt}|/s(C_{\rm Li}^{\rm salt})\le\tau_{\rm mass}.
\]

\item Convert property units used by the density fixed point:
\[
\rho_i^{\rm SI}=1000\,\rho_i^{\rm g\,mL^{-1}},
\qquad
M_i^{\rm kg}=10^{-3}M_i^{\rm g\,mol^{-1}}.
\]
Set \(V_0=1\,{\rm m^3}\) and \(V_{\rm add}^{(0)}=0\). For
\(k=0,\ldots,N_{\rm add}^{\max}-1\), compute
\[
\phi_s^{(k)}=\phi_s^{\rm raw}(1-V_{\rm add}^{(k)}),
\]
\[
M_0^{(k)}
=
V_0\sum_s\rho_s^{\rm SI}\phi_s^{(k)}
+
V_0\sum_\ell C_\ell^{\rm SI}M_\ell^{\rm kg},
\]
\[
V_{\rm occ,0}^{(k)}
=
V_0\sum_s\phi_s^{(k)}
+
V_0\sum_\ell C_\ell^{\rm SI}
\frac{M_\ell^{\rm kg}}{\rho_\ell^{\rm SI}},
\]
\[
M_{\rm soln}^{(k)}
=
\frac{M_0^{(k)}}{1-w_\Sigma},
\qquad
M_a^{{\rm add},(k)}
=
w_aM_{\rm soln}^{(k)},
\]
\[
V_a^{{\rm add},(k)}
=
\frac{M_a^{{\rm add},(k)}}{\rho_a^{\rm SI}},
\qquad
\rho_{\rm soln}^{(k)}
=
\frac{M_{\rm soln}^{(k)}}
{V_{\rm occ,0}^{(k)}+\sum_aV_a^{{\rm add},(k)}},
\]
\[
C_a^{(k)}
=
\frac{w_a\rho_{\rm soln}^{(k)}}{M_a^{\rm kg}},
\qquad
\phi_a^{(k)}
=
C_a^{(k)}
\frac{M_a^{\rm kg}}{\rho_a^{\rm SI}},
\]
\[
V_{\rm add}^{(k+1)}
=
\sum_a\phi_a^{(k)}.
\]
Stop at the first \(k\) satisfying
\[
|V_{\rm add}^{(k+1)}-V_{\rm add}^{(k)}|
\le
\tau_{\rm add}\max(1,V_{\rm add}^{(k+1)}).
\]
The converged values are
\[
\phi_s=\phi_s^{(k)},
\qquad
\phi_a=\phi_a^{(k)},
\qquad
C_a=C_a^{(k)},
\qquad
\rho_{\rm soln}=\rho_{\rm soln}^{(k)}.
\]
If the fixed point reaches \(N_{\rm add}^{\max}\), the calculation terminates.

\item Split additives into ionic and neutral-liquid sets. An ionic additive
must provide charge, transport radius, association, density, molar mass,
viscosity, dielectric-decrement, and limiting-conductivity descriptors. A
neutral liquid additive must provide density, viscosity, dielectric constant,
donor number, and solvation descriptors. Missing required data terminates the
calculation.

\item Form the liquid set
\[
\mathcal U=\mathcal S\cup\mathcal A_{\rm liq}.
\]
For every \(i\in\mathcal U\), define \(v_i=\phi_i\) and
\[
V_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}v_i,
\qquad
\bar v_i=v_i/V_{\rm liq}.
\]
Require \(V_{\rm liq}>0\). Compute
\[
\epsilon_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_i\epsilon_i,
\qquad
DN_{\rm liq}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_iDN_i,
\]
\[
\rho_{\rm liq}
=
\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}v_i\rho_i}{V_{\rm liq}},
\qquad
\log\eta_{\rm liq}
=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\bar v_i\log\eta_i.
\]
Set
\[
\eta_{\rm liq}=\exp(\log\eta_{\rm liq}).
\]
Require
\[
\rho_{\rm liq}>0,
\qquad
\eta_{\rm liq}>0,
\qquad
\epsilon_{\rm liq}>0,
\qquad
DN_{\rm liq}>0.
\]

\item Build the ionic constituent list. Explicit salts contribute
\[
m_i=m_\ell.
\]
Ionic additives contribute
\[
m_i=C_a/1000.
\]
After concatenation, require
\[
N_I\ge1,
\qquad
m_i>0\quad(i=1,\ldots,N_I).
\]
Each constituent has
\[
\nu_{+,i},\ \nu_{-,i},\ z_{+,i},\ z_{-,i},\
\Lambda_i^0,\ r_i^-,\ r_i^+,\ M_i,\ \rho_i,\ B_i,\
E_i^{\rm pair},\ \delta_i^\epsilon.
\]
Check total ionic charge neutrality after ionic additives are included:
\[
r_z^{\rm ion}
=
1000\sum_{i=1}^{N_I}
m_i(\nu_{+,i}z_{+,i}+\nu_{-,i}z_{-,i}),
\]
\[
|r_z^{\rm ion}|/s(1000\sum_i m_i)\le\tau_{\rm mass}.
\]

\item Compute salt-corrected liquid properties:
\[
m_{\rm tot}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_I}m_i,
\]
\[
\beta_\eta
=
\frac{\Theta_\eta[B_\eta]}{\Theta_\eta[B_\eta^{\rm lin}]},
\qquad
D_\eta=\Theta_\eta[D_\eta],
\]
\[
\eta_{\rm cP}
=
\eta_{\rm liq}
\exp\left(
\beta_\eta\sum_i m_iB_i+D_\eta m_{\rm tot}^2
\right),
\qquad
\eta_{\rm Pa\,s}=10^{-3}\eta_{\rm cP},
\]
\[
\bar\delta^\epsilon
=
\frac{\sum_i m_i\delta_i^\epsilon}{m_{\rm tot}},
\qquad
\epsilon
=
\epsilon_{\rm liq}\exp(-\bar\delta^\epsilon m_{\rm tot}).
\]
Require
\[
\eta_{\rm cP}>0,
\qquad
\eta_{\rm Pa\,s}>0,
\qquad
\epsilon>0.
\]

\item Allocate finite states. Two basis modes are allowed.

For the reduced composition basis, set
\[
n=1+2N_I.
\]
The state indices are
\[
0,\quad 2i-1,\quad 2i
\qquad(i=1,\ldots,N_I),
\]
for free lithium, free anion \(i\), and associated pair \(i\). Allocate
\[
c=\zero_n,
\qquad
K=\zero_{n\times n},
\qquad
Q=\zero_{n\times n},
\]
\[
d=\zero_{n\times n\times3},
\qquad
M=\zero_{n\times n\times3\times3}.
\]
Build \(\nu\in\R^{n\times(N_I+1)}\) by
\[
\nu_{0,0}=1,\qquad
\nu_{2i-1,i}=1,\qquad
\nu_{2i,0}=1,\qquad
\nu_{2i,i}=1,
\]
with all other entries zero.

For the sparse physical-feature basis, evaluate the joint feature label
\[
\alpha(q)=
\left[
f_{\rm pair},f_{\rm LiShell},f_{\rm ligand},f_{\rm anion},
f_{\rm orient},f_{\rm cluster},f_{\rm env},f_{\rm atm}
\right]
\]
on the quadrature set. Instantiate
\[
A_\alpha=\{q:\alpha(q)=\alpha\}
\]
only when
\[
Z_\alpha>Z_{\min}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\nu_{\alpha a}\le N_a\ \forall a.
\]
Then
\[
n=|\{\alpha:Z_\alpha>Z_{\min}\}|,
\qquad
c_\alpha=
\sum_{q_{r}\in A_\alpha}W_r,
\]
where \(W_r\) are the normalized density quadrature weights. In this mode
\[
\nu\in\R^{n\times |\mathcal C|}
\]
is read from the basin stoichiometry, \(D_\alpha^{\rm self}\) is computed from
the charged-center projector, and \(K,Q,d,M\) are computed by the transition
families and committor/moment-BVP rules above. The remaining tensor assembly,
Poisson memory, Mori memory, and conductivity readout are unchanged.

\item Compute association constants. For \(i=1,\ldots,N_I\),
\[
\Delta G_i=\frac{1000\,E_i^{\rm pair}}{\epsilon},
\qquad
\zeta_i^{\rm pair}
=
-\frac{\Delta G_i}{RT}+\log m_i.
\]
Evaluate the logistic fraction by the overflow-stable identity
\[
\alpha_i=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{1+\exp(-\zeta_i^{\rm pair})},
& \zeta_i^{\rm pair}\ge0,\\[8pt]
\dfrac{\exp(\zeta_i^{\rm pair})}
{1+\exp(\zeta_i^{\rm pair})},
& \zeta_i^{\rm pair}<0.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
K_i^{\rm eq}
=
\frac{\alpha_i}{(1-\alpha_i)^2m_i}.
\]
Require
\[
0\le\alpha_i<1,
\qquad
0<K_i^{\rm eq}<\infty.
\]

\item Solve shared-lithium mass action. Define
\[
L_{\rm tot}=\sum_i m_i,
\qquad
F_{\rm Li}(l)
=
l+\sum_i m_i
\frac{K_i^{\rm eq}l}{1+K_i^{\rm eq}l}
-L_{\rm tot}.
\]
Initialize
\[
l_{\rm lo}^{(0)}=0,
\qquad
l_{\rm hi}^{(0)}=L_{\rm tot}.
\]
For \(q=0,\ldots,N_{\rm bis}^{\max}-1\), set
\[
l_{\rm mid}^{(q)}
=
\frac{l_{\rm lo}^{(q)}+l_{\rm hi}^{(q)}}{2}.
\]
If \(F_{\rm Li}(l_{\rm mid}^{(q)})\le0\), set
\[
l_{\rm lo}^{(q+1)}=l_{\rm mid}^{(q)},
\qquad
l_{\rm hi}^{(q+1)}=l_{\rm hi}^{(q)}.
\]
If \(F_{\rm Li}(l_{\rm mid}^{(q)})>0\), set
\[
l_{\rm lo}^{(q+1)}=l_{\rm lo}^{(q)},
\qquad
l_{\rm hi}^{(q+1)}=l_{\rm mid}^{(q)}.
\]
Stop when
\[
\frac{|F_{\rm Li}(l_{\rm mid}^{(q)})|}{s(L_{\rm tot})}
\le\tau_{\rm Li}
\]
and
\[
\frac{l_{\rm hi}^{(q)}-l_{\rm lo}^{(q)}}{s(L_{\rm tot})}
\le\tau_{\rm Li}.
\]
Set \(l=l_{\rm mid}^{(q)}\). If bisection reaches
\(N_{\rm bis}^{\max}\), the calculation terminates.

\item Compute free-anion and pair molarities:
\[
a_i=\frac{m_i}{1+K_i^{\rm eq}l},
\qquad
p_i=m_i-a_i.
\]
Populate \(c\) in mol m\(^{-3}\):
\[
c_0=1000\,l,
\qquad
c_{2i-1}=1000\,a_i,
\qquad
c_{2i}=1000\,p_i.
\]
Check component balance. Let
\[
C=
\left(
1000\sum_i m_i,\,
1000m_1,\ldots,1000m_{N_I}
\right)^\T.
\]
The residual is
\[
r_{\rm mass}=\nu^\T c-C.
\]
Require
\[
\|r_{\rm mass}\|_\infty/s(C)\le\tau_{\rm mass},
\qquad
c_j>0\quad(j=0,\ldots,n-1).
\]

\item Compute obstruction scalars:
\[
\varphi_{\rm ion}
=
\sum_i\frac{m_iM_i}{1000\,\rho_i},
\qquad
\varphi_{\rm free}=1-\varphi_{\rm ion}.
\]
Require
\[
0\le\varphi_{\rm ion}<1,
\qquad
\varphi_{\rm free}>0.
\]
Compute
\[
R_{\rm size}
=
\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}
\sum_i m_i\frac{r_{\rm Li}+r_i^-}{r_{\rm Li}},
\qquad
R_{\rm charge}
=
\frac{1}{m_{\rm tot}}
\sum_i m_i\frac{r_i^+}{r_i^-},
\]
\[
S_{\rm solv}=1+\frac{m_{\rm tot}}{DN_{\rm liq}},
\qquad
S_{\rm add}
=
1+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_{\rm liq}}w_a\frac{DN_a}{DN_{\rm liq}}.
\]
The local obstruction is
\[
R_{\rm loc}
=
\lambda_{\rm loc}
m_{\rm tot}^{p_I}
R_{\rm size}^{p_S}
R_{\rm charge}^{p_Q}
\varphi_{\rm free}^{-p_V}
S_{\rm solv}^{p_D}
S_{\rm add}^{-p_A}.
\]
The atmosphere terms are
\[
R_{\rm atm}
=
(\lambda_{\rm ep}+\lambda_{\rm rel}+\lambda_{\rm cross})
m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon},
\]
\[
R_{\rm cloud}
=
\lambda_{\rm cloud}R_{\rm charge}m_{\rm tot}
\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon},
\qquad
R_{\rm act}
=
\lambda_{\rm act}m_{\rm tot}\frac{\epsilon_{\rm ref}}{\epsilon}.
\]
Set
\[
S_D
=
\frac{1}{1+R_{\rm loc}+R_{\rm atm}+R_{\rm cloud}+R_{\rm act}}.
\]
Require \(0<S_D\le1\).

\item Compute diffusivities:
\[
D_{\rm SE}(r,\eta,T,\alpha)
=
\frac{k_BT}{c_{\rm SE}\pi\eta(r\,10^{-10})}
\left(\frac{\eta}{\eta_{\rm ref}}\right)^{1-1/\alpha}.
\]
Then
\[
D_{\rm Li}
=
D_{\rm SE}(r_{\rm Li},\eta_{\rm Pa\,s},T,\alpha_{\rm Li})S_D.
\]
For each anion,
\[
\tilde r_i^-
=
\begin{cases}
\bar r_i^{-,{\rm solv}}, & \text{if specified},\\
r_i^-+\Theta_{\rm solv}[\Delta r_{\rm shell}], & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
\[
D_i^-
=
D_{\rm SE}(\tilde r_i^-,\eta_{\rm Pa\,s},T,\alpha_i^-)S_D.
\]
Require
\[
D_{\rm Li}>0,
\qquad
D_i^->0.
\]

\item Build self-current tensors:
\[
D_0^{\rm self}=D_{\rm Li}I_3,
\qquad
D_{2i-1}^{\rm self}=D_i^-I_3,
\qquad
D_{2i}^{\rm self}=\zero_{3\times3}.
\]
Every \(D_j^{\rm self}\) must be symmetric positive semidefinite.

\item Build symmetric capacity fluxes. For \(i=1,\ldots,N_I\), define
\[
R_i^{\rm cap}
=(r_{\rm Li}+\tilde r_i^-)\,10^{-10},
\]
\[
k_i^{\rm assoc}
=
\lambda_{\rm assoc}
4\pi N_A(D_{\rm Li}+D_i^-)R_i^{\rm cap},
\]
\[
J_i=k_i^{\rm assoc}c_0c_{2i-1}.
\]
Set the only nonzero off-diagonal capacity entries:
\[
K_{0,2i}=K_{2i,0}=\frac12J_i,
\qquad
K_{2i-1,2i}=K_{2i,2i-1}=\frac12J_i.
\]
Set \(K_{jj}=0\). Check
\[
K=K^\T,
\qquad
K_{ij}\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_{i,j}K_{ij}>0.
\]

\item Build the reversible generator:
\[
Q_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
K_{ij}/c_i, & i\ne j,\\
-\sum_{k\ne i}K_{ik}/c_i, & i=j.
\end{cases}
\]
Check row sums and detailed balance:
\[
\max_i\left|\sum_jQ_{ij}\right|
\le
\tau_{\rm row}\max(1,\max_i|Q_{ii}|),
\]
\[
\max_{i,j}|c_iQ_{ij}-c_jQ_{ji}|
\le
\tau_{\rm bal}\max(1,\max_{i,j}K_{ij}).
\]

\item Build transition moments. The edge set is
\[
\mathcal E
=
\{(0,2i),(2i-1,2i):i=1,\ldots,N_I\}.
\]
For each \(e=(u,v)\in\mathcal E\) tied to constituent \(i\), compute
\[
\ell_i
=
\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_\ell]\,
\exp(\Theta_{\rm cond}[\lambda_{\rm pol}])
(r_{\rm Li}+\tilde r_i^-)\,10^{-10}.
\]
Use six quadrature displacements
\[
\delta_{i1}=+\ell_i e_x,
\quad
\delta_{i2}=-\ell_i e_x,
\quad
\delta_{i3}=+\ell_i e_y,
\]
\[
\delta_{i4}=-\ell_i e_y,
\quad
\delta_{i5}=+\ell_i e_z,
\quad
\delta_{i6}=-\ell_i e_z.
\]
Set
\[
d_{uv}=\frac16\sum_{q=1}^6\delta_{iq},
\qquad
M_{uv}=\frac16\sum_{q=1}^6\delta_{iq}\delta_{iq}^{\T}.
\]
Set reverse moments
\[
d_{vu}=-d_{uv},
\qquad
M_{vu}=M_{uv}.
\]
All nonedge entries remain zero. Check
\[
\|d_{uv}\|_\infty\le\tau_{\rm sym}\ell_i,
\qquad
\left\|M_{uv}-\frac{\ell_i^2}{3}I_3\right\|_F
\le\tau_{\rm sym}\ell_i^2,
\]
\[
\|\delta_{iq}\|\le10^{-8}\ {\rm m}.
\]

\item If transition moments are obtained from generator quadrature instead,
use quadrature points \(q_{i\alpha}\), weights \(w_{i\alpha}>0\), and
\(\alpha=1,\ldots,N_i^{\rm quad}\). The total quadrature count is
\[
N_{\rm quad}=\sum_iN_i^{\rm quad}.
\]
The weak committor linear system for an edge has matrix dimension
\[
N_{\rm FE}^{(ij)}\times N_{\rm FE}^{(ij)}.
\]
Solve until
\[
\frac{\|R_{ij}^{\rm comm}\|_2}{s(f_{ij})}\le\tau_{\rm row}.
\]
For deterministic endpoint moments, assemble the conditioned stiffness matrix
\[
S^{h}_{mn}
=
\int_{\Omega_{ij}}
\nabla\varphi_m^\T D_x\nabla\varphi_n\,q_{ij}^2\rho_x\,\dd q.
\]
For each Cartesian component and pair, solve
\[
S^hu_a=f_a^h,
\qquad
S^hw_{ab}=g_{ab}^h.
\]
Stop only when
\[
\frac{\|S^hu_a-f_a^h\|_2}{s(f_a^h)}\le\tau_{\rm row},
\qquad
\frac{\|S^hw_{ab}-g_{ab}^h\|_2}{s(g_{ab}^h)}\le\tau_{\rm row}.
\]
Compute \(d_{ij}\) and \(M_{ij}\) by the surface quadrature on
\(\partial A_i\). Then enforce
\[
d_{ji}=-d_{ij},
\qquad
M_{ji}=M_{ij}.
\]

\item Build memory matrices. For every state \(s=0,\ldots,n-1\), form
\[
\nabla\psi_s\in\R^{4\times4},
\qquad
\mathcal D_s\in\R^{4\times4},
\qquad
\nabla P_s\in\R^{3\times4}.
\]
Use
\[
D_s=
\begin{cases}
D_{\rm Li}, & s=0,\\
D_i^-, & s=2i-1,\\
0, & s=2i.
\end{cases}
\]
The memory-current mobility coupling is
\[
f_{\rm mem}
=
\lambda_{\rm cross}
\frac{\Gamma_\epsilon}{1+\Gamma_\epsilon}.
\]
Require \(f_{\rm mem}<1\). Fill
\[
(\mathcal D_s)_{00}=1,
\qquad
(\mathcal D_s)_{aa}=D_s\quad(a=1,2,3),
\]
\[
(\mathcal D_s)_{0a}=(\mathcal D_s)_{a0}
=
f_{\rm mem}\sqrt{D_s/3}.
\]
Then
\[
A=\sum_s c_s\nabla\psi_s\mathcal D_s\nabla\psi_s^\T,
\qquad
h=\sum_s c_s\nabla\psi_s\mathcal D_s\nabla P_s^\T.
\]
Check
\[
\|A-A^\T\|_F/s(A)\le\tau_{\rm sym}.
\]
Diagonalize \(A=V\Lambda V^\T\) and require
\[
\lambda_{\min}(A)\ge-\tau_{\rm psd}\max(1,\lambda_{\max}(A)).
\]
Build \(A^\#\) using the pseudoinverse rule with threshold
\[
10^{-12}\lambda_{\max}(A).
\]
For each Cartesian column \(h_a\), require
\[
\|(I-AA^\#)h_a\|_2/s(h_a)\le\tau_{\rm null}.
\]

\item Compute the direct diffusivity-density tensor:
\[
B=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}c_jD_j^{\rm self}
+\frac12\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}K_{ij}M_{ij}.
\]
Check
\[
\|B-B^\T\|_F/s(B)\le\tau_{\rm sym},
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(B)\ge-\tau_{\rm psd}\max(1,\lambda_{\max}(B)).
\]

\item Compute finite-state memory. First compute
\[
b_i=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}Q_{ij}d_{ij}\in\R^3.
\]
For each Cartesian axis \(a=1,2,3\), solve the \((n+1)\times(n+1)\)
constrained system
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
-Q & \one\\
c^\T & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\chi^{(a)}\\
\lambda^{(a)}
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
b^{(a)}\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Require
\[
\frac{
\left\|
\begin{bmatrix}
-Q & \one\\
c^\T & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
\chi^{(a)}\\
\lambda^{(a)}
\end{bmatrix}
-
\begin{bmatrix}
b^{(a)}\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
\right\|_2
}{s(b^{(a)})}
\le\tau_{\rm row}.
\]
Set
\[
(C_Q)_{ab}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_i b_{ia}\chi_i^{(b)}.
\]
Symmetrize by
\[
C_Q\leftarrow\frac12(C_Q+C_Q^\T).
\]
Require
\[
\lambda_{\min}(C_Q)\ge-\tau_{\rm psd}\max(1,\lambda_{\max}(C_Q)).
\]

\item Compute the continuous memory correction:
\[
C_M=h^\T A^\#h.
\]
Symmetrize by
\[
C_M\leftarrow\frac12(C_M+C_M^\T).
\]
Require
\[
\lambda_{\min}(C_M)\ge-\tau_{\rm psd}\max(1,\lambda_{\max}(C_M)).
\]

\item Compute the final projected tensor:
\[
D_{\rm raw}=B-C_Q-C_M,
\qquad
D_{\rm proj}=\frac12(D_{\rm raw}+D_{\rm raw}^\T).
\]
Let \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) be the eigenvalues of \(D_{\rm proj}\).
Require
\[
\min_a\lambda_a
\ge
-\tau_{\rm psd}\max(1,\max_a|\lambda_a|).
\]
Roundoff-scale negative eigenvalues may be set to zero by spectral projection;
larger negative eigenvalues terminate the calculation.

\item Compute conductivity:
\[
\sigma_{\Sm}
=
\frac{F^2}{RT}\frac{D_{{\rm proj},11}
+D_{{\rm proj},22}
+D_{{\rm proj},33}}{3},
\]
\[
\sigma_{\mScm}=10\,\sigma_{\Sm}.
\]
Require
\[
\sigma_{\Sm}\ge0,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mScm}\ge0.
\]

\item Return the complete primitive ledger:
\[
\begin{gathered}
T,\ \phi_s,\ \phi_a,\ C_a,\ \rho_{\rm soln},\
\rho_{\rm liq},\ \eta_{\rm liq},\ \eta_{\rm cP},\
\epsilon_{\rm liq},\ \epsilon,\ DN_{\rm liq},\\

\end{gathered}
\]
The scalar conductivity must be reproducible from this ledger without any
terminal correction factor.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Effect Identification and Basis Refinement}

\subsection{Primitive sensitivity attribution}

An electrolyte effect is encoded only if a perturbation \(\theta\) changes at
least one primitive:
\[
c,\ K,\ Q,\ d,\ M,\ D^{\rm self},\ A,\ h.
\]
For a small perturbation \(\delta\theta\), compute central differences
\[
\partial_\theta X
\approx
\frac{X(\theta+\delta\theta)-X(\theta-\delta\theta)}{2\delta\theta},
\qquad
X\in\{c,K,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h,\sigma\}.
\]
Sensitivity scores are
\[
S_c=\|\partial_\theta c\|_2,
\qquad
S_K=\|\partial_\theta K\|_F,
\]
\[
S_{dM}=\|\partial_\theta d\|_F+\|\partial_\theta M\|_F,
\]
\[
S_D=\|\partial_\theta D^{\rm self}\|_F,
\qquad
S_{Ah}=\|\partial_\theta A\|_F+\|\partial_\theta h\|_F.
\]
The effect is assigned to every nonzero primitive family. A perturbation that
changes conductivity while leaving all primitive sensitivities zero is outside
the model.

\subsection{Conductivity-effect ledger}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.25em,style=nextline]
\item[Free-ion fraction, \(c_i\).] Population shifts between free and
associated states.
\item[Ion association, \(c_i,K_{ij}\).] Association changes basin weights and
transition capacities.
\item[SSIP/CIP balance, \(c_i,K_{ij},D_i^{\rm self}\).] Separation class
changes population and mobility.
\item[Aggregate formation, \(c_i,K_{ij},M_{ij},D_i^{\rm self}\).] Cluster
basins alter carriers, jumps, and self-current.
\item[Neutral ligand coordination, \(c_i,K_{ij},A,h\).] Ligand residence
changes populations and memory.
\item[Solvation competition, \(U_x\to c_i,K_{ij}\).] Donor/acceptor
competition changes free energy and flux.
\item[Coulomb association, \(U_x\to c_i,K_{ij}\).] Charge attraction changes
pair probability and capacity.
\item[Born/desolvation, \(U_x\to c_i,K_{ij}\).] Desolvation barrier changes
association and dissociation.
\item[Activity nonideality, \(U_x\to c_i,K_{ij}\).] Nonideal chemical
potentials alter populations.
\item[Viscosity, \(D_x\to D_i^{\rm self},K_{ij},A\).] Mobility and Dirichlet
forms change.
\item[Hydrodynamic size, \(D_x\to D_i^{\rm self},K_{ij}\).] Stokes resistance
changes transport.
\item[Anion shape/denticity, \(U_x,D_x,K_{ij},A,h\).] Orientation and
coordination alter flux and memory.
\item[Charge-cloud radius, \(U_x,D_x,A,h\).] Atmosphere relaxation changes drag
and memory.
\item[Local crowding/free volume, \(U_x,D_x,K_{ij},A,h\).] Packing changes
barriers and mobility.
\item[Li-anion co-motion, \(D_i^{\rm self},M_{ij}\).] Cross covariance
increases or suppresses current.
\item[Li-anion anticorrelation, \(D_i^{\rm self},M_{ij}\).] Opposite motion
increases charge displacement.
\item[Partner switching, \(K_{ij},d_{ij},M_{ij},A,h\).] Identity exchange
creates jump and memory terms.
\item[Structural hopping, \(K_{ij},d_{ij},M_{ij}\).] Site exchange changes
transition moments.
\item[Bound neutral polarization, \(A,h\).] Polarizable neutral motifs enter
memory coupling.
\item[Cage/backjump, \(C^Q,A,h\).] Repeated returns subtract memory from direct
motion.
\item[Ion-atmosphere relaxation, \(A,h\).] Slow charge-density modes subtract
correlated drag.
\item[Electrophoretic drag, \(D_x,A,h\).] Oppositely charged atmosphere changes
mobility and memory.
\item[Debye screening, \(U_x,D_x,A,h\).] Screening changes association,
atmosphere, and drag.
\item[Solvent-shell residence, \(c_i,K_{ij},A,h\).] Residence times shift state
and memory terms.
\item[Ligand-shell residence, \(c_i,K_{ij},A,h\).] Additive ligation changes
populations and memory.
\item[Temperature, \(RT,U_x,D_x,K_{ij},F^2/(RT)\).] Thermal factors, mobility,
rates, and prefactor change.
\item[Dielectric response, \(U_x,D_x,A,h\).] Pairing, atmosphere, and memory
respond to \(\epsilon\).
\item[Salt concentration, \(c_i,U_x,D_x,K_{ij},A,h\).] Loading changes
carriers, nonideality, obstruction, and memory.
\item[Additive microviscosity, \(D_x,K_{ij}\).] Local mobility and transition
capacities respond.
\item[Bulky-ion conductivity increase, \(D_i^{\rm self},M_{ij}\).] Reduced
co-motion drag or larger structural jumps can increase \(\sigma\).
\end{description}

\subsection{Basis refinement without production time integrals}

Let the current memory basis be
\[
\Phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_m).
\]
For a candidate missing coordinate \(g(q)\), compute
\[
A_{gg}=\int\nabla g^\T D_x\nabla g\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\]
\[
A_{g\Phi}=\int\nabla g^\T D_x\nabla\Phi\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\qquad
A_{\Phi\Phi}=\int\nabla\Phi^\T D_x\nabla\Phi\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\]
\[
h_g=\int\nabla g^\T D_xG_P^\T\,\rho_x(\dd q),
\qquad
h_\Phi=\int\nabla\Phi^\T D_xG_P^\T\,\rho_x(\dd q).
\]
Remove the current-basis projection:
\[
\widetilde h_g=h_g-A_{g\Phi}A_{\Phi\Phi}^\#h_\Phi,
\]
\[
\widetilde A_{gg}=A_{gg}-A_{g\Phi}A_{\Phi\Phi}^\#A_{\Phi g}.
\]
The candidate score is
\[
S(g)=\tr\!\left(\widetilde h_g^\T\widetilde A_{gg}^{\#}\widetilde h_g\right).
\]
Add
\[
g^\star=\argmax_g S(g)
\]
when
\[
S(g^\star)>\tau_{\rm basis}.
\]
Stop refinement only when
\[
\max_gS(g)<\tau_{\rm basis}
\qquad
\text{and}
\qquad
|\sigma_{n+1}-\sigma_n|<\tau_\sigma.
\]

\section{Convergence and Correctness}
\label{sec:convergenceproof}

\subsection{Claim boundary}

For one fixed finite reduced basis, finite quadrature rule, finite state set,
and finite memory basis, the algorithm returns one deterministic projected
conductivity:
\[
\sigma_n(x).
\]
That fixed value is the conductivity of the specified projected model. Equality
with the full microscopic Green--Kubo value is a theorem for a refinement
sequence satisfying the assumptions below.

The production recipe path does not evaluate a long-time Green--Kubo integral
or an Einstein--Helfand slope. Green--Kubo and Einstein--Helfand values are
validation targets for projection closure and for recipe-to-primitive closure.

\subsection{Shared-lithium bisection}

For \(l\in[0,L_{\rm tot}]\),
\[
F_{\rm Li}(l)=l+\sum_i m_i\frac{K_i l}{1+K_i l}-L_{\rm tot}.
\]
The derivative is
\[
F'_{\rm Li}(l)
=
1+\sum_i m_i\frac{K_i}{(1+K_i l)^2}
>0.
\]
Thus \(F_{\rm Li}\) is strictly increasing and has at most one root. Also
\[
F_{\rm Li}(0)=-L_{\rm tot}<0,
\]
and
\[
F_{\rm Li}(L_{\rm tot})
=
\sum_i m_i\frac{K_i L_{\rm tot}}{1+K_i L_{\rm tot}}
\ge0.
\]
Therefore a unique root exists in \([0,L_{\rm tot}]\). Bisection halves the
bracket length each iteration, so after \(k\) iterations the bracket width is
\[
\frac{L_{\rm tot}}{2^k}.
\]
The bisection solve stops when the normalized residual is below \(10^{-10}\);
otherwise it raises a non-convergence exception after 100 iterations.

\subsection{Finite generator}

Since \(K=K^\T\), \(K_{ij}\ge0\), and \(c_i>0\),
\[
c_iQ_{ij}=K_{ij}=K_{ji}=c_jQ_{ji}.
\]
Therefore \(Q\) is reversible with respect to \(c\), has zero row sums, and has
\(c^\T Q=0\). The constrained Poisson system is nonsingular on the
zero-\(c\)-mean subspace when the active transition graph is connected. If a
primitive input violates the required generator identities, validation raises an
exception before conductivity is computed.

\subsection{Mori correction}

The memory matrix is a Dirichlet Gram matrix:
\[
A_{\alpha\beta}
=
\langle\nabla\psi_\alpha,\mathcal D\nabla\psi_\beta\rangle_{\rho}.
\]
Hence \(A\succeq0\). The correction \(h^\T A^\#h\) is positive semidefinite
when every current-coupling vector lies in the range of \(A\). The implemented
nullspace test enforces this range condition. Therefore the continuous memory
term subtracts a nonnegative current-memory correction from the direct tensor.

\subsection{Microscopic Green--Kubo and Einstein--Helfand convergence}

Let \(L_x\) be the reversible microscopic generator, \(\mu_x\) its invariant
measure, and \(J_a\) the charge-current component in Cartesian direction
\(a\in\{1,2,3\}\). The exact microscopic conductivity is
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}(x)
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\sum_{a=1}^3
\langle J_a,(-L_x)^\#J_a\rangle_{\mu_x}.
\]
When the stationary current autocorrelation is absolutely integrable,
\[
\int_0^\infty
\left|\langle J_a(t)J_a(0)\rangle_{\mu_x}\right|\,\dd t<\infty,
\]
the same quantity is
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}(x)
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\sum_{a=1}^3
\int_0^\infty
\langle J_a(t)J_a(0)\rangle_{\mu_x}\,\dd t.
\]
For an ergodic trajectory, the finite-window Green--Kubo estimator converges to
the truncated integral as trajectory length grows. The truncated integral
converges to \(\sigma_{\rm GK}\) as the integration window grows. The
Einstein--Helfand estimator converges to the same value when the unwrapped
charge-polarization displacement has a finite diffusive long-time slope.

Every trajectory validation point must report block uncertainty and
window/plateau uncertainty. A \(\pm0.2\,\mathrm{mS\,cm^{-1}}\) model claim
requires the GK/EH reference uncertainty to sit below that scale.

\subsection{Projected-resolvent convergence}

Let \(V_n\) be the span of all state indicators and memory coordinates used at
basis level \(n\), with
\[
V_1\subseteq V_2\subseteq\cdots.
\]
Let \(u_a^\star\) solve
\[
-L_xu_a^\star=J_a
\]
in the mean-zero generator domain. Let \(u_{n,a}\in V_n\) solve the Galerkin
projection:
\[
\langle v,(-L_x)u_{n,a}\rangle_{\mu_x}
=
\langle v,J_a\rangle_{\mu_x}
\qquad
\forall v\in V_n.
\]
Define
\[
\|f\|_E^2=\langle f,(-L_x)f\rangle_{\mu_x}.
\]
The Galerkin identity gives
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}-\sigma_n
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}
\sum_{a=1}^3
\|u_a^\star-u_{n,a}\|_E^2
\ge0,
\]
provided the projected bilinear form uses the exact generator inner products.
If
\[
\overline{\bigcup_n V_n}^{\|\cdot\|_E}
\]
contains every \(u_a^\star\), then
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma_n=\sigma_{\rm GK}.
\]
This is the microscopic projection-convergence theorem. It applies to the
refinement sequence. A single coarse reduced recipe model is one element of
that sequence.

\subsection{Quadrature, committor, and primitive convergence}

The projected formula inherits the Galerkin limit when each numerical primitive
converges to its projected generator integral:
\[
c_i^{(n)}\to c_i,\quad
K_{ij}^{(n)}\to K_{ij},\quad
d_{ij}^{(n)}\to d_{ij},\quad
M_{ij}^{(n)}\to M_{ij},\quad
D_i^{{\rm self},(n)}\to D_i^{\rm self},\quad
A^{(n)}\to A,\quad
h^{(n)}\to h.
\]
The required checks are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item basin quadrature convergence for restricted partition functions and
inner products;
\item committor solver convergence for capacity fluxes;
\item endpoint moment boundary-value convergence for \(d_{ij}\) and \(M_{ij}\);
\item positive semidefinite convergence of \(D_i^{\rm self}\) and \(A\);
\item Mori nullspace range checks for every current-coupling vector;
\item stable scalar conductivity under basis and quadrature refinement.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Macroscopic thermodynamic convergence}

For a sequence of simulation cells with \(V\to\infty\), fixed intensive
composition, stable interactions, finite correlation length, and a well-defined
stationary current density, the intensive conductivity converges:
\[
\sigma_{\rm GK}^{(V)}(x)\to\sigma_{\rm macro}(x).
\]
Finite-size validation must check that the block-averaged GK/EH estimate and
the projected primitive estimate are stable with respect to cell size within the
requested tolerance.

\subsection{Empirical GK comparison}

For a trajectory-labeled formulation, define
\[
\Delta_{\rm GK}(x)
=
\sigma_{\rm projected}(x)-\sigma_{\rm GK,trajectory}(x).
\]
A small \(\Delta_{\rm GK}\) validates the chosen basis, quadrature, and
primitive closure for that trajectory and composition family. Recipe generation
needs a separate primitive residual:
\[
\Delta_{\rm recipe}(x)
=
\sigma_{\rm recipe\ primitives}(x)
-\sigma_{\rm trajectory\ primitives}(x).
\]
A \(\pm0.2\,\mathrm{mS\,cm^{-1}}\) claim requires the held-out residual budget
\[
|\Delta_{\rm GK}|+|\Delta_{\rm recipe}|+\delta_{\rm label}+\delta_{\rm stat}
\leq 0.2\,\mathrm{mS\,cm^{-1}},
\]
with no silent row dropping.

\section{Required Tests and Behavior Checks}

\subsection{Numerical validation classes}

The test suite must cover mass balance, density weights, generator reversibility,
zero-charge conductivity, Poisson correction, Mori null-mode failure,
displacement-scale validation, charge covariance, memory state means, Mori
candidate scoring, primitive sensitivity scores, the baseline electrolyte, labeled
formulation residuals, first-principles projection closure, microscopic
generator projection, trajectory primitive targets, and speciation hard cuts.

\subsection{Baseline numerical check}

For
\[
\text{EC:DMC}=3:7\ \mathrm{v/v},
\qquad
\text{LiPF6}=1.0\,\molL,
\qquad
T=298.15\ \mathrm K,
\]
the validation range is
\[
8.0 \le \sigma_{\mScm}\le 10.0.
\]
Leaving this range flags a model or implementation bug unless the primitive
inputs document a physically different electrolyte condition. The check also
evaluates the reduced populations:
\[
c \approx
(624.77933057,\ 624.77933053,\ 375.22066947)\ \molm,
\]
so that
\[
c_{\rm Li,free}+c_{\rm pair}\approx1000,
\qquad
c_{\rm anion,free}+c_{\rm pair}\approx1000.
\]

\subsection{A. Current-correlation and readout tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Zero charge-polarization gradient gives \(\sigma=0\).
\item With no transitions and no memory, conductivity reduces to the
Nernst-Einstein readout
\[
\sigma_{\rm NE}
=
\frac{F^2}{3RT}\tr\left(\sum_i c_iD_i^{\rm self}\right).
\]
\item With symmetric transition samples
\(\{\pm \ell e_x,\pm \ell e_y,\pm \ell e_z\}\), the first moment is zero and
\[
M_{ij}=\frac{\ell^2}{3}I_3.
\]
\item With nonzero first transition moments \(d_{ij}\), the finite-state Poisson
correction \(C^Q\) must be nonzero and subtract from the direct term.
\item A nonzero memory coupling \(h\) must produce
\[
C^\psi=h^{\T}A^\#h\succeq0.
\]
\item A current-coupling vector outside the range of \(A\) must fail the
nullspace check.
\item The final tensor must be symmetric positive semidefinite after removing
only roundoff-scale negative eigenvalues.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{B. Speciation and population tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Increasing association strength shifts mass from free lithium and free
anion states into associated-pair states while preserving total lithium and
total anion loading.
\item Mixed salts share one lithium pool:
\[
C_{\rm Li}=\sum_i C_i^{\rm salt}.
\]
Adding a second anion changes all free and paired populations through the same
free-lithium solve.
\item Neutral additives can create ligand-conditioned states but do not create
charged carriers unless they are ionic constituents.
\item Pure motif exchange with no net charge-polarization displacement has
zero direct charge jump.
\item Descriptor changes must affect populations through \(U_x\) or mass-action
terms, not through species-name branches.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{C. State-local mobility and obstruction tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Higher viscosity lowers lithium and anion diffusivities at fixed radius,
temperature, and obstruction.
\item Larger hydrodynamic radius lowers Stokes-Einstein diffusivity.
\item Finite occupied volume increases resistance through
\((1/\varphi_{\rm free})^{p_V}\).
\item Larger local charge-density ratio increases charge-cloud resistance.
\item Additive solvation support can reduce obstruction when donor support rises,
even if viscosity also rises.
\item Associated neutral-pair self-current remains zero unless internal charge
covariance is explicitly represented.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{D. Solvent and additive descriptor tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Solvent identity affects conductivity only through density, viscosity,
dielectric response, donor/acceptor numbers, molecular volume, polarizability,
coordination affinity, and packing descriptors.
\item The EC:linear-carbonate ratio must affect viscosity, dielectric constant,
donor environment, hard-sphere volume fraction, mean molecular volume, and
coordination support.
\item A neutral film-forming additive must be able to increase viscosity while
also increasing lithium coordination support.
\item Bulky neutral additives must change packing/free volume and solvation
support, not just a scalar viscosity term.
\item A generated basin label must be descriptor-based. Species-name physics
after descriptor parsing fails the test.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{E. Ion-atmosphere and electrostatic memory tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Dielectric decrement changes association, Debye screening,
charge-cloud resistance, and memory coupling.
\item Ion-atmosphere relaxation must appear in \(A,h\), not as one terminal
scalar factor.
\item The model must distinguish diagonal lithium drag, diagonal anion drag, and
cross relaxation.
\item Increasing charge-cloud radius can reduce relaxation drag while also
changing association; the two channels must be separately visible.
\item Electrophoretic drag and relaxation drag must be independently ablatable
at the primitive level.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{F. Multi-center transport tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item For a motif with charge centers \(a,b\), the self-current tensor uses
\[
\sum_{a,b}z_az_bD_{ab},
\]
not \(z_{\rm net}^2D_{\rm COM}\).
\item Li-anion co-motion and anti-correlation must change the sign and magnitude
of cross covariance.
\item Solvent-separated ion pairs can conduct through relative motion even when
contact ion pairs suppress charge motion.
\item Multidentate anions can create bridge/network populations that change
both \(M_{ij}\) and \(A,h\).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{G. Cage, backjump, and structural-memory tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Partner switching must change \(K_{ij}\), \(d_{ij}\), \(M_{ij}\), and
memory coordinates.
\item Cage backjump produces a negative correction through \(C^Q\) or \(A,h\).
\item Ligand-shell residence changes memory even at fixed Li-anion distance.
\item Anion orientation memory changes the relaxation matrix and current
coupling when orientation bins are perturbed.
\item Local free-volume memory changes obstruction and slow relaxation together.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{H. Concentration and high-loading tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Increasing salt concentration changes carrier count, activity, finite
volume, viscosity, atmosphere resistance, and mobility.
\item High salt cannot leave conductivity flat unless the primitive ledger
shows compensating effects.
\item Free volume must remain positive:
\[
\varphi_{\rm free}>0.
\]
\item The model must fail when occupied ionic volume reaches or exceeds the
available volume.
\item High-loading aggregates must be represented as population and mobility
changes, not hidden in a scalar correction.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{I. Solvent-matrix tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Changing solvent dielectric at fixed viscosity changes association and
atmosphere memory.
\item Changing viscosity at fixed dielectric changes diffusivities and capacity
fluxes.
\item Changing donor number at fixed viscosity and dielectric changes solvation
support and ligand-conditioned populations.
\item Changing molecular volume at fixed donor number changes packing and
free-volume resistance.
\item Temperature changes \(RT\), \(k_BT\), Stokes-Einstein diffusivity,
association, rates, viscosity when supplied, and the conductivity prefactor.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{J. Label, trajectory, and projection-gap tests}

Every validation point must separate
\[
\Delta_{\rm label},\qquad
\Delta_{\rm traj},\qquad
\Delta_{\rm projection},\qquad
\Delta_{\rm primitive}.
\]
The required audit reports:
\[
D_Q^{\rm target},\quad
D_Q^{\rm predicted},\quad
D_{\rm vehicular},\quad
D_{\rm jump},\quad
C^Q,\quad
C^\psi,
\]
motif populations, lifetimes, top state contributions, top edge contributions,
and the primitive family with the largest residual sensitivity.

\subsection{K. Descriptor completeness tests}

The model must fail before conductivity is produced when a required descriptor
family is missing. Required descriptor families are:
\begin{itemize}
\item identity: role, charge, molecular weight, density;
\item size and shape: hard-sphere radius, hydrodynamic radius, cavity radius,
molecular volume, accessible area, ligand-field asymmetry;
\item electrostatics: charge-cloud radius, dipole, quadrupole, polarizability,
pure dielectric, Born radius;
\item coordination: donor number, acceptor number, hydrogen-bond counts,
coordination sites, coordination affinity;
\item transport: pure viscosity, mixture viscosity, temperature response.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{L. Interaction tests}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Neutral ligand loading with bulky anions must be able to increase
Li-ligand and solvent-separated populations and change conductivity despite a
viscosity increase.
\item The same additive loading with compact and bulky anions must produce
different populations and covariances because radius, charge-cloud size,
denticity, and ligand-field asymmetry differ.
\item High lithium salt in a carbonate matrix must identify whether free Li
translation, free anion translation, atmosphere drag, association, or cage
memory owns the residual.
\item Mixed anions must share the Li pool without duplicating lithium carriers.
\item Additive viscosity and additive coordination must appear as distinct
primitive responses.
\item Solvent-shell identity at fixed Li-anion distance must change mobility,
generator rates, and memory when descriptors differ.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Primitive validity gates}

A numerical realization must fail if any of the following identities is violated:
\[
c_i\le0,\qquad
K_{ij}<0,\qquad
K_{ij}\ne K_{ji},
\]
\[
\sum_jQ_{ij}\ne0,\qquad
c_iQ_{ij}\ne c_jQ_{ji},
\]
\[
d_{ji}\ne-d_{ij},\qquad
M_{ji}\ne M_{ij},
\]
\[
D_i^{\rm self}\not\succeq0,\qquad
A\not\succeq0,
\]
\[
\|\Delta P_{ij,\ell}\|>L_{\max}.
\]

\subsection{Behavior coverage table}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.25em,style=nextline]
\item[Input unit contract, \(\phi_s,m_i,w_a\).] Solvents normalize to one;
salts remain mol L\(^{-1}\); additive percent is converted to weight fraction
before numerical evaluation.
\item[Missing required fields, input validation.] Missing solvent data, missing
salt data, nonpositive solvent entries, negative additives, and no ionic
constituents raise exceptions.
\item[Missing species descriptors, species lookup.] Every required liquid and
ionic descriptor is present before conductivity is returned.
\item[Free-ion fraction, \(c_i\).] Increasing association strength shifts
population from free states into associated-pair states while preserving
conserved lithium and anion totals.
\item[Mixed salts, \(c_i,K_i\).] Multiple anions share one lithium pool through
the scalar free-lithium solve.
\item[Ionic additive conversion, \(m_i\).] An ionic additive loading changes
ionic constituent molarity through \(m=w\rho_{\rm liq}1000/M\).
\item[Nonionic additive solvation, \(\rho_{\rm liq},\eta_{\rm liq},DN_{\rm liq},S_{\rm add}\).]
A nonionic additive changes liquid properties and additive-solvation support.
\item[Viscosity penalty, \(\eta_{\rm cP},D_{\rm Li},D^-_i,D^{\rm self}\).]
Higher liquid or salt-corrected viscosity lowers Stokes-Einstein diffusivities
at fixed other inputs.
\item[Dielectric decrement, \(\epsilon,K_i,S_D,A,h\).] Salt dielectric
decrement changes association, atmosphere resistance, and memory coupling
through \(\epsilon\).
\item[Finite occupied volume, \(\varphi_{\rm ion},S_D\).] High ionic loading
increases obstruction and fails if occupied volume reaches one.
\item[Zero charge, \(\nabla P,D^{\rm self},M,h\).] Zero charge-polarization
gradient returns zero conductivity.
\item[Free carrier Nernst-Einstein limit, \(\sum_i c_iD_i^{\rm self}\).]
Conductivity equals \(F^2\tr(\sum c_iD_i)/(3RT)\) when transitions and memory
are absent.
\item[Associated neutral pair, \(D^{\rm self}_{\rm pair}\).] Associated-pair
self-current is zero in the reduced composition model.
\item[Transition displacement, \(M_{ij}\).] Symmetric
\(\pm x,\pm y,\pm z\) transition moments give \(d_{ij}=0\) and
\(M_{ij}=(\ell_i^2/3)I_3\).
\item[Poisson memory correction, \(C_Q\).] A primitive input with nonzero
\(d_{ij}\) subtracts the weighted Poisson correction.
\item[Mori memory correction, \(A,h,C_M\).] Nonzero current-memory coupling
subtracts \(h^\T A^\#h\). A null memory mode with nonzero current coupling
raises an exception.
\item[Positive semidefinite final tensor, \(D_{\rm proj}\).] Roundoff-scale
negative eigenvalues may be projected to zero; physical negative eigenvalues
fail loudly.
\item[Temperature dependence, \(RT,k_BT,D_{\rm SE}\).] Changing \(T\) changes
Stokes-Einstein diffusivity, ion-pair association, and the conductivity
prefactor.
\item[Property-database coverage, end-to-end composition path.] All labeled
rows are evaluated, residual metrics are reported, and failed rows are reported
explicitly.
\item[Primitive attribution, \(c,K,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h\).] Finite-difference
sensitivity scores identify which primitive family changes under a recipe
perturbation.
\item[Projection closure, trajectory versus projection.] First-principles tests
compare projected conductivity against Green-Kubo and Einstein-Helfand
estimates within configured tolerance.
\end{description}

\subsection{Minimum pass/fail checklist}

A conductivity behavior test must fail if any of the following occurs:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Conductivity is returned with missing required species descriptors.
\item Solvent ratios, salt molarities, or additive weight percent are interpreted
in the wrong units.
\item Free-ion and paired populations violate conserved lithium or anion mass.
\item A neutral associated-pair self-current contributes as a charged free ion.
\item Viscosity or dielectric changes bypass the primitive tensors and appear
only as a terminal scalar multiplier.
\item The Mori nullspace range condition is violated without an exception.
\item The final projected tensor has a physically negative eigenvalue.
\item The EC:DMC 3:7 plus 1 M LiPF6 baseline leaves the 8--10 \(\mScm\) range
without a primitive-level explanation.
\item The property-database validator drops labeled rows silently.
\item A residual or recipe perturbation cannot be attributed to one of
\(c,K,d,M,D^{\rm self},A,h\).
\end{enumerate}
