{\LARGE\sffamily\bfseries\color{lithioxfontblue} First-Principles Molecular Conductivity}

{\large\sffamily\color{lithioxfontgreen} Recipe and nuclear species data to the exact charge-response operator}
\vspace{5pt}

The complete physical model is the nonrelativistic Coulomb theory of the electrons and nuclei in the electrolyte. A recipe fixes how many nuclei of each species are present. Temperature and density or pressure fix the thermodynamic state. The Hamiltonian generates the equilibrium state, molecular structure, charge flux, and conductivity. Solvation, ion pairing, aggregation, polarization, packing, and correlated charge motion are consequences of this one Hamiltonian rather than separately configured transport models.

\section{Inputs and the complete mapping}

The physical calculation requires four inputs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item an electrolyte recipe giving molecular identities and loadings;
\item species records giving each molecule's nuclei, isotope masses, nuclear charges, and stoichiometry;
\item temperature \(T\);
\item either cell volume or mass density, or an external pressure defining an isothermal--isobaric state.
\end{enumerate}
The physical constants \(e\), \(m_e\), \(\hbar\), \(k_B\), and \(\varepsilon_0\) are universal. No viscosity, partial-charge model, force field, association constant, ion-pair population, diffusivity, cage rate, or conductivity coefficient is an input to the exact theory.

Finite cell size, electronic basis size, reciprocal-space resolution, resolvent broadening, and sampling duration control numerical approximation. They are taken to limits and do not define new electrolyte physics.

The complete map is
\[
\boxed{
(\text{recipe},\text{nuclei},T,V\text{ or }p)
\longmapsto H\longmapsto(\rho,\mathcal L,\mathcal J)
\longmapsto\sigma_{\rm dc}.}
\]

\section{Recipe to nuclei and thermodynamic state}

A species record lists the nuclear charge \(Z_Ie\), isotope mass \(M_I\), and number of each nucleus in one molecule. The recipe is converted into integer molecular counts \(N_s\) that reproduce its solvent ratios, salt concentrations, and additive fractions while satisfying exact nuclear stoichiometry and electrical neutrality. Increasing the cell scale improves this rational approximation without changing intensive composition.

At fixed volume, the state is \((T,h,\{N_s\})\), where the cell matrix \(h\) has volume \(V=\det h\). At fixed pressure, the state is \((T,p,\{N_s\})\), and volume is integrated over the isothermal--isobaric partition function. These quantities remove the apparent ambiguity in molecule counts: a recipe defines ratios and concentrations, while \(V\) or \(p\) fixes the amount of matter represented by one finite cell.

After the nuclei have been enumerated, molecular names no longer enter the physical equations. Chemical bonding and molecular identity emerge from the nuclear charges, masses, quantum statistics, and Coulomb interactions. Molecular graph data may be used to construct an initial basis or initial nuclear arrangement, but it does not alter the Hamiltonian.

\section{Periodic Coulomb theory}

Let
\[
\Lambda_h=\mathbb R^3/h\mathbb Z^3,\qquad V=\det h.
\]
The zero-average periodic Coulomb Green function is defined by
\[
-\nabla^2G_h(x)=4\pi\left[\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^3}\delta(x-hn)-V^{-1}\right],
\qquad \int_{\Lambda_h}G_h(x)\dd x=0.
\]
This definition fixes the real-space, reciprocal-space, self, surface, and zero-wavevector terms through one periodic boundary-value problem. The finite cell is neutral, so no artificial neutralizing background is required.

Index all electrons and nuclei by \(\alpha=1,\ldots,N_{\rm p}\), with
\[
(q_\alpha,m_\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
(-e,m_e),&\alpha\text{ electron},\\
(Z_Ie,M_I),&\alpha\text{ nucleus }I.
\end{cases}
\]
The many-particle Hilbert space is antisymmetric under exchange of electrons and has the correct bosonic or fermionic symmetry for each nuclear isotope.

\section{Exact electron--nuclear Hamiltonian}

A uniform vector potential \(A\) is introduced as a boundary twist. The Hamiltonian is
\[
\boxed{
\widehat H_h(A)=
\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\rm p}}
\frac{[-i\hbar\nabla_\alpha-q_\alpha A]^2}{2m_\alpha}
+\widehat V_{C,h}+\widehat H_{\rm corr}.}
\]
The periodic Coulomb operator is
\[
\widehat V_{C,h}=
\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}
\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}q_\alpha q_\gamma
G_h(\widehat x_\alpha-\widehat x_\gamma)
+E_{\rm self}(h,\{q_\alpha\}).
\]
The optional correction operator declares relativistic, spin--orbit, finite-nuclear-size, or other chosen corrections. Setting \(\widehat H_{\rm corr}=0\) defines the nonrelativistic Coulomb model exactly.

This Hamiltonian contains electron--electron, electron--nuclear, and nuclear--nuclear interactions. Bonding, charge transfer, electronic polarization, solvation, association, dissociation, packing, and correlated ion motion follow from its eigenstates and dynamics. They are not separate closures.

\section{Microscopic generator and equilibrium state}

The observable Liouvillian is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_h\widehat B=\frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat H_h(0),\widehat B]},
\]
so \(\widehat B(t)=e^{t\mathcal L_h}\widehat B\). The adjoint generator acting on density operators is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_h^*\widehat\rho=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat H_h(0),\widehat\rho]}.
\]
At fixed particle numbers, volume, and temperature, equilibrium is
\[
\boxed{
\widehat\rho_{\beta,h}=
\frac{e^{-\beta\widehat H_h(0)}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta\widehat H_h(0)}},
\qquad \beta=(k_BT)^{-1}.}
\]
The driven generator uses \(E(t)=-\dot A(t)\):
\[
\mathcal L_{h,A(t)}^*\widehat\rho
=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat H_h(A(t)),\widehat\rho].
\]
Conductivity is the derivative of the induced charge flux with respect to this field at zero field.

\section{Hamiltonian-generated charge flux}

The cell-integrated charge flux is generated by the same vector potential:
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,a}(A)
=-\frac{\partial\widehat H_h(A)}{\partial A_a}.}
\]
At zero field,
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,a}
=\sum_\alpha\frac{q_\alpha}{m_\alpha}\widehat p_{\alpha,a},
\qquad \widehat j_{h,a}=V^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,a}.}
\]
The contact operator follows from the second derivative,
\[
\widehat{\mathcal K}_{h,ab}
=\left.\frac{\partial^2\widehat H_h(A)}{\partial A_a\partial A_b}\right|_{A=0}
=\delta_{ab}\sum_\alpha\frac{q_\alpha^2}{m_\alpha}.
\]
This term is retained in the full frequency-dependent response. For regular dissipative DC conductivity, let \(\Pi_0\) project onto conserved or ballistic zero-frequency current modes and define
\[
\widehat{\mathcal J}_a^\perp=(1-\Pi_0)\widehat{\mathcal J}_a.
\]

\section{Exact Kubo response}

The Kubo--Mori inner product is
\[
(\widehat A,\widehat B)_{\beta,h}
=\frac1\beta\int_0^\beta
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\widehat\rho_{\beta,h}e^{\lambda\widehat H_h}
\widehat A^\dagger e^{-\lambda\widehat H_h}\widehat B
\right]\dd\lambda.
\]
For broadening \(\eta>0\), solve
\[
\boxed{(\eta-\mathcal L_h)\widehat U_{h,b}^{(\eta)}
=\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,b}^\perp.}
\]
The regular conductivity tensor is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{h,ab}^{(\eta)}
=\frac{\beta}{V}\operatorname{Re}
(\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,a}^\perp,
\widehat U_{h,b}^{(\eta)})_{\beta,h}.}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\sigma_{h,ab}^{(\eta)}
=\frac{\beta}{V}\int_0^\infty e^{-\eta t}
\operatorname{Re}
(\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,a}^\perp,
e^{t\mathcal L_h}\widehat{\mathcal J}_{h,b}^\perp)_{\beta,h}\dd t.
\]
The isotropic macroscopic DC value is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm dc}=\frac13\tr\left[
\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\lim_{V\to\infty}
\boldsymbol\sigma_h^{(\eta)}\right].}
\]
The thermodynamic limit precedes the zero-frequency limit because a finite isolated quantum cell has a discrete spectrum.

\section{Finite numerical Liouvillian}

Choose a systematically complete many-particle basis \(\{|\Phi_\mu^{(K)}\rangle\}_{\mu=1}^{D_K}\). Its Hamiltonian and current matrices are
\[
H_{\mu\nu}^{(K)}(A)=\langle\Phi_\mu^{(K)}|\widehat H_h(A)|\Phi_\nu^{(K)}\rangle,
\quad
\mathcal J_{a,\mu\nu}^{(K)}=-\left.\partial_{A_a}H_{\mu\nu}^{(K)}(A)\right|_0.
\]
On the operator basis \(|\Phi_\mu\rangle\langle\Phi_\nu|\), the numerical generator is
\[
\boxed{
\mathbf L_K=\frac{i}{\hbar}
\left(I\otimes H_K-H_K^{\mathsf T}\otimes I\right).}
\]
The equilibrium matrix and resolvent system are
\[
\mathbf R_{\beta,K}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_K}}{\tr e^{-\beta H_K}},
\qquad
(\eta I-\mathbf L_K)\mathbf u_b=\mathbf j_b^\perp.
\]
The finite conductivity is the Kubo--Mori weighted bilinear form between \(\mathbf j_a^\perp\) and \(\mathbf u_b\). This is an explicit linear algebra problem once the basis matrix elements have been evaluated.

\subsection{Spectral form}

For \(H_K|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle\) and \(p_n=e^{-\beta E_n}/\sum_re^{-\beta E_r}\), define
\[
w_{mn}=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{p_m-p_n}{\beta(E_n-E_m)},&E_m\ne E_n,\\[2mm]
p_m,&E_m=E_n.
\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{ab}^{(K,h,\eta)}
=\frac{\beta}{V}\operatorname{Re}\sum_{m,n}
w_{mn}\frac{(\mathcal J_a^\perp)_{mn}^*(\mathcal J_b^\perp)_{mn}}
{\eta-i(E_m-E_n)/\hbar}.}
\]
The exact result is the ordered limit
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{\rm dc}=\frac13\tr
\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}\lim_{V\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}
\boldsymbol\sigma^{(K,h,\eta)}.}
\]
Basis completion, thermodynamic size, and zero-frequency response are separate numerical limits of the same Hamiltonian.

\section{Born--Oppenheimer approximation}

The exact operator system is closed but exponentially expensive. The standard first-principles molecular approximation solves the electronic problem at fixed nuclear positions:
\[
\widehat H_{e,h}(R)\Psi_0(R)=E_0(R)\Psi_0(R),
\qquad U_{\rm BO,h}(R)=E_0(R)+V_{nn,h}(R).
\]
The classical nuclear Hamiltonian is
\[
H_{\rm BO,h}(R,P)=\sum_I\frac{|P_I|^2}{2M_I}+U_{\rm BO,h}(R),
\]
with phase-space generator
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G_{\rm BO,h}f
=\sum_I\frac{P_I}{M_I}\cdot\nabla_{R_I}f
-\sum_I\nabla_{R_I}U_{\rm BO,h}\cdot\nabla_{P_I}f.}
\]
The canonical measure \(Z^{-1}e^{-\beta H_{\rm BO,h}}\dd R\dd P\) is invariant under this Hamiltonian flow. Canonical initial conditions may be prepared by an auxiliary sampler, but production dynamics use \(\mathcal G_{\rm BO,h}\). A thermostat used during production defines a different transport generator.

\section{Periodic BO charge flux}

Periodic electronic polarization is defined by a Berry-phase many-body polarization \(\mu_{\rm BO}(R)\). Its time derivative is the adiabatic charge flux:
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_a^{\rm BO}(R,P)
=\sum_{I,b}\frac{\partial\mu_{{\rm BO},a}}{\partial R_{I,b}}
\frac{P_{I,b}}{M_I}
=\sum_{I,b}Z_{I,ab}^*(R)V_{I,b}.}
\]
Born effective charges \(Z_I^*\) therefore come from the electronic polarization derivative. Fixed molecular partial charges are not the fundamental current. Integer oxidation-number currents are equivalent only when the liquid remains electronically insulating and their polarization differs from the Berry-phase polarization by a bounded quantity.

The BO conductivity is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{ab}^{\rm BO}=\frac{\beta}{V}\int_0^\infty
\langle\mathcal J_a^{\rm BO}(t)\mathcal J_b^{\rm BO}(0)\rangle\dd t.}
\]
Its Einstein--Helfand form is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma^{\rm BO}=\frac{\beta}{6V}\lim_{t\to\infty}
\frac{\langle|\mu_{\rm BO}(t)-\mu_{\rm BO}(0)|^2\rangle}{t}.}
\]

\section{What is proved and what must be computed}

The quantum equations above close the mapping from recipe, nuclei, temperature, and thermodynamic state to conductivity. For each finite \((K,V,\eta)\), they define a concrete matrix calculation. Under convergence of the many-particle basis and existence of the thermodynamic and regular DC limits, the ordered sequence converges to the conductivity of the declared nonrelativistic Coulomb theory.

The BO equations define a controlled physical approximation to the electron--nuclear theory. Their numerical result must converge with electronic basis and self-consistency tolerance, nuclear timestep, cell size, trajectory duration, and independent initial conditions while the BO Hamiltonian and charge-flux definition remain fixed.

Neither operator convergence nor the BO approximation proves agreement with an experiment. Experimental agreement tests the adequacy of the nonrelativistic corrections, BO separation, electronic-structure approximation, finite-temperature nuclear treatment, and achieved numerical limits. A predictive claim requires calculated conductivity values and uncertainties for held-out recipes with fully specified temperature and thermodynamic state.

\section{Final model statement}

The fundamental generator is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L_h\widehat B=\frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat H_h,\widehat B]},
\]
the microscopic flux is
\[
\boxed{\widehat{\mathcal J}_a=-\left.\partial_{A_a}\widehat H_h(A)\right|_{A=0}},
\]
and the conductivity is
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{ab}^{(\eta)}=\frac{\beta}{V}\operatorname{Re}
(\mathcal J_a^\perp,(\eta-\mathcal L_h)^{-1}\mathcal J_b^\perp)_{\beta,h}.}
\]
The practical BO generator and Berry-phase current are explicit approximations to these objects. No Smoluchowski mobility, viscosity closure, configured association population, or species-level conductivity term is part of this first-principles mapping.
